# Power Endurance: Short End First, Then Long End

**Type:** Principles · **Trains:** Power endurance

**TL;DR:** Power endurance lets you finish long, all-out boulders and routes, keep climbing when pumped and do a crux when already tired. It relies on both the aerobic and the anaerobic energy systems, so no single exercise trains it, and getting pumped a lot is not how to build it. Train the short end first (12–15 moves, anaerobic capacity, at least 18 weeks to adapt), then the long end (30+ moves, the very high-intensity end of the aerobic system, 6–8 weeks to adapt).

## What power endurance gives you
- With power endurance you can:
  - complete long boulder problems or medium/longer routes that are all-out and at your max
  - keep climbing when you are really pumped
  - do the crux of a route even though you are already a bit tired
- **Warning:** Power endurance is often misunderstood and not trained correctly, so it stays many people's weak area. The common belief "if I just get pumped loads, that'll get me fit" is wrong.

## Why it is complicated
- Power endurance relies on both the aerobic and the anaerobic energy systems. In this high-intensity zone, when you pull off a number of hard moves in a row, neither system works exclusively; both always contribute.
- No single climbing exercise sorts power endurance out; think carefully about your aims. To keep climbing hard, avoid the "fade" or climb when pumped, train the different aspects of power endurance (and of endurance).

## How to progress
| Order | Stage | Length | Energy system | Adaptation time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Short end power endurance (anaerobic capacity) | 12–15 moves | Anaerobic | At least 18 weeks |
| 2 | Long end power endurance | 30+ moves | Very high-intensity end of the aerobic system | 6–8 weeks |

- The workouts for each stage are in the note Power Endurance Training: Short End and Long End.
