# Power Endurance: What It Is and How Long to Train It

**Type:** Principles, Planning · **Trains:** Power endurance

**TL;DR:** Power endurance combines peak anaerobic output with sustained aerobic capacity: the ability to prevail through multiple intermittent bursts of high power output, as on sport routes dotted with crux zones or repeated attempts on a limit boulder. It is built with sport-specific interval training on the wall, which improves buffering, lactate shuttling and mitochondrial efficiency. Limit it to two or three sessions a week, for two to four weeks before a period of performance climbing: longer commonly leads to overtraining. Long-term gains come from raising maximum strength and aerobic power.

## What power-endurance is
- Power training targets peak anaerobic output; endurance training targets sustained aerobic capacity; power-endurance combines the two: the ability to prevail through multiple intermittent bursts of high power output.
- This is the fitness needed for hard sport routes dotted with crux zones, or for repeated maximum efforts on a limit boulder problem.
- Interval training is the proven method, but it must be sport-specific: climbers need to do their intervals on the wall.
- Effective interval training must be properly structured, appropriately dosed and executed with discipline to get the optimal training stimulus.
- Doing one or two interval protocols a couple of times per week (five are in the note Power-Endurance Training Protocols) increases the capacity to do consecutive hard moves, resist fatigue and endure the pump to the boulder top or the chains.

## Why it works
- Done right, the body adapts in ways that speed recovery between efforts and increase the capacity for repeated bursts of high power output.
- The main adaptations are improved intra- and extracellular buffering of H+ ions (acidosis), improved lactate shuttling, increased mitochondrial efficiency, and numerous enzymatic changes.

## Programming
- Power-endurance training is hard on the body: limit it to two or three sessions per week.
- The anaerobic lactic system is the least trainable of the three energy systems. Two to four weeks of power-endurance training tops off your tank ahead of a period of performance climbing (taper appropriately).
- **Warning:** Training with power-endurance protocols for longer than that commonly leads to overtraining syndrome, with reduced high-end performance and higher risk of injury or illness.
- Long-term gains in power-endurance come from raising alactic (maximum strength) power and aerobic (critical) power. Frequent periods of polarized maximum strength and aerobic capacity training are what develop elite-level power-endurance over successive seasons.
