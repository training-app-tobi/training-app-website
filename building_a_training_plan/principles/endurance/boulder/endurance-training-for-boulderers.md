# Endurance Training for Boulderers

**Type:** Principles, Session · **Trains:** Endurance, Recovery · **For:** boulderers

**TL;DR:** A foundation of recovery training (really low-end endurance training) improves recovery in hard, intense efforts, so you can complete more bouldering sessions in a training block, and that adds up to higher overall performance. Use two kinds of session: regeneration (very mild blood flow, no pump at all) and intervals on and off the wall that stay below the pump. Avoid spending much time getting really pumped and fatigued.

## Why boulderers should do endurance training
- A foundation of recovery training (really low-end endurance training) improves recovery rates: recovery is better in hard, intense efforts, whether hard boulders, power endurance sessions or endurance sessions themselves.
- Focusing on recovery rate and on how much training you can complete in a block of a couple of weeks or months makes the bigger difference in the end: bouldering more frequently and completing more sessions adds up to higher overall performance.

## What to expect
| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| At first | Performance at your maximum bouldering grade may drop a little because you feel slightly fatigued |
| After the initial dip | Bouldering performance improves consistently and goes beyond the previous level, instead of hitting a plateau at the same level with intermittent spikes of performance |

## Avoid the pump zone
- **Warning:** Be careful not to spend much time in the zone of getting really pumped and fatigued in endurance training, especially if you come from a route-climbing background. When specialising in bouldering, this is a detriment to overall performance and doesn't work well for boulderers.

## Sessions that work for boulderers
| Session | How |
|---|---|
| Regeneration | Very low-end, continuity-style work: very mild blood flow, no pump at all, aimed at the recovery rate |
| Interval-style training | On and off the wall. Time on the wall is a little harder, but you still don't get pumped; time off the wall is forced recovery that clears the waste products built up while on the wall. It doesn't mean doing 4–5 minutes at high intensity |

- The regeneration approach is backed by research on strength athletes.
- Done consistently, this type of training pays off; on rare occasions people feel it doesn't work for them.
