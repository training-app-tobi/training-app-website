# Aerobic Versus Anaerobic Training for Climbing

**Type:** Principles · **Trains:** Endurance, Power endurance

**TL;DR:** Aerobic training builds stamina (low to moderate intensity for a long time); anaerobic training builds muscular endurance (high-intensity bouts with brief breaks). Most climbers benefit from both, so include exercises for both energy systems, and build them with climbing-specific methods on the wall: the aerobic workouts are in the note Aerobic Training on the Wall, the anaerobic ones in the note Anaerobic Interval Training.

## Stamina and muscular endurance

| | Stamina | Muscular endurance |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | The ability to maintain a low to moderate level of intensity for an extended period of time | The capacity for bouts of high-intensity activity with brief breaks in between |
| Example | A long route with a fairly consistent level of difficulty throughout; the challenge comes from the length, not from cruxes at your limit | Repeated attempts on a hard boulder, a route punctuated by cruxes and rests, or a consistent climb that demands maximum effort from start to finish |
| Energy system that trains it | Aerobic | Anaerobic |

- Most climbers benefit from a combination of both stamina and muscular endurance, whether they boulder short climbs or do multi-pitch on tall walls: whatever your favored terrain, at some point you will find yourself out of your element.
- Include exercises for both energy systems in your training routine.
- Running or cycling a couple of times a week helps aerobic fitness, but both aerobic and anaerobic fitness can also be built with climbing-specific methods on the wall; the best way to train for climbing is climbing.
