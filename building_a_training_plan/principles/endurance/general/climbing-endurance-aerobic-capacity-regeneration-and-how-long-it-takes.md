# Climbing Endurance: Aerobic Capacity, Regeneration and How Long It Takes

**Type:** Principles · **Trains:** Endurance, Recovery · **For:** Route climbers, Boulderers

**TL;DR:** Endurance is the ability to climb constantly at a low-to-moderate level for over 1 minute; it drives route grade, recovery on holds and how fast you get pumped. Aerobic capacity training changes the muscle's structures and metabolism, while regeneration is closer to recovery and suits rest days or the end of long sessions. Both help boulderers and route climbers. Build aerobic capacity before power endurance, and expect measurable gains within 4–6 weeks, with aerobic adaptations compounding over months and years.

## What climbing endurance is
- Endurance is the ability to climb constantly at a low-to-moderate level for durations over 1 minute, on moves a long way away from your maximum bouldering level. It is highly correlated with route climbing ability (the best route climbers have a good level of endurance), it is highly related to your ability to recover on holds and in rests, and it affects how quickly you get pumped on a route.
- Endurance training is a localised, aerobic, low-intensity form of training.

| Category | What it is |
|---|---|
| Aerobic capacity | A more intense type of endurance training that creates changes in the physical structures and metabolic processes in your muscle |
| Regeneration | Closer to recovery activity; ideal for rest days or the end of long sessions |

## Why train endurance
- Training your muscles to improve their aerobic energy production gives obvious results in your grade: you can go for longer, climb harder grades without getting pumped, and it improves your power endurance further down the line.
- Regeneration training improves recovery rates and increases the amount of climbing training you can handle over the years. It is very important for both boulderers and route climbers.
- Endurance training is very beneficial to both boulderers and route climbers: making the forearm muscles work aerobically gives long-term physical, technical and psychological benefits to all climbers. For boulderers, an aerobic base improves recovery between attempts and increases the total volume of training they can handle.
- Getting the amount right for bouldering vs route climbing matters because the two specialisations have different physiological demands.

## Training vs performance
- Training and performance are not the same: training in short blocks (e.g. 1 minute), done correctly, lets you perform on blocks significantly longer than 1 minute.

## Aerobic capacity vs power endurance
| | Aerobic capacity | Power endurance |
|---|---|---|
| Intensity | Low, long duration | Higher, closer to your limit |
| Builds | Your aerobic base (the foundation) | Your ability to sustain near-maximal effort |
| Order in the training cycle | First | After aerobic capacity |

## How long it takes
- Most climbers notice measurable improvement within 4–6 weeks of consistent endurance training.
- Aerobic adaptations accumulate gradually, so don't skip regeneration work: it compounds over months and years.
