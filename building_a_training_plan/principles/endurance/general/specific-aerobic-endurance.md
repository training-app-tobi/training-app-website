# Specific Aerobic Endurance: Train Utilization, Not the Pump

**Type:** Principles · **Trains:** Endurance · **For:** pumps out on long routes

**TL;DR:** Specific aerobic endurance is the ability to do lots and lots of climbing at a low intensity, and only climbing builds it: general cardio doesn't transfer. Most climbers are limited by utilization (using the energy in the muscles to do hard moves), not by breathing or oxygen delivery. Power endurance is the cable between an aerobic building and a strength building, so improve it by making both bigger. Train in the aerobic zone (easy breathing, no pump, something left in the tank) and don't seek fatigue: volume is the key. Aerobic fitness is slow to build but persistent.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Tire during a climbing day or pump out on long routes | Train aerobic endurance: it may be one of the solutions, and it makes climbing feel easier and more pleasant |

## Endurance is specific
- Specific aerobic endurance means climbing endurance at a low intensity level: the ability to do lots and lots of climbing.
- Splitting training into power, power endurance and endurance is a simplistic, one-dimensional view. Developing specific endurance in the musculature and in the whole system is the best way to develop endurance.
- General cardio does not improve endurance across all other activities. Endurance is extremely specific: you have to do the specific thing to get good at enduring that thing (as triathletes who ride, swim and run know).

## Where endurance can be limited
| Limit | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Uptake | The ability to breathe and bring oxygen into the lungs and across them (a mechanical or respiratory limit) | Can be solved with some general training, but is a really rare limiter |
| 2. Delivery | The ability of the blood to bind oxygen and bring it to the working muscles | Can also be worked on |
| 3. Utilization | The ability to take that energy to the muscles and use it to do hard moves on rock | Most climbers are limited here |

- Focus most endurance training on utilization, unless you have a real breathing problem or a real problem moving oxygen through the blood.

## The two-buildings model
- Picture an aerobic building and a strength building joined by a cable. The cable is power endurance: the ability to produce high power outputs while fatigued.
- The only ways to improve the cable are to make the aerobic building bigger and to make the strength building bigger: get stronger (stronger fingers, better bouldering) and develop a better aerobic engine.

## How to know you are in the aerobic zone
- **Warning:** Don't seek fatigue as the marker of endurance training (getting super pumped in the gym, recovering, getting super pumped again). It is too intense and doesn't allow enough volume, and volume is the huge key to building aerobic endurance. It is like training for a 10 km race by running as fast as possible until falling over, recovering a little, then sprinting again.

| Marker | Right intensity |
|---|---|
| Breathing | No labored breathing; you could maintain a conversation, or continue to breathe regularly, throughout the movements |
| Feeling | Light to moderate exercise but no pump: you don't feel it in your forearms or feel the need to shake out between moves; you can keep moving without having to stop and recover |
| End of session | You could keep going: do enough circuits, moves or sets, but still have some in the tank (you could do another pitch or another set) |

- At the end of 4x4 bouldering you are done: that is not the zone wanted. Aerobic endurance is lower intensity and longer duration than the anaerobic endurance usually associated with getting pumped.

## Why it is worth the time
- Aerobic fitness is a very persistent part of general capacity: once built, it sticks.
- It takes a long time to build, so be patient and set aside time in the climbing year to develop it, giving it the same attention as finger strength or bouldering.
