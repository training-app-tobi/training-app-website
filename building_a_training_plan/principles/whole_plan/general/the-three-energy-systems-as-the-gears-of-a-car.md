# The Three Energy Systems as the Gears of a Car

**Type:** Principles · **Trains:** Endurance, Power endurance, Power

**TL;DR:** The body supplies energy to the muscles through three systems, like the gears of a car: System 1 (anaerobic alactic, first gear) is very powerful but lasts only a few seconds; System 2 (anaerobic lactic, second gear) supplies most of the energy for about a minute and covers much of climbing; System 3 (aerobic, cruising) is very low power but lasts a long time. All three always contribute; your "easy" grade is System 3 territory, and raising it is one of the primary goals of your climbing.

## The three energy systems

- The body sends energy to the muscles at a few different levels, like the gears of a car. First gear is very powerful and gets you moving from a stop, but only for the first few seconds; it can't keep that rate, so you switch to second.

| System | Also called | Car gear | What it does | How long it lasts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| System 1 | Anaerobic alactic, creatine phosphate or ATP-CP system | First gear | Uses the energy already stored in the muscles; for high speed or high strength. System 2 delivers less energy, so you might not be able to keep doing pull-ups on it until it is trained | A few seconds: the first pull-up and a couple more |
| System 2 | Anaerobic lactic or glycolytic system | Second gear | Not good for starting from a stop, but lasts longer than System 1. Much of climbing is in this system | Supplies most of the energy for about a minute of activity before System 3 takes over |
| System 3 | Aerobic or oxidative system | Cruising | Very low power. The main system for daily activity, energy during sleep, walking and easy bike riding | Produces energy for a long time |

- Your "easy" grade is System 3 territory: the level you can climb comfortably with no fear of pumping out. It isn't stressful to start up the pitch, you can talk with your partner, and you recover easily after a strenuous move here and there. This grade is firmly in your aerobic zone, and one of the primary goals of your climbing should be to raise it.
- All three systems contribute energy to movement and to living at all times; the body relies on the "best" one for an activity depending on how long it lasts and how hard it is.
