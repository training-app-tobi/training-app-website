# Specific Training Rules for a Climbing Goal: Specificity, Volume and Intensity, and Your Super Power

**Type:** Principles, Goals & mindset · **Trains:** Technique, Finger strength, Endurance

**TL;DR:** Once the general goal rules are nailed down, three specific training rules decide whether the effort achieves the goal. Follow a regime specific to the goal's demands and build specificity over the year towards the goal date; match training volume and intensity to the goal's daily demand (e.g. about 400 m of climbing a day with V7 cruxes for a big wall); and identify the one thing holding you back and go after it relentlessly.

## Specific training rules
- Specific training rules decide whether the effort you put in will actually achieve the goal. Nail down the general rules (inspiring, realistic, demanding, fun) first; only then look at the specifics.

### Sport specificity
- Identify and follow a regime specific to the demands of your activity. You may love the campus board or hours of yoga, but big parts of a successful athlete's training cycle are highly specific to the goal.
- Build specificity over the year: start the year more non-specific and keep building specificity towards the big day (the goal date).

### Volume and intensity
- Match the volume and intensity of training to the goal.

| Goal | Daily demand | Train for |
|---|---|---|
| Big wall (El Cap) | About 400 m of climbing per day, crux grade V7 | Work that daily volume and crux intensity into your plan |
| 2-week bouldering trip (Rocklands) | 6–8 hard classics every other day | Train for that loading; training only 2–3 max efforts a day won't prepare you for it |

### Super power
- Identify exactly what is holding you back from your goal right now (finger strength, aerobic power, flexibility or something else).
- Work out what you must change and how much it will determine your success, then go after it relentlessly.
- Remind yourself of it every day if possible and keep your focus on that area.
