# Less Is More: Adding New Training Habits Slowly

**Type:** Planning, Goals & mindset

**TL;DR:** Adding many new training habits at once usually lasts 1–2 weeks before mental or physical exhaustion, because mind and body need 3–4 weeks to adapt. Introduce 1 or 2 small new protocols, commit to a 4-week block, then evaluate before adding more. How well a new protocol sticks depends on your why, your schedule, and tracking/organization.

## Why adding a lot at once fails
- Long-term gains come from doing the right things repeatedly. Consistency is a habit, and new habits are hard to start.
- **Warning:** Adding many new habits at once does not work. Example: a climber already climbing 3 times per week (projecting, board climbing, occasional supplemental training) gets inspired and adds daily hangboarding, 4x4s, stretching, consistent weight training, journaling and foam rolling all at once. These habits usually last 1–2 weeks before the climber is mentally or physically exhausted, because the brain and body can't adapt that quickly.
- Mind and body need 3–4 weeks to acclimate: to develop the neurological and physiological adaptations that sustain a higher work capacity and to build the base for more workload and more mental load. If this is rushed, you become mentally or physically fatigued to the point where you need to deload in both areas. For long-term progression, add slowly and steadily.

## How to add new protocols
| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1. Pick | Instead of starting a new, elaborate training program, introduce 1 or 2 new protocols and give them your full attention |
| 2. Commit | Do a 4-week training block with them |
| 3. Evaluate | Check whether they work and whether you have capacity to take on more |
| 4. Add more | Only once you have evidence that it works and is sustainable for you |

- "1 protocol" means something small: not, for example, a 2-hour mobility program with 8 exercises done 3 times per week. Start small, master it, make sure you are consistent and improving, and add more only once you have evidence that it works and is sustainable for you.

## What makes a new protocol stick
Three factors decide how "sticky" (sustainable) a new protocol is:

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Your why | Determines how much motivation you'll have for the new activity |
| Your schedule | Your free time outside climbing affects your motivation and capabilities |
| Tracking/organization | A reliable time slot and tracking increase consistency and motivation |

### Your why
- Your "why" determines how much motivation you'll have for the new activity. If you're not highly motivated by your goal, or the new activity doesn't directly help you reach it, your drive to keep doing it fades quickly.
- The less enjoyable the activity, the stronger your why needs to be: an activity you dread, done without a powerful why, rarely becomes a long-term practice.
- For an activity you don't enjoy, use habit stacking.

### Your schedule
- Your schedule outside climbing strongly affects your motivation and capabilities. The more free time you have, the easier it is to add new training sessions, feel prepared for them and be fully present to enjoy them.
- **Warning:** Adding several things to an already packed schedule leads to skipped or rushed sessions. Example: instead of giving 6 activities 30 minutes of practice every 5 days, the climber ends up giving 12 activities 15 minutes each every 8 days. This disproportionately reduces the benefit of everything practiced: a plan meant to improve results ends up diminishing them.

### Organization and tracking
- Set a reliable time slot for the new protocol: it reduces stress and increases consistency. Organizing work, life and training plans together prevents activities from overlapping and tasks from being forgotten.
- Track your progress. Tracking shows whether the protocols work and boosts motivation and confidence: when records show improved results, you're eager to continue. If results keep improving and your schedule allows, adding more practices and maintaining the habits you've built becomes easier.
