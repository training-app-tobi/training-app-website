# Six Rules for an Effective Training Framework

**Type:** Principles, Planning · **Trains:** Recovery

**TL;DR:** Successful training programs are built on six rules: have a reason, everything is too much, begin with the end in mind, what goes up must come down, recovery is a real thing, and get one better. Pick one or two specific qualities per program, start each phase way too easy and progress session after session, and make sure every session asks your body for a little more than the last.

## The framework

| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| 1. Have a reason | A legitimate goal that means something to you |
| 2. Everything is too much | Pick one or two specific qualities to develop per program |
| 3. Begin with the end in mind | Plan from the first session for where you want to be at the end of the cycle |
| 4. What goes up must come down | Build each quality beyond what your performance environment needs, then let it drop back |
| 5. Recovery is a real thing | Recover deliberately; treat rest days as recovery training days |
| 6. Get one better | Every session contains a way to advance |

## Rule 1: Have a reason
- The first and most important part of starting a training program is having a reason to do it.
- A legitimate goal that means something to you is what lets you carry out a training plan effectively.

## Rule 2: Everything is too much
- Don't try to work on everything you feel bad at (the typical "laundry list"). Pick one or two specific qualities to develop in a training program; that is how you succeed.
- During a specific training program, try to limit the loss of other qualities (e.g. endurance or strength), but put all your eggs in the basket of the program's goal.
- **Warning:** Going to the gym to get as tired as possible by doing every climbing workout you can think of gets you nowhere.
- **Warning:** Training a whole bunch of different, conflicting qualities at once gives very little progress.

## Rule 3: Begin with the end in mind
- From the very first session, think about the end goal of the whole training cycle. If you want to be stronger, more resilient and more injury-proof at the end, address that from the first session; being burned out, injured or overtrained at the end is also decided from the first session.

| Point in the training phase | Training |
|---|---|
| Start (week 1) | Way too easy, not the hardest training you can do |
| Through the phase | Progress session after session, so you can always give a little more |
| End | The hardest training you can do |

- **Warning:** Training "pretty hard" and about the same in every session for one, two or three months gives at best mediocre results.

## Rule 4: What goes up must come down
- However well a quality is developed (e.g. finger strength, upper-body endurance), it comes back down after a hard training phase.
- So training must build each quality beyond the level your performance environment needs, then let it drop back to the level that environment needs. Apply this to every facet of fitness.
- Cycle over cycle: peak a quality, let it come down, and start the next cycle before it has returned all the way to baseline, then build it up again.
- Over time your low points (when you don't feel fit) end up higher than your best performances of a few seasons ago.

## Rule 5: Recovery is a real thing
- As you age and as your performance level rises, you need to recover more effectively (more deliberately than just eating and sleeping).
- Treat rest days as "recovery training days". Taking recovery seriously gives better performances more often.
- The overall goal of training is the greatest number of good performances in a given time period. Recovering well, taking care of your body and using good recovery strategies lets you get better training in and reach higher performance levels.

## Rule 6: Get one better
- Every session should contain a way to advance.
- Bouldering for two hours doing various things that may or may not overload the body, with one or two harder problems, isn't necessarily progress: ask whether you tried a little harder than the previous time.
- Through a training cycle, keep asking your body for more and more.

| Setting | How to advance |
|---|---|
| Crag | Do an extra pitch or another burn on a hard route |
| Bouldering gym | Track sessions and add a little more volume or a little more intensity |
| Weight room | Add weight, reps or sets |
