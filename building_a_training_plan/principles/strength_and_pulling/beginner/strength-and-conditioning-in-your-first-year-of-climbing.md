# Strength and Conditioning in Your First Year of Climbing

**Type:** Principles, Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** General strength, Pulling strength, Antagonists & core · **For:** Beginner

**TL;DR:** Basic strength training off the wall is really important early on: it helps you avoid elbow and shoulder pains, execute the techniques you are learning and tolerate more climbing. Start with pull-ups and press-ups, add basic floor core (Spider-Man holds, dish tucks, moving plank variations), then deadlifts, and fit them in at the end of your climbing sessions, after all the skill work. Add supplementary exercises only if you have the time and capacity to recover.

## Strength and conditioning
- Basic strength training off the wall is really important in the early stage of climbing; it is not a distraction from the skill focus.
- Many beginners pick up elbow and shoulder pains. This may be getting more common with modern route setting on macro holds, where finger strength is not needed but hard pulling and pressing is. Doing the basics helps a lot with staying healthy.
- Basic strength also helps you execute the techniques you are learning; for example, a twist lock needs core tension and upper-body strength.
- Step 1, pull-ups and press-ups: they cover most upper-body muscles and build the capacity of these muscles and tendons to deal with all the climbing. Done with good form they are safe and reduce your risk of injury on the wall.
- Supplementary exercises (the "maybe" pile): shoulder press, bicep curls, low rows and face pulls are excellent examples, among many other options, but add them only if you have the time and capacity. Their aim is to increase the body's capacity to cope with climbing; if you are already doing a lot, you will max out your capacity to recover.
- Step 2, basic floor core: it is not climbing-specific, but at this stage don't get hung up on specificity; the aim is general athletic ability while you refine skills on the wall.

| Core exercise | What it trains |
|---|---|
| Spider-Man holds | Bracing tension between hands and feet |
| Dish tucks | General core conditioning and stability |
| Moving plank variations | General core conditioning and stability |

- Step 3, deadlifts: a great exercise for posterior-chain strength and core bracing, which you need for pushing and pulling with your feet; worth doing as a stand-alone exercise.
- **Warning:** if you are not confident with deadlift form, practise the form with an experienced trainer; you will get much more out of the lift.
- Start with the basics, such as press-ups and pull-ups, and fit them in at the end of your climbing sessions, after all the skill work.

## Why it works
- Strength and conditioning helps you avoid injuries and builds overall capacity to climb, so you can tolerate more climbing and spend more time learning skills without forced breaks for injury.
