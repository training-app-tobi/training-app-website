# Choosing Exercises That Transfer to Climbing: Underclings and Steep Lock-offs

**Type:** Principles, Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Pulling strength, General strength

**TL;DR:** Don't oversimplify a climbing movement into a weight-room exercise: biceps curls for underclings and bar pull-ups for steep lock-offs are good starting places but terrible places to end up. Look at how the movement is actually used on the rock (range of motion, duration, muscles involved, where you are weak) and progress from general strength to specific strength to applying it on the rock. For underclings, train with the arms extended overhead; for steep rock, train horizontal and rotational rowing.

## The basic mistake
- A basic mistake is to oversimplify a climbing movement and try to simulate it in the weight room.
- Common examples: biceps curls with a dumbbell to build strength for underclings, and pull-ups on a bar to build lock-off strength for steep rock.
- Each may be a good starting place for the skill, but it is a terrible place to end up: it doesn't give enough of what the movement needs.

## Underclings
- An undercling resembles a biceps curl in only one respect: palm facing up and pulling weight in toward the body.
- The curl's range of motion is where you are already strongest in an undercling: once the undercling is at chest level or below the shoulder, the move is easy.
- To strengthen underclings, extend the range of motion and integrate the movement with the rest of the body (shoulders, legs, core).
- If you are very weak in the biceps, a few sets of curls early in the season can help get you stronger.
- A better starting place is a reverse-grip (underhand) pull-up, starting with the hands far above you and pulling down: much more specific to the undercling motion.
- An undercling usually starts by reaching up to a hold above the head, then holding it with a compromised wrist and an extended arm: very unlike holding a dumbbell in a curl.
- To get into that body position: undergrip a TRX trainer held backward, or a pair of holds on a climbing wall, with the hands above the head; squat down below them and pull yourself back up, to feel how the movement engages muscles throughout the whole body.
- Then work on pulling all the way through the range of motion; the same can be done on a system board or bouldering wall.
- Think in stages: how to develop the general strength, how to develop more specific strength, and how to apply it on the rock. Move from curls to how the movement happens motorically, how long it lasts, and where you are really weak.
- Most climbers get weak in an undercling when the wrist folds back and they reach far above the head, or when holding a lot of tension with a straightened arm. Curls don't address either.
- Underclings are a rare movement type, so practising them (at a home gym, or set in the commercial gym where you train) makes you better quickly. This has nothing to do with standing in the weight room with dumbbells.

### Undercling progression
| Stage | Focus | Training |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | General strength | Reverse-grip pull-ups |
| 2 | Specific strength | Exercises with the arms extended above the head, integrating core tension |
| 3 | Movement positions | Actual movement positions on a campus board setup |
| 4 | Apply | On the bouldering wall or at the crag |

## Pull-ups and lock-offs on steep rock
- Pull-ups are an excellent exercise and a good foundation (general strength) for upper-body pulling, but may not be the most specific pulling exercise for climbing.
- Climbing usually gets difficult when it gets steep, where people struggle to hold a one-arm lock-off while rolled back on the rock. The horizontal rowing motion is more specific to most of that difficulty than pulling down on a bar.
- When pulling on steep terrain (40, 45, 50 degrees) there is very little lat involvement and a lot of the rowing muscles.
- Climbers tend to rotate while rowing/pulling on steep rock; simulate this in the gym with rotational rowing work.
- Building strength with weighted rows, or suspended rows on rings or TRX straps, is an excellent foundation for steep climbing.
- This is not mastered right away: it may take several seasons of going through general pulling, horizontal pulling, pulling on huge holds and pulling on actual steep problems before it feels like a skill you have mastered.

### Steep-climbing progression
| Stage | Focus | Training |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | General pulling | Pull-ups |
| 2 | Horizontal pulling | Rows, rotational rows (weighted, or suspended on rings or TRX straps) |
| 3 | Moving on steep terrain | Steep rock or a steep gym wall, integrating the foot positions and hip movement of steep terrain, on really big jugs you can hold easily so that the limiter is the rowing motion, not the holds |
| 4 | Apply | Actual steep problems / steep rock |

## Choosing off-rock exercises
- Make general strength exercises apply correctly to the specific demands on the rock: look at the main movements, how they are actually used on the rock, how long they take, the range of motion and the muscles involved, and choose off-season / off-rock training from that.
