# Leg and Lower-Body Training Is Underrated

**Type:** Principles · **Trains:** General strength

**TL;DR:** Many injuries come from doing no lower-body training. Weak hamstrings hurt heel hooks, strong legs (within reason) give better balance, control and a sense of where the centre of mass is, and a little leg strength helps avoid the knee injuries that come from cranking through the fingers out of a stuck high foot.

## Leg / lower-body training
- **Warning:** many injuries come from doing no lower-body training.
- Weak hamstrings hurt performance: heel hooks can't generate power or pull you up. They improve by making the legs stronger and practising heel hooks.
- Within reason, strong legs are good for climbing, though not the primary goal for most people.
- Strong legs give significant proprioceptive benefits: better balance and control, a better sense of where the centre of mass is over the feet, and they reveal movement-pattern, flexibility and mobility deficits.
- **Warning:** a common pattern is getting into a high foot, getting stuck, then cranking through the fingers to get out, which can injure the knee; a little leg strength would help.
