# Training Strength and Power Together

**Type:** Principles · **Trains:** General strength, Power, Finger strength

**TL;DR:** Strength and power are used together in almost every hard climbing move, so train them in the same sessions, more and more often as you approach performance phases. Qualities get separated to avoid training competing factors at once, and the biggest disruptions come from training different energy systems in the same workout, so plans increasingly develop capacities within one energy system instead of putting strength and power in different months.

## Strength and power

- Strength must be paired with skill, capacity and power. Power is the primary physiological need of performance rock climbing.
- Power = strength displayed with a speed component (strength x speed). All power movements fall somewhere along the force-velocity curve.
- You can show high force at lower speed (snatching a barbell) or lower force at higher speed (throwing a baseball). Most power use in climbing is in between, in a zone called "peak power".
- Strength and power are used together in almost every move of a hard route or boulder, so they should not be trained separately. Training them together more and more often as you get closer to performance phases makes gains come faster.
- How to build such a session (power first, progressive warm-up, long rests, stop when performance drops) is in the note Designing a Session With Strength and Power.

## Why qualities get separated

- The main reason to separate training qualities is to avoid training two competing factors of fitness at once. A high volume of anaerobic endurance activity limits strength gains; hours of intense intervals can block gains in anything else. You can't max everything all the time.
- **Warning:** the biggest disruptions come from training different energy systems within the same workout.

## Training within energy systems

- Training plans increasingly aim at developing capacities within specific energy systems rather than single load parameters or speeds. So climbers can train strength and power in the same sessions (possibly more effectively) rather than in different months.
- Training is about managing compromise, not finding a perfect balance.
- In climbing movement, strength and power are used concurrently in almost every move: holding a tiny crimp statically while a foot stabs explosively to a distant edge, the arms contract slightly and the hips drive powerfully to position the body. Separating these into individual training phases is difficult and foolish.
- Climbing performance combines high force, explosiveness, movement and timing. These are persistent qualities (easy to maintain) that degrade over time if ignored.
