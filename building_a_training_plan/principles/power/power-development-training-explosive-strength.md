# Power Development: Training Explosive Strength

**Type:** Principles · **Trains:** Power

**TL;DR:** Power is strength times speed: the faster you generate force, the more powerful you are, and the key in climbing is learning when to be explosive. An exercise trains power when doing it fast makes it more efficient. Start with a few general power exercises for a couple of cycles, then spend most of your time on specific power that moves like climbing: hip and knee extension and upper-back pulling. Power is one piece of the puzzle, to be combined with strength, mobility and a better head.

## What power is
- In sports science power has a narrow definition: strength times speed, the ability to generate force over time. The faster you can generate force, the more powerful you are.

| Part of the force-velocity curve | What it is |
|---|---|
| Very high force, low velocity | E.g. a maximal weighted pull-up or maximal deadlift: one slow, very heavy rep |
| Power zone (somewhere in the middle) | Doing things slightly more explosively makes us more efficient in sport |
| Very low force, high velocity | E.g. pitching a baseball |

## When to be explosive
- Climbing ranges from very slow, "slothful" movement (easy, very low-angle climbs) to super explosive movement (steep terrain on big holds). The key for climbers is learning when to be explosive and when not to be.
- Strength and power are really hard to uncouple. The old split (hangboarding = strength, bouldering = power, circuits = endurance) is not that clean: watching any bouldering (a World Cup boulder, someone on a Kilter board, a video of yourself in your gym) shows explosive moves, static moves and very slow moves mixed together, so we need to learn to move between them.

## Choosing power exercises
- To develop power, build exercises done at high velocity under some amount of high load. An exercise is powerful when doing it with speed makes it more efficient.
- Box jump: impossible to do slowly, because you never leave the ground. Get some momentum, explode and let the movement happen.
- Explosive pull-up: done quickly, you reach the top and hit the dead point where you could move on to another hold, as in campusing.
- Start power development with general power exercises, to teach the body the coordination, speed and timing that power requires.

| Phase | What to do |
|---|---|
| General | A few general power exercises for a couple of cycles, to get your power up. You don't need much general explosive work: it's about teaching the nervous system to do it and reminding connective tissue and muscles they can move fast |
| Specific | Spend most of your time on more specific power |

- The general and specific exercises (jumps and throws, kickboard campusing, power bouldering, jumping pulls) are in the note Power Exercises: General Power Work, Kickboard Campusing, Power Bouldering and Jumping Pulls.

## General versus specific movement patterns
- Most sport-specific power movement is extension of the hips and knees and pulling with the upper back; explosive pushing and hinging are rare in climbing performance.

| Phase | Exercises |
|---|---|
| General phase, or as assistance exercises | Explosive pushing and explosive kettlebell work are good |
| Specific explosiveness | Stick with exercises very much like climbing |

## Power in context
- Power is one piece of the puzzle: also develop greater strength, greater mobility and a better head on your shoulders.
- **Warning:** Don't get sucked into chasing more and more explosiveness: it's a discrete skill, used on a limited basis, that must be married with other skills to be applied correctly.
