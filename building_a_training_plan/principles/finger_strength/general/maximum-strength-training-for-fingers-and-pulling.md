# Maximum Strength Training for Fingers and Pulling

**Type:** Principles, Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Finger strength, Pulling strength · **For:** Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Elite

**TL;DR:** Maximum grip and pull strength is a common limitation and the single most important aspect of an effective training program. Train it with brief, near-maximal efforts that bring near-failure in around 10 seconds (3 to 5 reps or 90% of 1RM), with at least 3 minutes of rest between sets. Beginners just climb three days per week; intermediate and advanced climbers train strength twice per week; elite climbers two or three days per week.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Beginner | Climb three days per week: this yields plenty of sport-specific strength gains. No highly targeted strength training: it is not necessary or appropriate at this level. Training technique and movement skills is paramount. Any time spent on strength training goes to the antagonist, stabilizer and larger pulling muscles rather than the fingers |
| Intermediate to advanced (a couple of years of climbing or more) | Targeted strength training benefits you, and you have likely developed enough tendon strength to begin gradually progressive, climbing-specific strength training twice per week |
| Elite (years of climbing, very strong tendons) | A high-intensity strength and power training regimen two or three days per week. Breaking through the next performance plateau depends on it |

- **Warning:** For beginners, highly targeted strength training could lead to injury.

## Why maximum strength matters
- Maximum strength in the forearm flexor and pulling muscles is a common physical limitation in climbing. Gripping small holds, making a powerful pull and locking off with one arm all require the muscles to contract briefly with near-maximal force.
- Resting effectively on a barely adequate, medium-sized hold and expressing strength endurance on a pumpy sequence also depend on maximum strength. Therefore developing greater absolute grip and pull strength is the single most important aspect of an effective training program.

## How maximum strength is gained
- A higher level of maximum strength comes from increased neural recruitment, muscle hypertrophy and greater cellular stores of ATP-CP.
- The training goal is to maximize fiber recruitment and liberate ATP-CP at the highest possible rate for 5 to 12 seconds. So a properly executed maximum strength exercise uses a resistance high enough to produce near-failure in around 10 seconds.
- **Warning:** Any strenuous exercise performed for more than 10 to 15 seconds trains local endurance rather than strength, because longer efforts are fueled more by anaerobic glycolysis.

## How to train maximum strength
| Element | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Effort | Brief exercises of 3 to 5 reps, or 90% of 1RM |
| Rest between sets | Near-complete recovery, at least 3 minutes, so that every set is an all-out effort |
| Sets, intermediate | 3 |
| Sets, advanced | 4 |
| Sets, elite | 5 |

### Climbing-specific strength exercises
| Exercise | Load / duration |
|---|---|
| Bodyweight fingerboard hangs | Holds small enough to be difficult when held for 10 seconds; train different grips |
| Weighted (hypergravity) fingerboard hangs | Medium-sized holds with enough weight to make a 10-second hang very difficult |

- The pulling exercises (weighted pull-ups, one-arm lock-offs and one-arm pull-ups) are in the note Maximum Pulling Strength: Weighted Pull-ups, One-Arm Lock-offs and One-Arm Pull-ups.

## Bouldering versus targeted exercises
- Near-limit bouldering can help develop maximum strength, especially in sub-elite climbers.
- Except for beginners, more targeted supplemental exercises are essential to give the best stimulus for maximum strength gains: failure on boulders often comes from movement flaws, inadequate flexibility or lack of power, so bouldering does not necessarily make the grip or pulling muscles fail in under 12 seconds.
- Precise training that follows the principles of exercise science is paramount for further strength gains over the long term.
