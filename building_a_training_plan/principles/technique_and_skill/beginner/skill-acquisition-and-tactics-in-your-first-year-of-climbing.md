# Skill Acquisition and Tactics in Your First Year of Climbing

**Type:** Principles, Technique · **Trains:** Technique · **For:** Beginner

**TL;DR:** Skill acquisition is the highest priority in the first year: climb a lot, on every style, and learn from others. Think about how you climb, not just whether you reach the top: repeat climbs to refine your movement, brush holds, rest longer than you think between hard attempts, warm up properly, and learn to route read through second-go redpoints, flash attempts with visualisation and thinking like the route setter.

## Skill acquisition
- Skill acquisition should be the highest priority, especially for a complete beginner. Climbing is a skill sport with an ever-changing environment: no two climbs are the same. You need to learn to move well, but also to visualise movement, problem solve and build awareness of yourself. This is best done on the wall and takes a LOT of climbing.
- Climb a lot: if you can only get to a gym 2 times per week, spend most of that time climbing. There is an upper limit to what you can recover from, but you can still learn great skill on very easy terrain, where you can handle more volume.
- Get breadth of climbing styles, not just depth of volume: overhangs, slabs, comp style, roofs. Try not to shy away from any style; trying everything builds a foundation of skills and confidence early on.
- Climb with others and learn from them: watch what they do, ask how they did it, pay attention to climbers who are good at things you are not so good at, and ask strangers for advice.
- The social side of climbing is good for mental wellbeing: talk to others and be friendly; you may learn new things or make a friend.

## Tactics
- Think about the way you climb, not just whether you reach the top. Don't be afraid to climb things several times over. Look for ways to refine your movement: what was necessary and what was a waste of energy.
- Brush holds, especially holds that need friction, such as slopers and pinches.
- When trying something hard for your grade, rest a little longer than you think you need between attempts. Set a timer between attempts so you can track how long you rest.
- Warm up properly: working some flexibility into the hips and activating the upper-body muscles improves your movement.
- Learn to route read. Climbing is highly cognitive, not only physical. Route reading is learned mainly through experience: getting it wrong the first time is a lesson, but only if you reflect on what went wrong and what you can do differently.

Exercises to improve route reading:

| Exercise | How |
|---|---|
| Second-go redpoint | Reflect on your mistakes and aim to correct them on the second attempt |
| Flash / onsight (the next step) | Use visualisation: imagine yourself climbing it and act it out on the floor, then aim to climb it first try |
| Put yourself in the route setter's shoes | What were they thinking? A handhold is usually placed in an exact orientation for a reason; how does it relate to the other holds? |
