# Drill Work for Technical Skills: How Long, Regressing, Progressing and Focus

**Type:** Principles · **Trains:** Technique

**TL;DR:** Drill skills for about 20–30 minutes in total within a bouldering or rope-climbing session, and stop earlier if movement quality breaks down. Regress a drill you can't do (fewer moves in a row, slower or simpler movement, or less time more often) and progress one that is too easy (steeper wall, smaller or farther holds, more repetitions). Work on 1–2 skills for 1–2 months at a time, and decide exactly what to focus on.

## How long to drill
- Skill development (drills) within a bouldering or rope-climbing session: typically about 20–30 minutes in total. Around that time technical movement starts to degrade and fatigue sets in; those are the markers for when to stop.
- Stop before the 20–30 minutes if movement quality breaks down, e.g. feet skating all over the place, eyes darting from one point to another, movement feeling sporadic.

## Regressing a drill
- If you can't do the baseline drill, regress it. Example: in a power-bouldering part of a session (developing the skill of moving explosively and fast), if you can't link 3, 4, 5 or 6 powerful moves in a row, the drill needs regressing.

| Way to regress | How |
|---|---|
| Reduce the repetitions done in a row | Half the boulder instead of the whole problem, or link just 1–2 moves at a time |
| Slow the movement down | Move more slowly |
| Reduce the complexity of the movement | From a steep angle to a less steep or more vertical angle |
| Regress by frequency (counterintuitive) | Spend less time per session but do it more often. Example: struggling with power development → instead of 20–30 minutes of explosive work, do 5–10 minutes per session, 3–4 sessions a week. As progress comes, reduce the frequency and increase the duration of each session |

- Track progress: "I'm starting to be able to do 2–3 power moves in a row" shows capacity for the drill is improving.

## Progressing a drill
- If the drill is too easy, progress it: always work slightly against yourself, a little outside your comfort zone, by adding complexity or demand.

| Way to progress | How |
|---|---|
| Increase the angle | Steeper wall |
| Smaller holds | Make hand holds and footholds smaller |
| Farther holds | Make the distance between holds farther |
| More repetitions | Increase the repetitions within a drill session |
| Frequency | Reduce the frequency |

## How many skills at a time
- Don't focus on too much at once: spend about 1–2 months at a time working on 1–2 skills.
- Packing in lots of skill work at once may seem faster, but as a habit, 1–2 skills per month develops a whole, better climber over time and makes it easier to judge whether you are improving on those skills.

## Focused attention
- Stay specifically focused during drills: decide exactly what to pay attention to, rather than e.g. "footwork" globally.
- Footwork example: (a) notice how your eyes focus on your feet and track how often your attention drifts away from them; (b) try to grab each foothold with your toes as you step on it.
- Three positioning drills (rest anywhere, diagonal, banded climber) are in the note Positioning Drills: Rest Anywhere, Diagonal and Banded Climber.
