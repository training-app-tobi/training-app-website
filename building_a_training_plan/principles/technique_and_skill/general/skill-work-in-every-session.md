# Skill Work in Every Session: 10–20 Minutes on What Gives You Trouble

**Type:** Technique, Principles · **Trains:** Technique

**TL;DR:** Every climber is a candidate for better skills, even those who think they already "know" how to boulder. Practise skills in every session, 10–20 min on the movements, holds, angles and positions that give you trouble; the simple version is one skill for 10 min per session, for 10 sessions, before moving on. Find the skills to work on in what you avoid, or by comparing your grades across angles, hold types and settings.

## Who should do what

| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Every climber | Practice skills in every session, 10–20 min: isolate one skill, practice it for 10 min per training session, and repeat for 10 sessions before moving on (see Skill work) |

## Skill work

- Many climbers think that once they reach a certain level (e.g. V7) they "know" how to boulder, because they developed that ability by just climbing a lot. Instead of practicing climbing, they start training to be physically able to handle the demands of their current skills.
- Every climber is a candidate for developing better skills: simple body-positioning exercises, learning to better use momentum and timing, or getting better at moving on certain hold types and angles.

| Skill practice | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Per session | No need for e.g. an hour of heel-hook practice, but address the movements, holds, angles and positions that give you trouble, 10–20 min per session |
| Simple version | Isolate one skill and practice it for 10 min per training session |
| How long | Repeat that practice for 10 sessions before moving on to a different skill |

- Choosing which skills to work on: the answer is generally in the things you try to avoid. If you think you're well-rounded, compare your performances:

| Compare | What grades can you climb on… |
|---|---|
| Angles | Slab, vertical, 45° overhanging and horizontal |
| Hold types | Crimps, slopers, compression and pockets |
| Settings | Comp boulders vs board problems vs outdoor boulders |
