# Constraints-Led Approach to Climbing Technique

**Type:** Principles, Technique · **Trains:** Technique · **For:** Experienced climbers

**TL;DR:** Repetitive practice and common technique drills are inefficient for mastering climbing skills; variability in movement is the key to developing adaptability. Train movement coordination with the Constraints-Led Approach (CLA): use constraints and attractors, action-perception coupling, error amplification and affordances, and organize technique sessions that encourage exploration and creativity rather than one prescribed way of moving.

## Why variability beats repetition
- Repetitive practice and common technique drills are inefficient for mastering climbing skills. Variability in movement is the key to developing adaptability, which makes you a stronger and better climber.
- Breaking movements into parts and drilling them may work for beginners, but falls short for more experienced athletes; for them, use the Constraints-Led Approach (CLA) to train movement coordination.

## What CLA is
- Motor skill acquisition is non-linear, and each athlete's development is unique and keeps changing.
- CLA encourages exploration, creativity and individuality in how you move.

## Applying CLA to climbing practice
| Concept | What it does |
|---|---|
| Constraints and attractors | Understand and use them to refine your technique |
| Action-perception coupling | Train how your movements are shaped by what you perceive |
| Error amplification | Learn from mistakes to speed up skill acquisition |
| Creating affordances | Shape your environment (the climbing setup) to promote better movement solutions |

- Organize technique sessions so they foster self-organization and adaptability, encouraging exploration and creativity rather than one prescribed way of moving.

## Why it works
- Movement is self-organized and closely coupled with perception: for example, gauging the distance to a hold and generating the momentum needed to reach it are one integrated action. Training should work on this coupling rather than on isolated parts.
