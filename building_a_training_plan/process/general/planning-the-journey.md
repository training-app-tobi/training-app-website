# Planning the Journey: From Assessment to a Realistic Timeline

**Type:** Planning, Principles · **Trains:** Finger strength, Pulling strength, Endurance, Mobility · **For:** self-coached climbers

**TL;DR:** After the assessment, start with a broad view: set intermediate training goals as stop-offs along the journey, then join up the dots. Work backwards from each target to set a realistic timeline of small steps, monitor progress against it and reconsider the time frame if you fall behind. Make your weaknesses the main focus of the plan, e.g. an 8-week plan that progresses endurance and pull-ups much more than finger strength and flexibility.

## From assessment to plan: broad view first
- After the assessment you know where you are starting and have an idea of where you want to end up (weaknesses to address, goals). Now plan the journey from point A to point B.
- Start with a broad view and set the foundation before filling in the details: first set training goals as intermediate goals ("stop-offs") along the journey, then simply join up the dots by planning the journey between them.
- Without goals it is really easy to get lost in your training and not know where you're going. Some of the most accomplished climbers in the world are very good at setting meticulous goals, which makes their training very directed.

## Planning the timeline
- The best plans are realistic and achievable. To be realistic, break your training goals into small steps, so you can monitor progress along the way, see whether you're hitting these steps and whether the training was achievable.
- Method: set the target (e.g. 130%) at a point in the future and work backwards. This shows the small steps and the rate at which you plan to increase finger strength to reach the goal: the training now has a timeline, and you can see whether it looks realistic.
- The timeline also lets you monitor training along the way: if you're not progressing at the planned rate, look back at your goals and reconsider what is now a realistic time frame, according to your time, your energy and how much you can train.

## Example: 8-week plan
- Built on the four measures from the assessment: endurance, pull-up strength, finger strength and flexibility.
- Endurance and pull-ups, identified as weaknesses, are the main focus: across the 8 weeks they are progressed much more than the lower-focus measures.
- Making the weaknesses the focus of the plan improves your success in building on them.

| Measure | Focus |
|---|---|
| Endurance | Main |
| Pull-up strength | Main |
| Finger strength | Lower |
| Flexibility | Lower |
