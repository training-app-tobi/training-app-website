# Intermediate Training Goals and How Many Training Focuses to Choose

**Type:** Goals & mindset, Planning · **Trains:** Finger strength · **For:** self-coached climbers

**TL;DR:** Intermediate training goals are specific goals that work towards your big goal: start from what the assessment says to work on, make it specific and measurable (e.g. finger strength from 120% to 130% of bodyweight on a test), and you get both a focus and a framework for a timeline. Choose two, or maybe up to four, training focuses at most, or even just one: an over-complicated first self-coached plan is harder to stick to.

## Intermediate training goals
- Intermediate training goals are specific goals that help you work towards your big goal (usually improving performance, reaching a new grade or climbing a specific project).
- The assessment tells you whether to work on, for example, flexibility, finger strength or endurance: that is where the goal starts, but make it more specific so it directs the training. Example: a bouldering specialist works on finger strength and core strength to achieve the ultimate goal of climbing a harder grade.
- Make the goal measurable with numbers, referring back to the assessment. Example: a boulderer improving finger strength starts at 120% of bodyweight on a finger-strength test and wants to build to 130%, so the training has measurable direction.
- This gives two details for planning: a focus (finger strength) and a framework for a timeline: moving from 120% to 130% needs a realistic time frame in which to achieve that change.

## How many training focuses
- A self-written plan usually addresses several things. Choose two, or maybe up to four, training focuses, but no more, because the plan needs to be simple. It could even be just one focus (e.g. finger strength).
- If it is your first time writing a self-coached plan, don't overcomplicate it at this stage: an over-complicated plan is harder to stick to, and without a decent focus you may end up plateauing in several areas.
