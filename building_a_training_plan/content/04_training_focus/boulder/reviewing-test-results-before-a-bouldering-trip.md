# Reviewing Test Results Before a Bouldering Trip: Edge Sizes, Fitness and Mobility

**Type:** Test, Planning · **Trains:** Finger strength, Endurance, Power endurance, Mobility · **For:** preparing a bouldering trip

**TL;DR:** Compare finger strength on a 20 mm and a 10 mm edge against your current and goal grade: the added load on a 10 mm edge is generally around 50% of that on 20 mm, so a big shortfall marks small-edge strength as the area to improve. Check base endurance (recovery between attempts, session fitness) and power endurance (linking many moves), and whether you cover low mobility with strength or the reverse: if mobility is the weaker side, work on it slightly more than strength.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Mobility and strength in relative balance | Work on both |
| Mobility weaker than strength | Work on mobility slightly more than strength |

## Reviewing strengths and weaknesses from testing
- Worked example: a V9 boulderer going on a summer bouldering trip, 12 weeks available (start of April to mid-June), test results from a couple of weeks before planning.
- Compare finger strength on a larger edge (20 mm) and a smaller edge (10 mm) against your current grade and goal grade. Ask yourself whether you are better on smaller or larger edges, and what your climbing actually needs.
- Rule of thumb: the added load on a 10 mm edge is generally around 50% of the added load on a 20 mm edge. Example: +45 kg on 20 mm → expect about +22.5 kg on 10 mm; only +5 kg was managed, so small-edge strength stands out as the area to improve.
- General fitness: base endurance and power endurance both underperformed.

| Quality | Why a boulderer needs it |
|---|---|
| Base endurance | To recover between attempts and have good session fitness |
| Power endurance | To link many moves in a row: key for any longer problems |

- General conditioning, two areas: mobility underperformed (less of a concern for a slightly taller climber, but an area to improve, so it goes into the plan); general strength had translated well from gym training into test scores, so keep working on it, but it is not a weakness to address dramatically.
- Many climbers compensate for low mobility with more strength, or for low strength with more mobility. Ask which side you usually sit on.
