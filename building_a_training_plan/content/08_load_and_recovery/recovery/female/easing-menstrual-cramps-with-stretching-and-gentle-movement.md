# Easing Menstrual Cramps with Stretching and Gentle Movement

**Type:** Injury & health · **Trains:** Mobility, Recovery · **For:** Female, menstrual cramps

**TL;DR:** Menstrual cramps just before or during menstruation can hurt climbing and training. Stretching routines that lengthen the hamstrings, hip adductors and psoas have been shown in some studies to help reduce menstrual pain, and gentle movement, such as relatively easy, low-angled climbing, can reduce cramps. Experiment with different tactics and track your cycle each month to find what works for you.

## How cramps affect climbing
- Menstrual pain or cramps are a common symptom just before or during menstruation and can hurt climbing and training.
- They can be distracting, can make wearing a harness or weight belt uncomfortable, or can be bad enough that you don't want to get out of bed.

## Approaches that may ease cramps
- Loosen the hamstrings, hip adductors and psoas. Climbers use all these muscles and they may get tight. In some studies, stretching routines that lengthen these muscles (which connect to the pelvis or affect its position) have been shown to help reduce menstrual pain.
- Keep moving: gentle movement can be effective for reducing cramps. Relatively easy, low-angled climbing could help climbers who have painful periods.

## Track and experiment
- Experiment with different tactics and track your cycle each month (a cycle-tracking app helps) to see what makes a difference for you over time.
- Cycles vary a lot from person to person and from one cycle to the next, so keep trying until you find what works.
