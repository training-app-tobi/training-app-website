# Rest Is Training: Rest Days, Building Capacity and Deload Weeks

**Type:** Principles, Planning · **Trains:** Recovery

**TL;DR:** Rest is training: passive rest days are essential when you stack high-intensity sessions, and they need complete rest, because even a 30-minute light hangboard session on a day off can tip your stress balance. Build capacity by growing total weekly load by 5–10% at submaximal RPE 4–6, 3 to 4 days a week. Every 4 weeks take a deload week that keeps all the training but halves intensity and volume and shifts towards skill.

## Rest is training
- Rest is not "not training", it is training: it is when the body builds back strength. Different types of rest serve different purposes:

| Type of rest | Use |
|---|---|
| Passive rest (full days off, no climbing or intense exercise) | Essential when you are stacking high-intensity sessions or coming off a heavy week |
| Active recovery | Can be even more powerful when used properly |

- **Warning:** Don't sneak more work into rest days ("just a quick core session", "just a couple of weighted pull-ups"). Every little session adds up: even a 30-minute light hangboard session on a day off can tip your stress balance the wrong way and delay recovery from a hard limit-bouldering day 2 days earlier. Rest days need complete rest.
- When the balance is right, training stacks instead of dragging fatigue from session to session: you show up fresh, perform better, and adaptations come faster.

## Building capacity
- Building capacity means adding more and more total load to your week so the body (mainly soft tissue) tolerates more load, recovers more efficiently metabolically, clears waste products from the muscles, and becomes more robust.
- High intensity builds maximal strength and power; capacity is total load (volume × intensity: reps × weight of each rep), and total load is much easier to increase at lower intensity.

| Curl example | Total load |
|---|---|
| 4.5 kg × 10 reps | 45.5 kg |
| 4.5 kg × about 300 reps possible | 1,361 kg |
| 22.5 kg × 6 reps (a hard max-strength set) | 136 kg |
| 22.5 kg × 6 reps, 3–5 sets | 408–680.5 kg, still about half |

| Capacity training | Do this |
|---|---|
| Load | Submaximal (not as light as the example), at RPE 4–6: still good-quality skill practice and enough strength adaptation, not boring and not junk volume; a little pump is wanted for metabolic reasons |
| Progression | Grow total load each week by 5–10% (sometimes more; up to 15% in highly trained athletes) |
| Duration | 4 weeks, 8 weeks, or as long as you want to build capacity |
| Frequency | 3 to 4 days a week; many workout styles work |

## Deload weeks
| Deload | Do this |
|---|---|
| When | Every 4 weeks |
| What | Not a complete rest week: keep all the training you are doing |
| How much | Cut intensity and volume by half |
| Style | Shift towards skill so complexity stays high and you keep developing skills during that week |
