# Use RPE and the Intention of the Session, Not Fixed Grades or Loads

**Type:** Principles, Planning · **Trains:** Recovery · **For:** Female, energy that varies from week to week

**TL;DR:** Energy fluctuates across the menstrual cycle, so sticking rigidly to last week's grades or weights can lead to frustration, inconsistent training and unnecessary fatigue: use RPE and focus on the session's intention instead (e.g. the powered-out feeling in Boulder Triples). Progress is a general trend over time, rarely linear from one session to the next; still note the weights and grades you use and review them after a couple of cycles.

## Use RPE and focus on the intention of the session
- Grades or weights are often used to find the right intensity for a session, but fluctuations in energy are common across the menstrual cycle, so sticking rigidly to last week's intensity may lead to frustration, inconsistency in training and unnecessary fatigue. Use RPE and focus on the intention of the session instead.

Example, Boulder Triples: in the follicular phase you feel good and use V5s; the following week this feels too hard and you fail early in the session.

| Option | Result |
|---|---|
| Continue | You get frustrated |
| Rearrange the session | It moves to another day |
| Adjust and focus on getting the right level for that session | Focusing on the intent (e.g. achieving the powered-out feeling in Boulder Triples) rather than the grade or the added weight lets you hit the right intensity for that day |

- The aim of training is a general trend of progression over time, and this is rarely linear from one session to the next (menstrual cycle or not).
- Focusing on the intention rather than external weights or grades also helps avoid negative self-talk when training fluctuates ("I am weak", "I'm rubbish at training"); avoiding it helps you enjoy training, be more consistent and likely make better progress.
- Still note the weights and boulder grades you use, as this information is useful, but check it after a couple of cycles for a better idea of progression.
- Understanding why sessions feel harder (RPE higher than in a previous session) can itself be enough to motivate you to push through. This can be empowering.
- **Warning:** Pushing through only works as long as it isn't done to excess and doesn't cloud an objective view of training load and recovery.
