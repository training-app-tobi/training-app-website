# The Menstrual Cycle and Injury Risk

**Type:** Injury & health, Planning · **For:** Female

**TL;DR:** Soft tissue injury risk is greater in the late follicular phase (around ovulation) and in the days after menstruation was expected to start (a late period). Around ovulation, be aware of muscle and tendon injuries: train flexibly when fatigued, avoid higher-intensity or higher-coordination sessions while tired, and keep track of time in high-intensity sessions so you stop strong.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Recovering from a soft tissue injury | The late follicular phase may not be the best time to progress exercises, to experiment with introducing climbing, or to increase the intensity of climbing |

## What the research shows
- A longitudinal study over a 4-year period found that soft tissue injury risk was greater in the late follicular phase (around ovulation) and in the days after menstruation was expected to start (i.e. a late period).
- The study used injuries recorded in footballers, so the body areas under stress in training or play are likely different from climbing (although knee and lower-limb injuries do occur in climbing). The takeaways are still worth being aware of for training.

## Late follicular phase (around ovulation)
- **Warning:** Be aware of muscle and tendon injuries during the late follicular phase (around ovulation).
- If you are fatigued from training in this phase, take a flexible approach to training and avoid higher-intensity or higher-coordination sessions while tired. Be aware of your injury "hotspots" and work around them.
- **Warning:** This phase can be associated with high energy, so keep track of time during high-intensity sessions and stop strong.
