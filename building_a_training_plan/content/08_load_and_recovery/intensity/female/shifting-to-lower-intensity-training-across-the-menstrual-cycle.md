# Shifting to Lower-Intensity Training Across the Menstrual Cycle

**Type:** Planning · **Trains:** Recovery · **For:** Female, menstrual-cycle symptoms

**TL;DR:** When fatigue, cramps, or reduced motivation and energy make high-intensity training feel less accessible, reduce the intensity of training or climbing, or swap some higher-intensity sessions for lower-intensity ones, so the overall load suits that point in the cycle. Lower-intensity work counts as training (see the note Types of Lower-Intensity Work), and valuing it gives the flexibility to keep energy levels and training quality consistent. Not all higher-intensity training needs to stop.

## When to shift intensity
- In some phases of the menstrual cycle, high-intensity training may feel less accessible because of fatigue, cramps, or reduced motivation and energy. In those phases, reduce the intensity of training or climbing, or swap some higher-intensity sessions for lower-intensity ones, so the overall load suits that point in the cycle.
- The late luteal phase and menstruation are two phases where these symptoms are commonly reported. Everyone is different: track your cycle and find out how these phases feel for you.

## Valuing low intensity
- Lower-intensity training or climbing is not a "failure", "lazy", or less important, and it counts as training.
- The first step in adjusting training to the cycle is appreciating the range of intensities: not all training requires 10/10 effort, and giving 10/10 may miss the intended training stimulus entirely.

## Keeping training consistent
- Not all higher-intensity training needs to stop during cycle phases when symptoms are experienced, but valuing lower-intensity exercises gives the flexibility to shift focus and keep energy levels and training quality consistent.
