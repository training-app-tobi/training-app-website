# Strength Training During the High Hormone Phase

**Type:** Planning · **Trains:** General strength, Finger strength · **For:** Female

**TL;DR:** Strength training adaptations have been shown to be better in the follicular phase than in the luteal phase, but strength training should not stop in the high hormone phase. Keep it going: reduce volume, shorten high-intensity sessions, maintain loads and use RPE to guide load.

## Strength adaptations across the cycle
- Studies have shown adaptations to strength training to be better in the follicular phase (rising estrogen and low progesterone) than in the luteal phase (high estrogen and progesterone).
- **Warning:** Don't put all strength training in the low hormone phase and stop it in the high hormone phase. Climbing relies heavily on strength, and training strength for half a cycle then stepping away for the other half gives peaks and troughs in training load and an inconsistent training stimulus. Keep strength training going in the high hormone phase.

## High hormone phase adjustments
| Adjustment | Detail |
|---|---|
| Reduce the volume of strength training | Allows more recovery time and keeps sessions high quality |
| Reduce the length of high-intensity strength sessions | Recovery is easier; e.g. do half a session and allow a longer warm-up period |
| Maintain loads (don't push them up) | Consolidates strength in strength-based conditioning or fingerboard sessions |
| Use RPE to guide load | Focus on the intention of the session when setting the difficulty |

## Why it works
- In the high hormone phase the body is in a glycogen-sparing mode, which limits the fuel available for high-intensity training.
