# Types of Lower-Intensity Work: Aerobic Training, Movement Practice and Accessory Exercises

**Type:** Principles, Planning · **Trains:** Endurance, Technique, Antagonists & core

**TL;DR:** Low-intensity training with very little or no pump develops the aerobic system and is an easy stimulus to hit when you can't hit higher-intensity work; it should feel like you could stay on the wall for a very long time. Low-intensity climbing is a good time to work on fluid movement and on timing your breathing with your movement, and accessory exercises load smaller muscle groups at lower intensity, with less systemic fatigue and shorter recovery.

## Types of lower-intensity work
- **Low-intensity training:** with very little or no pump, it develops the aerobic energy system. An increasing pump means an increasing contribution from the anaerobic (usually the lactic) system.
- When targeting only the aerobic system, it should feel like you could stay on the wall for a very long time, so it is an easy stimulus to hit when you can't hit higher-intensity training. Raising the intensity isn't bad in itself, but it changes the range of adaptations you get.
- **Low-intensity climbing:** a good time to focus on fluid movement and on timing your breathing with your movement, a skill that helps you stay present and focused when climbing.
- **Accessory exercises:** these often use a lower intensity to target smaller muscle groups. This intensity gives those muscles the right load, and the systemic fatigue is lower, so recovery takes less time.
