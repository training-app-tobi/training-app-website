# Upper and Lower Bounds for Sessions, and Training Without Fatigue

**Type:** Principles, Planning · **Trains:** Recovery · **For:** returning after a layoff, injury or illness, Older

**TL;DR:** Dedication to a fixed routine becomes a problem with injury, layoffs or ageing: keep the time slot but give each session an upper and a lower bound (e.g. around 25 attempts in a hard bouldering session, with 6–8 still good value), without trending towards always doing the minimum. Judge training over months, not one session, and train to do as much useful work as possible without fatigue: it is better to undertrain by 5% than to overtrain by even 1%.

## Who should do what

| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Coming into the session beat up, coming off illness, or short on time | It's OK to go hard, but not now |
| Detrained | 2–4 sessions at the lower bounds are not only OK but probably much more effective than coming back to the gym and punishing yourself |

## The problem with "your routine"

- **Warning:** climbers are creatures of habit. One of the biggest problems with injury, layoff or even aging is dedication to a fixed "routine", e.g. always training three hours on Tuesday night, and getting frustrated and demoralized when you can't make time for it or can't climb or train as hard as last year.
- Build upper and lower bounds for your sessions: keep the time slot (e.g. Tuesday night), but have a maximal and a minimal duration. If you've only got 90 min, do a shorter session; if you have a full three hours, do a long one.
- Too often we follow what the app says and stop paying attention to what the body says.
- Look at training over a month, six months or a year: nothing of any use can be measured from this session, now. The last forty sessions show a whole lot about you, what you're capable of, what kind of injuries might be coming, and more.

| Session | Upper bound | Lower bound |
|---|---|---|
| Difficult bouldering session | Around 25 attempts | 6–8 attempts still give good value (not ideal, but better than skipping the session) |
| Strength session | 5 sets per exercise (as planned) | Occasionally only 1–2 sets |

- **Warning:** sessions can stay flexible as long as you don't trend toward always doing the minimum (just one set of each exercise and 8 boulders).

## Train without fatigue

- Only on rare occasions should sessions be fatiguing: the goal of an athlete's training is to do as much useful work as possible without fatigue.
- **Warning:** no climber would go to the crag trying to get destroyed as quickly as possible, consciously seeking out the flash pump, and expect positive long-term results, yet climbers do it in the gym all the time.
- It is better to undertrain by 5% than to overtrain by even 1%: if you aren't destroyed by today's session, you can get more done tomorrow.
