# Progressive Overload From Your Own Training History: Volume First, Intensity Later

**Type:** Principles, Planning · **Trains:** Finger strength · **For:** self-coached climbers

**TL;DR:** Progressive overload means adding load to the body progressively over time so it adapts; it has to happen slowly, and the rate depends on your own training history, not someone else's (e.g. someone new to hangboarding builds in one short session at first, someone already doing two a week can add faster). Build volume first, especially with training methods that are new to you, and bring intensity in later: with the build-up to a trip, peak season or competition, or in blocks that consolidate strength.

## Progressive overload
- To improve you need to get stronger, fitter, more flexible, whatever the goal. The principle for this is progressive overload: if you add load to the body progressively over time, the body adapts to handle that load.
- This needs to happen slowly and progressively over a time period.
- To plan progressive overload, first appreciate your training history and starting point.
- **Warning:** Don't follow someone else's plan based on their training history.

The rate of build-up depends on training history (hangboarding example):

| Training history | Rate of build-up |
|---|---|
| Starting hangboarding, never hangboarded before | Build it in very slowly in the first few weeks: maybe just one session, and a really short one, so it is gradually built into the plan |
| Two hangboard sessions a week for the past few months | Can add volume or intensity at a quicker rate |

## Volume first, intensity later
- The primary focus at the beginning of a training plan is to build volume, not intensity, especially if the training methods are new to you.
- This builds movement awareness, confidence and robustness in the body. Being able to tolerate the training you're doing is one of the most important factors in progressing.

| Situation | How intensity is placed |
|---|---|
| Building for a climbing trip, a peak season or a competition | Building intensity often comes later in the plan and coincides with that build |
| Not training to perform at your best at any particular point in the year | Simply add blocks of intensity or recruitment to consolidate strength, then build back into a volume-building block later in the year |
