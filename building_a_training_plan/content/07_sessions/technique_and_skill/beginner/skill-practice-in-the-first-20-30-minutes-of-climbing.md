# Skill Practice in the First 20–30 Minutes of Climbing

**Type:** Technique, Session · **Trains:** Technique · **For:** Beginner

**TL;DR:** Before going to your project, spend the first 20–30 min of climbing on skill development while fresh and cognitively sharp: deliberate practice on the warm-up boulders (repeat a climb if it doesn't feel right), a technique you are weak at, or a style you avoid. On repeats, swap heels and toes, hit the good part of each hold directly with fewer hand adjustments, and make the moves more static.

## Skill practice: first 20–30 min of climbing
- Before going to your project, spend 20–30 min focused on skill development at the very beginning of the session, while fresh and cognitively sharp. Makes a big difference to the rate of progression in the first couple of years of climbing.

| Option | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1. Deliberate practice | Climb the warm-up boulders as normal, with as much focus as possible on moving efficiently: good technique, relaxing in the right places, weight over the feet. If it doesn't feel right, repeat the climb. It is not about getting to the top but being introspective and refining movement; it doesn't need a specific drill |
| 2. A specific technique | Choose a technique you are weak at (e.g. good at heel hooks, bad at toe hooks → look around the gym for toe-hook problems). If your gym doesn't set that style, make up your own, e.g. eliminates or "plus one" sessions with friends |
| 3. A style you avoid or dislike | E.g. comp slab: even if you can't do it from the start (e.g. only from one move in), you are still learning and developing skill, so it is worth adding at the beginning of the session |

- What to change on repeats: try a heel instead of a toe (or the reverse) and keep the one that works better; once you know where the good part of a hold is, hit it directly and climb with as few hand adjustments as possible; aim to make the moves more static (even to a sloper); a third go may bring further changes.
