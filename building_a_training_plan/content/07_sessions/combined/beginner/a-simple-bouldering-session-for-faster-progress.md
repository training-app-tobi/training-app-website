# A Simple Bouldering Session for Faster Progress

**Type:** Session, Principles · **Trains:** Technique, Finger strength, General strength · **For:** Beginner

**TL;DR:** Build each session on four principles: warm up the fingers on a fingerboard and do flexibility work, spend the first 20–30 min of climbing on skill practice while fresh, then do either a boulder mileage session or a limit bouldering/projecting session, and finish with optional supplementary strength and conditioning. A mileage session builds capacity and skill on up to 20 flash-level boulders with 2.5 min rest; a limit session spends at least 20 min on each of 3 boulders at your limit, with 5–10 min rest.

## Session outline

| Order | Block | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Warm-up | Fingers on a fingerboard, then flexibility work |
| 2 | Skill practice | 20–30 min while fresh |
| 3 | Climbing | A boulder mileage session or a limit bouldering/projecting session |
| 4 | Supplementary strength and conditioning | Optional |

- The warm-up, skill practice and supplementary strength blocks are described in the notes Warming Up the Fingers on a Fingerboard, Five Flexibility Stretches for the Warm-Up, Skill Practice in the First 20–30 Minutes of Climbing, and Simple Supplementary Strength: One Pull, One Push and One Hip Hinge.

## Climbing: two session types
- Advanced athletes' plans put a lot of structure into a climbing session so it holds an intention, but in the first stages of climbing there is no need to be too prescriptive: two sessions do most things, a boulder mileage session and a limit bouldering/projecting session.

| | Boulder mileage session | Limit bouldering/projecting session |
|---|---|---|
| Intensity | Slightly lower: still challenging but low enough to put a lot of focus into movement and refining skills (lower intensity gives more ability to focus) | At your limit: you won't send all of them in the session, possibly some if you refine them and work really hard |
| Boulders | Flash-level boulders: try to flash each one, but 2 or even 3 attempts are fine if it is above flash level. Build up to 20 boulders maximum (a big session) | 3 boulders, at least 20 min on each |
| Rest | 2.5 min between boulders; after 10 boulders, a 10 min rest before the second half | 5–10 min between boulders; rest per attempt depends on how far you get on the boulder |
| Purpose | Builds capacity (tolerance to more and more training) and skill development | Refines the skills learned in other sessions and trains executing them in a high-intensity environment; builds strength: finger, upper-body and hip strength are built primarily in these sessions |

- In a mileage session, if a boulder doesn't go within those attempts and you still have many boulders to do, move on and save it for the next session.
- Projecting tips: work individual moves; you don't always have to start from the bottom and go to the end; climb up other problems (e.g. jugs on a nearby climb) to reach and try individual moves; skipping or not using a hold is fine if that method works better. Once you have a good idea of the moves, try to do it in one go.
- When the fingers are tired and the skin is sore, you can end the session there; but to progress and stay healthy, follow with a little supplementary strength and conditioning.
