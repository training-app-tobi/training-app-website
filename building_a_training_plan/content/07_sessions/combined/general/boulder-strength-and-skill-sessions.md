# Boulder, Strength and Skill Sessions

**Type:** Session, Principles · **Trains:** Technique, General strength, Pulling strength, Finger strength, Power, Antagonists & core, Mobility

**TL;DR:** A session should warm you up properly, overload the right target and leave you able to do the next session. Three session formats combine a lot of movement, some supplemental strength and power work, and skill practice: Brick-Style Training, Integrated Boulder and Skill-Strategy-Strength. Start by replacing one session every two weeks with one of them, then one per week, eventually two per week.

## Who should do what

| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Limited by time | Shorten Integrated Boulder by dropping from 3 sets of each group to 2 |

## What a session should do

- A detailed year-long training plan can help, but getting your individual training sessions right is the first step.
- A session should do three things: optimize the body's ability to adapt (by warming up), overload the body properly (by aiming at the right target), and do the right amount of work so you can effectively come back and do the next session.

## Where session structure goes wrong

- **Warning:** A simple look at the structure of a climber's usual sessions (e.g. a Monday/Thursday format) is often all it takes to see where they are stuck. Common faults: warming up just by climbing; climbing only on limit problems; sticking to one training board, often at only one angle; bouldering well past the "stimulus" point.
- **Warning:** The big issues behind these faults: most climbers think they don't need to improve their skills and that training longer will somehow make them stronger, and they end up needing extended recoveries between sessions.

## Three session formats

- Skill work in every session and supplemental strength on boulder days are covered in the notes Skill Work in Every Session: 10–20 Minutes on What Gives You Trouble, and Supplemental Strength on Boulder Days.
- Three session formats combine lots of movement, some supplemental strength and power movements, and attention to developing sport skill:

| Session | Aim |
|---|---|
| Brick-Style Training | Be efficient with your time and bolster rest between hard boulder problems |
| Integrated Boulder | Keep developing high levels of strength in the weight room while getting back into the mode of movement |
| Skill-Strategy-Strength | Combine skill focus and hard climbing, with an emphasis on using your brain intentionally during the session |

## Session 1: Brick-style training

- Format: a short block of bouldering alternated with a set of strength exercises or mobility drills. Goal: be efficient with your time and bolster rest between hard boulder problems. Standard format: about 10–15 min per brick, 3 rounds of each.

| Block | What to do |
|---|---|
| Warm-up | Movement prep 5 min, then a boulder ladder starting 3 grades below your onsight level; once at onsight level, do 3 problems (e.g. V1, V2, V3, V4, V4, V4) |
| Skill set | 10 min practicing a critical skill. Select a discrete skill, practice it, assess your performance (possibly on video), plan your next set of practice, and start the process again. Often only 3–4 problems get done in the 10 min |
| Boulders | 6–8 medium-to-hard boulders |
| Strength | 2 exercises, 4 sets each, 5–8 reps per exercise (e.g. Bulgarian split squat + knees to elbows) |
| Boulders | 6–8 medium-to-hard boulders |
| Strength | 2 exercises, 4 sets each, 5–8 reps per exercise (e.g. power push-ups + inverted rows) |
| Boulders | 6–8 medium-to-hard boulders |

- **Warning:** The point of breaking the work up this way is to reduce accumulated fatigue, not to let you "go deeper" into it.

## Session 2: Integrated boulder

- Similar in structure to the classic integrated strength session, except that instead of simply hanging from an edge for finger strength you do a couple of boulder problems, and the resistance exercises are adjusted to more specific movements or loading. Aim: keep developing high levels of strength in the weight room while getting back into the mode of movement.
- Use strength-focused boulder problems: ones where difficult holds, hard body positions or tension are the limiters. Avoid power-oriented, balance or excessively long problems; steeper walls with small holds are best.

### Specialized resistance

Integrated Boulder is an excellent time to train it:

| Form | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Unilateral strength | Exercises done with one limb or side at a time. Typically more core-intensive than bilateral exercises; help develop balanced strength | Lunges, single-arm push-ups, step-ups, single-arm pulls |
| Isometric strength | Holding static positions under load. A great way to build strength quickly, but ideally just one or two isometrics per session. Holds of 5–10 s are a good place to start, and a drive to exert maximum force is key to progress | Lock-offs are the standard, but isometrics can be done in almost any movement pattern |
| Concentric-only strength | In climbing you often pull down hard under load, then reach up with an unloaded arm to grab the next hold, a different load from typical exercises. Less taxing than a full up-down cycle, with similar strength gains | Do a pull-up, but instead of lowering from the top, step off onto a box |

### Session set-up

- A typical Integrated Boulder group: 2× strength-focused boulder problems; one unilateral, isometric or concentric-only exercise; one mobility or flexibility drill.
- Session: 3 sets of each group, done circuit-style.

| Group | 1. Strength-focused boulder problems | 2. Unilateral, isometric or concentric-only exercise | 3. Mobility or flexibility drill |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | A1: 2× strength-focused boulder problems | A2: Deadlift with drop, mid-thigh pull or single-leg deadlift, 2–5 reps | A3: Hip mobility, 60 s |
| B | B1: 2× strength-focused boulder problems | B2: Single-arm push-up, isometric bench or single-arm overhead press, 4–6 reps | B3: Shoulder mobility, 60 s |
| C | C1: 2× strength-focused boulder problems | C2: Pistol squat, step-up or squat hold, 4–6 reps | C3: Hip mobility, 60 s |

- In total, 18 boulder problems.
- The climbing, switching shoes and moving around the gym take time: if you are limited by time (sessions of an hour or so), shorten the workout by dropping from 3 sets of each group to 2.

## Session 3: Skill-strategy-strength

- Combines skill focus and hard climbing, with an emphasis on using your brain intentionally during the session.

| Block | What to do |
|---|---|
| Warm-up | General warming for 2–4 min (rowing machine or ski erg), then range-of-motion and light strength work for another few minutes |
| Skill block | Two skill exercises, one movement-based on the wall and one not, alternated for a total of about 20 min. Example: a drill requiring an inside high step (foot placed within the frame of the shoulders, not out to the side) on easy boulders, followed by a band-resisted high-step exercise on the floor |
| Apply the skill | Actively consider when the skill is best used, and possibly try some harder problems where it may come into play |
| Strength block | E.g. an Integrated Boulder group, 3 rounds of: A1: 2× strength-focused boulder problems; A2: single-leg deadlift, 3 reps each side; A3: box piriformis stretch, 30 s each side |
| Bouldering | Medium-to-hard problems (onsight grade −1 to onsight grade +1) for 30–50 min |

- After each set of skill work, do a brief analysis ("How were my hips in this move?", "Am I pulling enough with my flexors?"); note any revelations and keep focused on nuance.
- Skill is very responsive to training, unlike many favorite ("pet") exercises, yet far too many experienced climbers dismiss skill training.

## How to progress

| Stage | How often to use the templates |
|---|---|
| Start | Replace just one session in your schedule every two weeks with one of the templates |
| If you like the formats | One per week |
| Eventually | Two per week |

- Improvements come slowly, but they come.
- The more you address any specific skill, the sooner it comes around.
