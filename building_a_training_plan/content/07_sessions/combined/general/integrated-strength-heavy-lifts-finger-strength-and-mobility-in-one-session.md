# Integrated Strength: Heavy Lifts, Finger Strength and Mobility in One Session

**Type:** Session, Planning · **Trains:** General strength, Finger strength, Mobility, Antagonists & core · **For:** in-season

**TL;DR:** Do just enough heavy weightlifting, heavy finger strengthening and mobility to maintain those qualities through a performance phase. A workout has three circuits of about 15 minutes, each with one heavy strength exercise (2–4 reps), one dynamic mobility exercise and one short set of hard 3 s finger hangs, done three times. Train it about twice a week in-season or pre-season, keep adding load, and keep showing up.

## What integrated strength is
- Strength training for climbing is a continuum that runs from weight training to hanging on very small edges, and every place along that continuum is an appropriate place to train.
- Purpose: do just enough heavy, high-intensity weightlifting, just enough heavy finger strengthening and just enough mobility and flexibility training to maintain those qualities through a performance phase.
- Frequency: about twice a week during an in-season phase, or also during a pre-season phase.
- The mobility and finger strength work placed with each main lift serves two purposes: it makes you rest enough between heavy sets, and it keeps a little finger strength work and a little mobility work in the program.
- The volume of these sets is very small, so you should be able to do all your normal climbing training in between without much problem.
- Strength training throughout the season is essential to maintain strength and avoid significant loss, so you don't have to regain the same base level every year.
- In the prime of your climbing career you should be getting a little stronger each season.

## Workout structure

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Workout | 3 circuits (blocks), each about 15 minutes |
| Each circuit | 1 heavy strength exercise, then 1 mobility exercise, then 1 finger strength exercise |
| Rounds | Go through each circuit 3 times |
| Block 1 | Leg (lower body) strengthening exercise |
| Block 2 | Upper body strengthening exercise |
| Block 3 | Hinging or core exercise |

### Example block

| Step | Exercise | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Heavy deadlift | 3 sets |
| 2 | Mobility exercise | About 30–50 s |
| 3 | Finger strength | 2 minutes |
| Repeat | Go through the whole thing three times | About 15 minutes |

- The next block is the upper body exercise, another mobility exercise and another finger strength exercise.

## Heavy strength exercises

| Block | Exercise options | Reps and load |
|---|---|---|
| Lower body | Deadlifts, squats, lunges or step-ups | 2–4 reps; very difficult but still done with correct technique; very high intensity, very low repetitions |
| Upper body (preferred: pushing) | Bench press, overhead press or dip | Same 2–4 reps, loaded heavy |
| Upper body (not pulling hard or doing hard cruxes at the crag) | Weighted pull-up or heavy dumbbell row | Same 2–4 reps, loaded heavy |
| Core | Levers, dragon flags, inverted sit-ups, hanging straight-leg raises, or an inverted or inclined setup | Integrated, hard and really challenging for you |

- Pushing is preferred for the upper body because climbing training already gives so much pulling.
- Core: it is hard to get a core exercise into the 2–4 rep range, but if you can do 10, 12 or 15 reps and start to feel a burn in your abs, it is not an appropriate choice. Pick something integrative that uses many muscles and is complex for you.
- Progression: if you can do all sets at the given number of reps, add load next time. Strength depends on adaptation to heavy weights; if you don't keep adding weight or making the exercise more complex, you plateau.
- The mobility set and the finger strength set are described in the notes Dynamic Mobility Between Heavy Sets, and Short, Hard Finger Strength Sets Between Heavy Lifts.

## Principles

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Efficiency | Get the strength training done quickly and get back to climbing |
| Overload | Go to high intensity, because moving into redpointing and rock climbing automatically means longer days at lower intensity |
| Habit | Keep it going |

- Getting and staying strong depends on continuing to show up: even a bad program done continuously over a long time is better than a great program stopped after one week.
- The 3 × 3 format is not required; what you need is a way to stay flexible, stay strong and keep the fingers healthy even during a performance phase.
