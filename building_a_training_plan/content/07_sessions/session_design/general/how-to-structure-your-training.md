# How to Structure Your Training

**Type:** Planning, Principles · **Trains:** Finger strength, Power, Power endurance, Antagonists & core

**TL;DR:** When clustering several workouts in one session, keep them within the same stimulus, e.g. fingerboard hangs, then strength and power bouldering, then antagonists. Do workouts with different stimuli on separate days. Fingerboard inside a session: 3 or 4 sets before the wall; on a day of its own: 6 to 8 sets.

## Why structure matters
- How training is structured within the week and within each session matters: done right, you feel good and the session quality is as high as it can be; done wrong, the session quality can be compromised.

## Clustering stimuli
- When clustering several workouts in one session, keep them within the same stimulus.

| Order | Workout (example of a good cluster) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 3 to 4 hangs on the fingerboard |
| 2 | Strength and power bouldering session |
| 3 | Antagonist workout to finish |

- **Warning:** Avoid mixing stimuli in one session. Typical mistake (often made by sport climbers before a trip or a peak phase): a strength and power bouldering session followed by a very pumpy aerobic power session. More efficient: do those two workouts on separate days so both are of good quality.

## The fingerboard
- Whether the fingerboard belongs in every climber's training plan is debatable.
- If the fingerboard is in your plan:

| Option | Sets | Placement |
|---|---|---|
| Fit it into a session | Low: 3 or 4 | Do the hangs before the on-the-wall session |
| A day of its own | More: 6 to 8 | Fingerboard session alone |
