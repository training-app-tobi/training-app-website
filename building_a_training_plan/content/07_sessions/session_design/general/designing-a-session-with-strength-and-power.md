# Designing a Session With Strength and Power

**Type:** Session, Principles · **Trains:** General strength, Power, Finger strength

**TL;DR:** In a session that trains strength and power together, do the power work fresh: the most complex and heavy exercises come before a lot of volume (box jumps before heavy squats, campus moves before heavy hangboarding). Warm up progressively with power movements at the planned velocity, rest plenty between power sets even when they don't feel hard, judge by actual performance, and stop the session when performance drops.

## Designing a session with strength and power

- Train power fresh: do the most complex and heavy exercises before a lot of volume. Box jumps before heavy squat sets; campus moves before heavy hangboarding. A slight drop in strength loads (from fatigue after the power sets) supports greater power development. Not always the case: see complex training in the note General Strength Sessions: Movement Patterns x Strength Qualities.
- Warm-ups should include power movements similar in velocity to the planned training.
- Warm-ups should be progressive in load and duration: start with 1-2 reps of an exercise at 50% of working load, then slowly add both reps and weight.
- Rest plenty between all power exercises, even though the sets are very short and often don't feel hard. Judge by actual performance, not by how it feels: if performance tapers off, you are either not resting enough between sets or out of energy.
- **Warning:** don't go deep into fatigue: when performance drops, stop the session and plan to go hard again in a couple of days.
