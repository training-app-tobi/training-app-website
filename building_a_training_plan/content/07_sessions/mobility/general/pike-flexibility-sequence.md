# Pike Flexibility Sequence

**Type:** Session, Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Mobility

**TL;DR:** A pike is a forward fold, and strength through the range in the pike position helps heel hooking, toe hooking and driving through far-away footholds. Do this 4-part sequence 1-2 times a week, in order: awareness/control, facilitator stretches, tempo stretching for range of motion, then hip flexor strengthening. Lengthen the antagonists (hamstrings, calves) before strengthening the agonists (hip flexors), and track progress towards a specific goal over about 12 weeks.

## Why train the pike
- Climbers typically focus on upper body and finger strength and neglect lower body flexibility. Being able to move through a wider range of movement and keep strength throughout that range opens new movement possibilities and lets you try beta that was inaccessible before; lower body flexibility and end-range strength may be holding you back.
- A pike is a forward fold. Pike exercises mainly give a stimulus to the back, hip flexors, hamstrings and calves.
- Strength through the range in the pike position is useful for heel hooking, toe hooking, and reaching and driving through footholds that are far away.
- Example, heel hooking: when you lift a leg to place it on a hold, the hamstrings lengthen and the hip flexors and quads shorten. So climbers should both lengthen the hamstrings and strengthen the shortening side of the joint.

## How to use the sequence
- Use the sequence 1-2 times a week, in addition to any lower-intensity flexibility sessions.
- Do the exercises in the order shown, to get the most out of each component.
- The sequence has 4 sections, a holistic approach to lower body mobility and strength through the range:

| Section | Aim | Exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Awareness/Control | Move well, without overcompensating with other dominant muscles or compromising form | Seated Pike Core Compressions (single leg) or Single Leg Good Morning |
| 2. Facilitator | Set up the rest of the session by lengthening the antagonists | Calf Stretch, Hamstring Stretch |
| 3. Range of Motion / Antagonist | Improve range of motion on the antagonist side (hamstrings, calves) | Elevated Single Leg Good Morning (tempo stretch) |
| 4. Strengthening / Agonist | Increase strength through the full range of motion (hip flexors) | Supine Hip Flexor Lift, then Barre Lift Medial |

## Why it works: length before strength
- Strength training should be integral to flexibility work.
- When you train the strength of the agonists (lifting strength, e.g. hip flexors), range of motion is limited by the antagonists (e.g. hamstrings/calf muscles).
- If you first lengthen/stretch the antagonists, you can train lifting strength through a greater range of motion and so consolidate your range with greater mobility.
- So do the lengthening component of the session before the strengthening component, with awareness of your movement so you move with intention.

## 1. Awareness/Control
- To execute a pike/forward fold, first do a hip hinge without flexing the spine/back, and without tensing the abdominal muscles (this can feel counterintuitive and take practice).
- **Warning:** A pike/forward fold done incorrectly, without a hip hinge, can place unnecessary strain on the spine.
- Aim of this part: make sure you move well and don't overcompensate with other dominant muscles or compromise form. Knowing how to forward fold (hip hinge) correctly is needed for the exercises in this sequence to work.
- The objective of the Seated Pike Core Compressions (single leg) and the Single Leg Good Morning is to bring awareness to the hip flexors; nothing helps you feel a muscle working more than pump or fatigue.

| Option | Exercise |
|---|---|
| Stand-alone session | Seated Pike Core Compressions (single leg) |
| Short, simple starter at the beginning of the sequence | Single Leg Good Morning |

| Exercise | How | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| Seated Pike Core Compressions, single leg | Sit with legs out straight in front of you, hands on the floor either side of the legs, close to the body. Keeping both legs straight, lift one leg off the floor, locking the hips in place. Squeeze and hold, feeling the hip flexors. | 1 set of 10 reps per side |
| Single Leg Good Morning | Stand with one leg slightly in front of the other, soft bend in the back knee. Hands on the ASIS (anterior superior iliac spine, the bony bit on the front of the pelvis). Set the pelvis in a slight anterior (forward) tilt. Relax the abdominal muscles. Move into a forward hip hinge with the hands still on the ASIS, leading with the belly button and pushing the butt back. Keep the upper body still, back flat. | 1 set of 10 reps per side at bodyweight |

| Exercise | How to progress or make it easier |
|---|---|
| Seated Pike Core Compressions | Progress by reaching the hands further towards the feet each time you lift a leg |
| Single Leg Good Morning | If it's tricky, put a folded towel between your legs and squeeze it as you forward fold |

## 2. Facilitator
- This part sets up the rest of the session by lengthening the antagonists. Both exercises are done at bodyweight.
- Calf Stretch: many athletes find that calf tightness, rather than hamstring tightness, limits their range of motion. This stretch reduces that sense of tension and reduces compression of the sciatic nerve.

| Exercise | How | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| Calf Stretch | On a step, drop your heels and lower your weight one foot at a time. Relax and breathe deeply. | 3 reps of 30 seconds |
| Hamstring Stretch | Sit with one leg fully extended forward and one leg folded in. Lean forward and stretch one leg at a time. If you can't reach your foot, use a band, belt or similar to pull you deeper into the stretch. | 3 reps of 30 seconds |

- **Warning:** Calf Stretch and Hamstring Stretch: do NOT stretch so far that you feel pain, pinching or discomfort in the joint.
- To save time, superset the Calf Stretch and the Hamstring Stretch.

## 3. Range of Motion / Antagonist
- This part improves range of motion on the antagonist side. For the pike/front fold, the main antagonists are the hamstrings and the calf muscles.

| Exercise | How | Sets × reps | Working rep time | Rest between sets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Elevated Single Leg Good Morning (tempo stretch) | Stand on one leg with the other foot on the floor in front of you. Hinge forward at the hips, pushing the butt back, back flat throughout. | 3 × 4 | 8 seconds | 60 seconds |

| Tempo phase | Time |
|---|---|
| Lowering (eccentric) | 3 s |
| Pause at the bottom | 2 s |
| Lifting (concentric) | 2 s |
| Pause at the top | 1 s |

- Tempo stretching is dynamic stretching. You MUST move slowly to avoid the muscle reflex (tightening of the muscles); moving too fast limits its effectiveness.
- Move into the maximum tolerable limit on each rep, aiming to get lower each time.

| Progress by | How |
|---|---|
| Range | Reach a greater range of motion |
| Elevation | Elevate the front leg on a box/step |
| Load | Add weight |

- **Warning:** If adding load, the weight should only pull you a little deeper into the stretch. Don't overdo it: overloading the muscles can hold back range of motion development, because the muscles won't let you go deeper when they feel more vulnerable in that position.

## 4. Strengthening / Agonist
- The final part increases strength through the full range of motion by strengthening the agonists. For the pike/front fold, the agonists are the hip flexors.
- Intention for the strength exercises: an integral part of developing flexibility is being able to control and display your full range of motion using your own strength.

| Stage | Exercise | When |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Supine Hip Flexor Lift | New to flexibility work, or strength through the range is limited |
| Next | Barre Lift Medial | Comfortable with the leg up at 90 degrees in the Supine Hip Flexor Lift |

| Exercise | How | Sets × reps | Rest between sets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supine Hip Flexor Lift | Lie on your back, legs out straight, next to something to rest the working leg on (a door frame or squat rack). Lift one leg and rest it on the door frame/rack. Engage the hip flexor and lift the leg, straight, to its end range towards your face. | 2 × 6 | 30 seconds |
| Barre Lift Medial | Stand with one leg lifted straight in front of you, resting on a bar, box or step. Lift the leg, straight, to its end range. Change the bar height to make it harder or easier. | 2 × 6 | 30 seconds |

- Supine Hip Flexor Lift: to work end-range strength, start in a position where you feel a stretch with the leg resting.

## Goals and tracking progress
- Progress in all these exercises is easy to measure and track over time. Have a goal: it improves motivation and session satisfaction.
- Plan a sequence of sessions over time (12 weeks recommended) with a specific goal; it helps you stick with the sessions and rewards the work when progress is made. Goals depend on your starting flexibility.
- Example goal: touch the floor with your fingers in a standing forward fold.

| Step | Reach down and touch |
|---|---|
| 1 | A yoga block/object |
| 2 | The floor with your fingers |
| 3 | The floor with your knuckles |
| 4 | The floor with flat palms |

- Measuring progress towards the goal shows whether you are working at the right intensity and frequency. Check your progress at the end of each sequence, and keep a record of sessions in a diary or on your phone.
- If you hit a plateau, increase the intensity: elevate the leg higher, try to reach a greater range of motion, or add weight. Alternatively, switch focus to a different lower body position, such as the pancake.
