# Five Flexibility Stretches for the Warm-Up

**Type:** Session, Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Mobility · **For:** Beginner

**TL;DR:** The warm-up is the best place for flexibility work: the range of motion opened up right after stretching is most useful when you move to the wall, and climbing consolidates strength through it. Do butterfly (or frog) pose, a seated straddle with foot push, a hamstring stretch, a lunge with thoracic twist and a Cossack squat, and never move fast in a stretched position.

## Warm-up: flexibility
- Include stretching/flexibility work in the warm-up: it is the best place for it, because the range of motion opened up immediately after stretching is most useful when you move to the wall. You move better, and you consolidate strength through that range by climbing with it. Makes a big difference over many years of climbing.

| Stretch | How |
|---|---|
| 1. Butterfly pose (can be swapped for frog pose; butterfly is a bit easier to get into and nicer on the knees) | Back against a wall or mat and kept flat; pull the feet in toward the groin (just getting them there is good enough at first), then after a while bring the knees down. Light weights on the legs are fine for assistance (it is a strong position) and won't be too much load before climbing as long as they are light. Hold about 1 min; 2 sets; if pushed for time, just keep it as a habit |
| 2. Seated straddle with foot push | Legs out wide; push each foot outward so the legs slide wider and wider; stretches the inner thigh (adductors). Prop yourself up with the hands if you feel you will fall back. Stay a few minutes, or as long as you want until you have maxed out the width; if pushed for time, just do some |
| 3. Hamstring stretch | From the straddle, bring one leg in, face the other leg, grab the end of the foot and pull in; if you can't reach, wrap a band around the foot. Relax into it. Hamstring range helps heel-hook positions |
| 4. Lunge with thoracic twist | One leg extended back, the other forward: hip extension and hip flexion at the same time, as in a high step. Bring the elbow toward the floor to increase hip flexion (you may not reach it; go as deep as you can), then come up and twist the torso toward the front leg, arm reaching to the sky (thoracic mobility through the spine and rib cage). At least 6–8 reps, alternating sides, slowly |
| 5. Cossack squat | More active; works ankle flexibility, adductor flexibility and hip flexion and warms the muscles around the hip joint. Go only as deep as you can |

- **Warning:** Never move fast in a stretched position.
