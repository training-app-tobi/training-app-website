# General Strength Sessions: Movement Patterns x Strength Qualities

**Type:** Session, Principles · **Trains:** General strength, Pulling strength, Power, Antagonists & core

**TL;DR:** A short, difficult general strength session is 4 sets of two circuits (pull-ups, squats, hanging straight leg raises; presses, deadlifts, weighted sit-ups) at 3–10 reps, loaded so the reps are just barely achievable, in the 70–85% of max range, never missing a rep. To build sessions year-round, pick one exercise from each strength quality (unilateral, bilateral, explosive, isometric, integrative) across the five movement patterns, and switch exercises and loads after 6–8 sessions.

## Example general strength session (short, difficult)

4 sets of each circuit, rest 1-2 minutes between exercises.

| Circuit | Exercise | Reps |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pull-ups | 3 |
| 1 | Squats | 3 |
| 1 | Hanging straight leg raises | 5-10 |
| 2 | Presses | 5 |
| 2 | Deadlifts | 5 |
| 2 | Weighted sit-ups | 5-10 |

- A full-value session; a powerlifter might find the volume too high and the movements too many.

## Effort and load

- Increase the load until the assigned reps are just barely achievable. Example of getting it wrong: 3 bodyweight pull-ups for someone whose max is about 15, bodyweight squats, presses with a pair of 7 kg dumbbells and deadlifts at 29.5 kg: not even enough for a good warm-up, no benefit, and probably not the intended energy system.
- If the intensity is right, overloading the muscles in just a few reps or a few seconds, the body is stimulated to handle those loads next time. That is why the sets are so short and only 3-5 sets are done: you are out of energy for that load for that day.
- Loads close to max don't necessarily make you stronger faster. Train in the 70-85% of max range: strength gains are still high and injury risk drops sharply. Make it look easy (technically correct) and never miss a rep.

## General strength: movement patterns x strength qualities

- Break the needs (explosiveness, concentric strength, isometric strength, core stability, single-side strength) into movement patterns and strength qualities to build simple training cycles that can run year-round.

| Movement pattern | Unilateral | Bilateral | Explosive | Isometric | Integrative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hip hinge | 1-leg deadlift | Romanian deadlift | Kettlebell swing | Mid-thigh pull | Clean |
| Squat | Step-up | Rack squat | Jump squat | Iso squat hold | Long push press |
| Upper body press | 1-arm push-up | Barbell or military press | Clap push-up | Wedge/T-push-up | - |
| Upper body pull | Dumbbell row | Pull-up | Jumping pull-up | 1-arm iso hold on pull-up bar | Kettlebell snatch |
| Midsection | Plank/pull | Dip bar straight leg raise | Partner ball pass | Hardstyle plank | Turkish get-up |

- Complex training: doing some jumping after a heavy leg set makes sense, and the same can be done across the full range of patterns and qualities.
- To build a session, pick one exercise from each category. This covers several movement speeds and contraction types and all 5 major movement patterns. Example:

| Exercise | Sets x reps |
|---|---|
| 1-leg deadlift | 3x3 |
| Rack squat | 3x5 |
| T-push-up | 3x5+5 |
| 1-arm iso hold on pull-up bar | 3x1+1 |
| Partner ball pass | 3x10 |

- After 6-8 sessions, switch to different exercises and loads and keep going.
- Don't overcomplicate it: the training supports climbing; you are not competing in weightlifting.

## Terms

- **Bilateral:** a strength exercise using two limbs at once, e.g. squat, pull-up, 2-hand hang on an edge.
- **Unilateral:** single arm or leg training, e.g. lunges, 1-arm rows, single-arm hangs. Increases core involvement, improves balance and increases strength through the bilateral strength deficit phenomenon.
- **Explosive:** powerful movements, often a sped-up version of normal strength moves, e.g. box jump, kettlebell swing. Rule of thumb: if you can do it slowly, it isn't power.
- **Isometric:** static holding of a position, e.g. holding a 90-degree squat or a 1-arm lock-off.
- **Integrative:** movement involving two or more patterns at once; if you can't easily say "this is an upper body pull", it is probably integrative. Examples: front levers, Turkish get-ups, barbell cleans, climbing.
