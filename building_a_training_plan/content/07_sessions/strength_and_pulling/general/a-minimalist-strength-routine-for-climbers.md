# A Minimalist Strength Routine for Climbers

**Type:** Session, Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Finger strength, Pulling strength, General strength, Antagonists & core, Mobility · **For:** Beginner (new to strength training)

**TL;DR:** Off-the-wall strength training does not need many fancy exercises: pick a functional routine that is easy to perform consistently and progress the difficulty over time. Before climbing, do recruitment pulls for the fingers, with shoulder exercises in the rests. After climbing, do two supersets: pullups + dips (the priority), then optionally rows + bench press, each exercise 5–8 reps at 1–2 reps in reserve, 3 rounds with 3–5 minutes of rest. Do the routine once a week if brand new to training, twice a week with some athletic background, eventually up to three times a week.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Brand new to training | Do the routine once a week |
| Some athletic background | Do the routine twice a week |
| Have the time and recovery capacity | Eventually increase to three times a week, but only without compromising your climbing sessions |

## Principle
- Off-the-wall strength training does not need many fancy exercises. What makes you stronger is a functional routine that is easy for you to perform consistently, with the difficulty progressed over time.

## Session order
| Order | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Before climbing | Recruitment pulls (finger training), with shoulder exercises in their rests |
| 2 | Climbing session | Climb |
| 3 | After climbing | Superset one: pullups + dips |
| 4 | After climbing, optional | Superset two: rows + bench press |

- The before-climbing work is described in the notes Recruitment Pulls Before Climbing, and Shoulder Exercises Between Recruitment Pulls: No Moneys and Stick Dislocates.

## Climbing session
- Arrive at every session adequately recovered, try things at your limit, rest appropriately between attempts, and stop climbing when you feel a notable drop in performance.

## After climbing: supersets
- After climbing, the rest of the strength training is done as supersets (two exercises back to back without resting) to save time. Two supersets: one primary, one optional.
- **Warning:** The goal of this training is to supplement climbing, not take away from it. If you arrive at each session fatigued, you're either going way too hard or your recovery is compromised by some other factor.

| Every exercise in the routine | Dose |
|---|---|
| Reps | 5–8 |
| Intensity | 1–2 reps in reserve (RIR): 2 RIR means two reps left in the tank at the end of the set |
| Too easy | You could have done several more reps |
| Too intense | You barely finish the final rep |

## Superset one: vertical pull-push (pullups + dips)
- Superset one is the priority: if you only have time or energy for one superset, do this one.

### Pullups
- Pullup-like motion is the most common movement in climbing, so proficiency is a major benefit for climbers.
- Form: hands slightly wider than shoulder width; lead with a proud chest, as if trying to touch the chest to the bar. This feels harder than a hollow-body or rounded-shoulder pullup but is the preferred method for climbers.

| Pullup progression | Do this |
|---|---|
| Can't yet do a bodyweight pullup | Use resistance bands, cable machines, or the lowering portion only (negatives / eccentric pullups). All can work; the best option is a pulley system, because unlike a band it gives exactly the same assistance through the full range of motion |
| Can already do multiple pullups | Same guidelines, but add weight to stay in the 5–8 rep range; otherwise you end up doing sets of 12 or more, which is not the goal. Add a little weight every week or two |
| About 50% of bodyweight added | Switch to assisted one-arm pullups |

### Dips
- Dips train the triceps, pecs and anterior deltoids; they go a long way to improving mantle ability, which is imperative if you plan to climb outdoors. Dips on rings also increase rotator cuff activity, which increases shoulder stability for dynamic moves.

| Dips progression | Do this |
|---|---|
| Never done dips | Start on a solid bar |
| Can't do bodyweight reps | Assist with a resistance band or cable machine |
| As soon as you're able | Switch to rings |

- Form: in position with the arms straight, let the chest come slightly forward so it is in front of the hands. The chest keeps moving forward as you lower by bending the elbows. Keep the elbows tucked in toward the body, not flared to the side. Back at the top, the chest is still slightly forward, not perfectly vertical.
- **Warning:** Shoulder discomfort during dips: decrease the depth and/or increase the assistance.

| Superset one | Dose |
|---|---|
| Rounds | 3 in total |
| Rest between rounds | 3–5 minutes |
| During the rests | Your favourite stretch, or the frog stretch for 30–45 seconds for hip mobility |

## Superset two (optional): horizontal pull-push (rows + bench press)
- Horizontal pulling helps you pull into the wall, especially on overhanging climbs, and teaches better scapular retraction. Horizontal pushing further strengthens muscles useful for compression moves and improves general upper-body strength and robustness.

### Rows
- Choose any row type that works for you (e.g. inverted rows on rings, dumbbell rows); here, inverted rows. Proper row technique strengthens muscles used constantly in climbing, and learning proper scapular retraction can improve movement patterns and success with other exercises later.
- Inverted row form: grab the rings, position the body at an angle, and pull the body toward the hands. Lead with a proud chest and feel the squeeze in the back muscles at the end of the motion, rather than hunching over and pulling with just the biceps. To make it harder, increase the lean angle.

### Bench press
- Why the bench press for horizontal pushing: accessible in most gyms, approachable even for beginners, much more intuitive to progress than pushups, and easier to overload safely than a dumbbell press. Pecs and triceps are used far more than you might think in many climbing moves.
- Form: start with the bar directly over your eyeline and tuck the shoulders back as if puffing out the chest. Don't lower the bar straight down: keep the elbows gently tucked toward the body, not flared, so the bar lands in the middle of the chest, near the bottom of the sternum rather than the top of the chest. Push the bar back up over the eyeline.
- Foot and lower-back position are personal preference; a mild arch in the back is fine or even beneficial. Squeeze the shoulder blades together as you lower the bar.
- No barbell: use dumbbells. At the top the weights are parallel, thumbs pointing toward each other; as you lower, allow some natural rotation so the elbows tuck in toward the sides, which puts the shoulders in a better position to press back up.

| Superset two | Dose |
|---|---|
| Rounds | 3 in total |
| Rest between rounds | 3–5 minutes |
| During the rests | Your favourite stretch, or the standing or seated pancake, reaching centre, left and right, for 30–60 seconds total |
