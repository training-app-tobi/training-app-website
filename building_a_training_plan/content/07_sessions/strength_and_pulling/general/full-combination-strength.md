# Full Combination Strength

**Type:** Session, Planning · **Trains:** General strength, Power, Finger strength, Antagonists & core

**TL;DR:** Most weight training uses a single tempo and mostly bilateral exercises, which misses many movements and speeds of contraction useful on the rock. A Full Combination session trains all five movement patterns in different strength variations (bilateral, unilateral, isometric, explosive, concentric-only, unstable). Do 2–3 sets of 4–6 reps per exercise (isometrics 5–10 seconds), keep the intensity high and rest enough to be fresh for each set; the session takes about 30–40 minutes and pairs with hangboard or climbing work.

## Who should do what

| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Crowded gym or limited equipment | Use the bi-set / tri-set format instead of one large circuit |
| Hard to move away from standing under the hangboard for a whole session | Combine Full Combination exercises with hangs in simple bi-sets |
| Athlete who has already mastered several variations of each exercise | Rotating variations every week (4-week example below) trains breadth; there is no need to change exercises that often |
| Haven't mastered an exercise in each category | The smart move is not to programme those variations |

## Why combine types of strength

- Most weight training addresses only one speed of movement, and most exercises chosen are bilateral. Training in such a narrow path misses many movements and speeds of contraction that are useful to climbers.
- Most people unconsciously train a single tempo in every exercise (squats, lunges, presses, pull-ups), and the muscles learn to work according to those rules. On the rock things change: you have to stand in a crouched position for 30 seconds, lunge for a jug, or lock off to place the next finger lock. Full Combination sessions bridge strength from heavy "normal" exercises to doing the exercises in ways more like those met on the rock.
- The standard Full Combination workout trains five different facets of strength in five different movement patterns. You can pick any exercise you like, but most people benefit most from training all five patterns in every training session.

## Movement patterns

| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| Upper body press | Push-ups, presses, bench press, dips |
| Upper body pull | Rows, pull-ups, cleans and the like |
| Hip hinge | Deadlifts, swings, levers |
| Squat | Squats, lunges, step-ups, sled work and most other "leg exercises" |
| Core | Planks, leg raises, stability work |

- Normal tempo training means a 1–2 second lowering phase, no pause at the bottom, a 1–2 second concentric phase and little or no pause at the top. It is a good way to train but not the only way: in sport, look not only at which muscles and movements are used but at the way they are used.

## Exercise variations

| Variation | What it is | Examples | Why / notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bilateral | Both arms or both legs at once, in the same pattern, with a barbell or a pair of weights | Pull-ups, squats, deadlifts, bench press | This is where most people spend the most time. Essential to most programmes, because a heavy bilateral movement is where you can put out the most force |
| Unilateral | One limb at a time or different patterns | Step-ups, single-arm presses, dumbbell rows, lunges, single-leg raises | Excellent for balance, for creating stability and for recognising left/right strength imbalances |
| Isometric | Holding a static position, normally not at the beginning of the range of motion. Almost anything you could do concentrically, held against an immovable object | Iso-hold pull-ups, presses, squats, planks | You can hold at any of several joint angles; strength gains tend to be greatest at the angle trained |
| Explosive | A power version of a strength exercise | Jumps, explosive push-ups, doubles on the campus board, medicine ball work | See below: train some speed/explosiveness at all times |
| Concentric-only | In climbing you often move under load and then reach up with an unloaded limb, unlike a typical pull-up where the arms are under load for the whole movement | Deadlift with drop | There is good research supporting concentric-only training for strength gain without hypertrophy, and for specific sport adaptations |
| Unstable | A destabilised version of an exercise, using an uneven or unstable surface or a variable-resistance tool such as a sandbag | Core work on a stability ball or medicine ball, elastic band work, offset deadlifts or squats, lunges on an Airex pad | Usually done at lighter loads than the stable exercises |

- Power work is often separated into whole different training phases. This is OK, but you should train some speed/explosiveness at all times.

## How to build a session

- The standard session is built on one of each exercise variation and one of each movement pattern; the programming can be creative.
- **Warning:** this is still strength training: keep the intensity high and rest enough between sets to be fresh for the next set.

| Element | Dose |
|---|---|
| Sets per exercise | 2–3 |
| Reps per set | 4–6 |
| Isometrics | Hold 5–10 seconds |
| Session length | About 30–40 minutes |

- Combine the session with a hangboard or climbing session. A Full Combination workout takes about 30–40 minutes and can be followed by finger strength work, or a hangboard session can be integrated into it if the facility allows.
- Format: one big circuit of five exercises, a couple of groups, or single sets combined with mobility or finger strength. As long as you keep pushing strength, the format doesn't really matter.

## Example sessions

### Large circuit (3 rounds)

| Exercise | Reps / time | Pattern / variation |
|---|---|---|
| Front squat | ×5 | Squat / bilateral |
| Dumbbell row | ×5+5 | Upper body pull / unilateral |
| Double kettlebell swing | ×5 | Hip hinge / explosive |
| Bench press iso hold | 8 s | Upper body press / isometric |
| Sandbag get-up | ×1+1 | Core / unstable |

### Bi-set / tri-set format

More effective in crowded gyms or with limited equipment.

| Group | Rounds | Exercise | Reps / time | Pattern / variation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 rounds | Step-up | ×5+5 | Squat / unilateral |
| 1 | 3 rounds | Iso-hold pull-up | 5 s | Upper body pull / isometric |
| 2 | 3 sets | Deadlift with drop | ×5 | Hip hinge / bilateral / concentric-only |
| 2 | 3 sets | Power push-up | ×5 | Upper body press / explosive |
| 2 | 3 sets | TRX knee tuck / body saw | ×5+5 | Core / unstable |

### Combined with hangboard training (3 rounds of each group)

Simple bi-sets, useful for people who find it hard to move away from standing under the hangboard for a whole session.

| Group | Exercise | Reps / time | Pattern / variation | Hang |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | TRX 1-arm inverted row | ×5+5 | Upper body pull / unilateral | Edge hang 8 s |
| 2 | Rack squat | ×5 | Squat / bilateral | Pocket hang 8 s |
| 3 | Single-arm kettlebell swing | ×5+5 | Hip hinge / unilateral + explosive | Pinch hang 8 s |
| 4 | Hardstyle plank | 10 s | Core / isometric | Edge hang 8 s |
| 5 | Iso overhead press | 5 s | Upper body press / isometric | Pocket hang 8 s |

## Rotating variations over 4 weeks

| Pattern | Week 1 | Week 2 | Week 3 | Week 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upper body press | Unilateral | Concentric-only | Bilateral | Unstable |
| Upper body pull | Explosive | Explosive | Unstable | Isometric |
| Squat | Bilateral | Bilateral | Isometric | Unilateral |
| Hinge | Unstable | Unilateral | Concentric-only | Explosive |
| Core | Isometric | Isometric | Explosive | Concentric-only |

- There is no need to change exercises as often as in this example; it trains breadth for an athlete who has already mastered several variations of each exercise.
- If you haven't mastered an exercise in each category, the smart move is not to programme those variations.
