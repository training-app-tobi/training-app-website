# Adaptive Hangboard Session: Half Crimp Build-Up and Open-Grip Hangs

**Type:** Session, Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Finger strength

**TL;DR:** 8 two-arm, 10-second hangs in a four-finger half crimp: 6 slow build-up sets and only 2 working sets at about 8 out of 10 effort, then 3 easy 20–30-second hangs in a front-three drag. Loads are not planned in advance: set them during the session by how each set feels.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Climbing frequently | This session once or twice a week |
| Climbing less than 3 times a week | This session far more often |

## The session
| Sets | Do this |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | 10-second hang, feet on the ground, about 3 out of 10 effort; about 1 minute rest between them |
| 3–4 | 10-second bodyweight hang, feet off the ground; 2 minutes rest before set 4 |
| 5–6 | 10-second hang with an additional 5 kg, a small incremental step |
| 7–8 (working sets) | 10-second hang at the target load, about 8 out of 10 effort; 3 minutes rest between them |
| Open-grip hangs | 3 hangs of 20–30 seconds in a front-three drag, feet on the ground, pretty low intensity, about 2 minutes rest between each |

- Climbing around on the fingerboard is a good way to get the fingers ready before the hangs.
- Creaky or cold fingers in the early sets are fine: the low intensity keeps it safe.
- Bodyweight hang too difficult at sets 3–4: keep the feet on the ground and increase the intensity more slowly. With lifts or one-arm hangs, make each set an incremental step of difficulty.
- Aim for a challenging load for the day, not your maximum; a recent max test is not needed. Want more warm-up: do more hangs before the heavier load.
- Alternative: a more linear build-up, changing the load every set; two sets per load is a preference.

## Adapting the load
| Situation | Do this |
|---|---|
| Repeated set at the same load feels easier | Progress the weight for the next set |
| Repeated set doesn't feel easier | Rethink the rest of the session; increase the load a little, but less than on a good day |
| Repeated set felt really easy | Ramp up the load quite a bit more |
| Not feeling it today | Keep the same load for set 6, then increase it for the last two sets depending on how you feel |
| Working set feels like 9 out of 10, or gets shaky | Feet down and end the set, keep the same 3-minute rest, then reduce the weight slightly to get a good-quality, full 10-second hang |

- Example: after two +5 kg sets that didn't feel easier, another 10 kg added, a load known to be very manageable, and no higher that day; on a shaky working set, back down to about 10 kg added.
- Perceived effort spikes on the first set with a new load, then drops back a little as the fingers recruit; this zigzag grows closer to your maximum load. Example: target 8 out of 10, first +5 kg set felt about 5–6 out of 10.
- Don't stick with a planned structure when you don't feel right: be flexible and adapt.
- **Warning:** Don't test your maximum every session, at any level (V5 to V15). Injury in the session is relatively unlikely, but maximal loads add up week on week on top of your climbing, eat into recovery and usually lead to overtraining and injury further down the line, even if you feel you're progressing.

## Open-grip hangs
- After the half crimp work, a couple of overcoming isometrics in a more open grip keep the fingers healthy there and stop you becoming detrained (de-recruited) in the open position; they also stretch the hands out a little.
- Either order works, but the positive grip first at higher loads, then the open grip at a lighter load, is really beneficial for climbing and finger health throughout the training season.

## Frequency
- Up to 2, 3 or 4 times a week, depending on experience and how much you climb; adapting the loads to how you feel each session is what makes this possible.
