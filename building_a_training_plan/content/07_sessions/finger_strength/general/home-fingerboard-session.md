# Home Fingerboard Session: Max Hangs, Offset Pulls and Repeaters

**Type:** Session, Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Finger strength, Pulling strength, Power endurance · **For:** limited gym access

**TL;DR:** A home fingerboard session in three parts: max hangs (usually 5 to 15 seconds, few reps), climbing-specific pulling (offset hangs and pull-ups, or moving around the board with the feet on the floor or a chair carrying part of your weight), then a quarter to half session of anaerobic or slightly aerobic repeaters (e.g. 7 s on / 3 s off or 10 s on / 5 s off) for forearm work capacity. Build up over time: all three back-to-back in full might be too much.

## Home fingerboard session
- Built for a boulderer with limited gym access who trains at home with a fingerboard and rings, to train the forearms for the anaerobic stress of bouldering and increase work capacity.

| Order | Work | Details |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Max hangs | Usually 5 to 15 seconds depending on the protocol; you don't need many reps because they are so intense |
| 2 | Climbing-specific pulling exercises | Offset hangs, offset pull-ups, assisted movement around the board (see below) |
| 3 | Energy-systems work: anaerobic or slightly aerobic repeaters | E.g. 7 s on / 3 s off or 10 s on / 5 s off, for set numbers of sets and reps, to increase the forearms' work capacity; a quarter to half session |

- Climbing-specific pulling on the fingerboard (convenient at home): many modern fingerboards have several tiers of holds (e.g. four). Do offset hangs and offset pull-ups on the bigger holds, or put your feet on the floor, a chair or an object that keeps your body under the board, so 60%, 70% or 80% of your weight is on the fingerboard, then move around the board recreating climbing movements. Builds work capacity while working the upper-body muscles in a specific way. Good for anyone without regular access to a wall (e.g. students).
- Keep the energy-systems work to a quarter to half session so the volume on the fingerboard isn't too high; build up over time. Doing all three back-to-back in full might be too much.
