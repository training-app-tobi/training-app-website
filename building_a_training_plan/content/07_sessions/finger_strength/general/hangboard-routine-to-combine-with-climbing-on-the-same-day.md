# Hangboard Routine to Combine With Climbing on the Same Day

**Type:** Session, Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Finger strength, Mobility, Antagonists & core · **For:** outdoor-only climbers with a hangboard, climbers who climb only a couple of days a week, newer to hangboarding

**TL;DR:** A hangboard supplement to climbing, not a replacement: a shoulder and finger warm-up, then four rounds of hangs and pulls, with the rests filled by hip and lower-body mobility work. Round 4 is either Minimal Edge (no added weight, smallest edge you can hold) or MaxHang (added weight), never both. Every hang has a target time with an effort-level window; start easy, aim for the top of the window and change edge or weight only after a full session. Ideally do it in the morning and climb later that day; if you climb right after, rest 15 minutes first.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Climb outside only, no gym, but have a hangboard | Use this routine to build strength for your climbing |
| Want to add finger-strength training to your routine | Use this routine |
| Can only climb a couple of days a week | Use this routine to optimise those training days |
| Experienced climber | Only experienced climbers should hangboard and climb on the same day: you need to be in tune with your body to know when to push and when to pull back |

## What the routine is
- You can keep hangboard training and getting strong while you climb. This routine is a hangboard supplement to climbing (not a replacement for a climbing session): it has no "burnout round", and the rests between hangs are filled with climber-specific mobility exercises and stretches.
- Timing: do it before climbing that day (with 15 minutes of rest before climbing), or, ideally, in the morning with climbing later that day (afternoon or evening). Use it as a training day for climbing, not as your max-effort day.
- The exercises place little demand on the cardiovascular system, so you can recover fully between sets and keep energy for climbing later.
- Equipment: some rounds need added weight, attached via a harness, backpack or vest.
- **Warning:** Round 4 is either the Minimal Edge protocol or the MaxHang protocol. Do not do both; pick the one that suits you.

| Choosing Round 4 | Protocol |
|---|---|
| Newer to hangboarding | Start with the Minimal Edge protocol and work up to the MaxHang protocol |
| Experienced climber who hasn't hangboarded recently, or at least 6–12 months of climbing and want to start working on strength | Minimal Edge protocol (best as an introduction to the hangboard) |
| Don't know which edge size to train on | Minimal Edge protocol |
| Experienced with hangboarding, project involves crimping on a small edge | Minimal Edge protocol, to build strength on that crimp size |

- The best hangboard routine lets you load your tissue safely in a way that improves your climbing without injury. If you get hurt you can't climb or train: start light and work up.

## Edge sizes
Edge sizes are relative to your strength. A 6 mm edge can be a jug for a very strong climber while another climber still struggles on 10 mm.

| Edge | What it takes |
|---|---|
| Large | You can hang on it with little warm-up and minimal effort |
| Medium | Needs a warm-up and moderate effort |
| Small | Needs a full warm-up and high effort |

- Edge sizes given in the routine are suggestions: modify them to fit your abilities. Always start easy and work up.

## Rests as active rest
- The rests between hangs are used for exercises that benefit climbing: hip and lower-body mobility for functional climbing movement, high feet, big reaches, better heel hooks and keeping the hips closer to the wall. This is true active rest: forearms and fingers recover while you do something useful.
- The rest exercises, including Nordic hamstring curls (padding under the knees is mandatory) and the mandatory Round 4 stretches, are described in the note Hip and Lower-Body Mobility for Hangboard Rests.

## No pump needed
- Hangboarding for strength will not give you the pump you feel after a lot of climbing, and that is fine: stop chasing the pump. This is a strength programme; muscle burn is not needed for it to work, because burn comes from training a different energy system.

## Warm-up
| Order | Exercise | How | Dose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foam rolling | Mobilises tissue and improves thoracic mobility. Reaching fully overhead needs thoracic extension, so a few quick rolls prepare the body for hanging and may help shoulder issues such as impingement. Roll the rhomboids, infraspinatus, teres minor and lats for circulation and muscle mobility | A few quick rolls |
| 2 | Tendon glides | Touching all 3 positions = 1 glide; both hands at the same time. Can also be done before you arrive (e.g. one-handed in the car or walking into the gym); the aim is to get the tendons moving and the hands a little warm | 10 glides |
| 3 | Bilateral external rotation with scapular retraction ("no moneys") | Warms up the infraspinatus, teres minor and rhomboids. Squeeze the shoulder blades together as you rotate out; palms up or thumbs up. Resistance band | 12–15 reps, 1–2 sets |
| 4 | Horizontal abduction ("human T") | Warms up the middle trapezius and back of the shoulder. Keep the band parallel to the ground for a strong T; don't let the hands drop into a "droopy T". Squeeze the shoulder blades together | 12–15 reps, 1–2 sets |
| 5 | D2 flexion ("sword from the sheath") | Works the lower trapezius, important for the hangs, and moves toward the hanging position so the body gets used to being engaged while hanging. Thumb points backwards at the end of the movement | 12–15 reps, 1–2 sets |
| 6 | Tendon glides, second round | Only if the hands don't feel warm yet | 10 glides |
| 7 | Hangboard pull-ups or scapular pull-ups | On a large hold (one you can hold close to the first joint, the PIP joint, of your middle finger). Scapular pull-up: engage the shoulders, stick the chest out and squeeze the shoulder blades together | 10 reps; if you can't do 10, as many as are comfortable |

## Effort level
- Effort level uses hold time to set the force on the body: lower force means lower effort and a longer possible hold, and vice versa. Each hang has a target time plus/minus a window.

| Example: 12-second hang, effort level ±3 seconds | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hang lasts 9–15 seconds | Inside the window |
| Fail at 8 seconds | The edge is too small (or too much weight on max hangs) |
| Hang 16 seconds or more without failing | The edge is too large (or not enough weight) |

- This is how you track progress.
- Example: 4 sets of 15+ seconds with +25% bodyweight on a 20 mm edge with no issue is inside the window, but you are bordering on too easy and may be ready for a smaller edge or more weight next time.
- Best practice: start on a large enough hold or with minimal added weight so you reach the top of the window (e.g. 3 sets of 15 seconds) and work down from there, rather than starting by failing at 6 seconds and then adjusting.

## The rounds
| Round | Edge and grip | Hang / pull | Effort level | Rest (timer start) | Sets | Rest exercise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1: submax hangs, no weight | Medium to large edge, 20 mm+ | 30-second hang | ±5 seconds | About 1 minute (60-second timer starts right after the hang) | 2 | Side split against a wall with lateral trunk flexion |
| 2: single-arm recruitment pulls | Small edge, 10–15 mm, half crimp | 5-second pulls, 5 reps each hand | Build to max over 5 seconds | 90 seconds (timer starts after the second hand's reps) | 3 | High-feet drills |
| 3: submax hangs with weight | Medium edge, 21–30 mm, open hand | 20-second hang, with weight | ±5 seconds | 90 seconds (timer starts at the end of the hang) | 3 | Nordic hamstring curls against a wall |
| 4a: Minimal Edge | Half crimp on the smallest edge that fits the window, no added weight | 10-second hang | ±3 seconds | 3 minutes (timer starts right after the hang) | 3–4 | Round 4 stretches |
| 4b: MaxHang | Weighted | 10-second hang | ±3 seconds | 3 minutes (timer starts right after the hang) | 4 | Round 4 stretches |

### Round 1: submax hangs, no weight
- Purpose: get the shoulders used to hanging safely, make small corrections, and raise body awareness before adding stress. The longer hold gives time to check: are your shoulders active, how do your fingers feel.
- Rest is kept short because this is a warm-up and not yet strenuous. After 2 sets, go to Round 2.

### Round 2: single-arm recruitment pulls
- Single-arm recruitment pulls (not hanging): short, near-max-effort pulls, part warm-up and part training. The force you can safely produce increases each round or even each rep as the tissue warms up and neuromuscular recruitment improves.
- Hold: small enough that the hand is in a half crimp and you cannot lift yourself off the ground; 10–15 mm. Start with a slight bend in the elbow, not fully extended. The aim is to load the tissue safely in preparation for Round 4, not to overload it with full bodyweight on one arm on a small hold: think of pulling the hangboard down.
- **Warning:** If you are strong enough to lift yourself off the ground, don't: the tissue may not be fully warmed up yet.
- Each rep: 5-second pull, building slowly toward max effort over the 5 seconds, each second a higher "gear" of effort/force. This lets you listen to your body and stop if you aren't ready for the next gear. In the first set you may only reach 3rd gear; by the 3rd set you are safely pulling hard in 5th.

### Round 3: submax hangs with weight
- Back to an open-hand position, as in Round 1 but with added weight. Purpose: get the shoulders safe for hanging if you do MaxHangs in Round 4; further warm up the two main finger flexors (flexor digitorum profundus and superficialis); and give the pulleys a short break after the Round 2 crimp pulls, before Round 4 goes back to a crimp.

### Round 4a: Minimal Edge
- Push your strength on smaller edges without added weight.
- Start with a medium edge you can half crimp. The goal is to hang on the smallest edge you can hold for 12 seconds (±3 seconds).
- Don't overestimate your strength: start on a hold large enough to complete the time or slightly exceed it (15 seconds is fine). Don't move to a smaller edge straight away: finish all sets, and if you reach 15 seconds every time, you may need a smaller edge. Write it down for next time.
- You need the full 3-minute rest: the energy system used takes longer to recover, and this is a high-effort round, so the longer rest keeps it safe.
- After 3–4 sets you're done: rest 15 minutes before climbing, or carry on with your day.

### Round 4b: MaxHang
- MaxHangs are like your limit boulder or project: be careful and respect your experience and skill level. Done with added weight, 10-second hang ±3 seconds; the hang time can be varied according to skill and experience level.
- Why add weight: the hangs are two-handed, so force is shared equally between both hands. On the hardest climbs you often put a lot of stress on one hand; weighted two-hand hangs simulate the effect of a hard one-hand project hold while being safer and equal on both sides.
- MaxHangs also create physiological change in connective tissue: they promote its growth and help organise the cellular matrix so it can handle the stress of hard problems.
- Not only for boulderers: the stronger you are, the smaller the holds you can rest on, so you can move through hard sections and find rests where others can't.
- Aim for the top of the window. If you fail at 15 seconds in the first set, that's fine; don't move to a smaller edge or add weight straight away. Do all 4 sets, and if you reach 15 seconds every time, add weight next time. What may happen instead: the first 2 sets at 15 seconds, then failure at 10 and 7 seconds on the last sets, which shows why not to change things too quickly. Write it down for next time.
- You need the full 3-minute rest: the energy system used takes longer to recover, and this is a high-effort round, so the longer rest keeps it safe.
- After 4 sets you're done. If you are experienced you can do more sets, but no more than 6 sets in total.
- If you can add more than 50% bodyweight, reduce the weight and use a smaller edge.

## Climbing after the routine
- Wait 15 minutes before trying anything hard. You can climb some V0s, but nothing beyond V1, so you still get some rest.
- Doing the routine in the morning and climbing in the afternoon or evening is a great option.
- Why hangboard early and climb later the same day: part recovery, part total tissue load. Each energy system has time to recover, the training schedule is optimised, and the body responds to the stress by reinforcing the tissue even more.
- Accept that the climbing session after this routine may not be your limit-problem day, because you have already trained once.
