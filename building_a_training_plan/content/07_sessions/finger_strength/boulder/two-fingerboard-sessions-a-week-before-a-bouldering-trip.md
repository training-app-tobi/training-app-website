# Two Fingerboard Sessions a Week Before a Bouldering Trip

**Type:** Session, Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Finger strength · **For:** preparing a bouldering trip

**TL;DR:** Two fingerboard sessions a week through a 12-week trip plan. One keeps building intensity on the larger edge: four-finger half crimp with hangs going from 10 to 5 seconds as the load rises, then three-finger open hangs and repeaters. The other works the weakness: front-three and back-three at low intensity, then full-crimp dead hangs at really low intensity and loaded half-crimp hangs on a 10 mm edge. More than two sessions a week might overload the fingers or create monotony.

## Fingerboarding
- From a 12-week plan for a V9 boulderer's summer bouldering trip, whose testing showed small-edge strength as the area to improve: the climbing sessions are planned first, then the fingerboard sessions, the weights-gym session and specific conditioning.
- Fingerboarding goes in next, as finger strength is a priority. Two fingerboard sessions per week through the plan, based on what the climber already does and what fits the week.

| Session | When | Content |
|---|---|---|
| A, part 1 | Whole plan | Four-finger half crimp with a relatively linear training load: hangs go from 10 seconds at the start of the plan to 5 seconds at the end while the load increases (shorter hangs, higher intensity), with loads set from the test results |
| A, part 2 | Whole plan | Three-finger open: longer-duration two-arm hangs first, then bodyweight repeaters |
| B | Mesocycle 1 | Front-three and back-three combination, at low intensity |
| B, part 1 | Mesocycles 2 and 3 | Full-crimp dead hangs at really low intensity |
| B, part 2 | Mesocycles 2 and 3 | Half-crimp dead hangs on a 10 mm edge, with increased load |

- Combining the four-finger half crimp with the three-finger open grip creates a much more adaptable climber.
- Repeaters get the climber used to repeating movements in a grip they may not find comfortable.
- Front three and back three: for a climber who finds those grips uncomfortable while climbing. The aim is learning to use the grip, not building specific strength, so intensity must stay low; familiarity alone makes you stronger in those positions. Only one mesocycle, expecting the ability to transfer into climbing for the rest of the plan.
- Full crimp then half crimp, to improve small-edge ability. Full crimp first at really low intensity, for familiarity with a grip the climber doesn't like but will need outside; placed first so it isn't done fatigued; be fully warmed up before it. Then half crimp, a grip already familiar, with increased load to build strength on the smaller edge: a learning effect, then a strength effect.
- Why two fingerboard sessions a week: one keeps building intensity on the larger edge, an existing strength to keep; the other works the specific weakness.
- **Warning:** More than two fingerboard sessions a week might overload the fingers or create monotony, which isn't good for motivation.
