# Bouldering Circuit Session

**Type:** Session · **Trains:** Endurance, Technique · **For:** competition climbers

**TL;DR:** Go around one colour circuit of boulders in the gym at flash level or just above, with 1 to 3 attempts per boulder and roughly 2 to 6 minutes rest between boulders, for about an hour and a half. It builds capacity, movement variety, flash practice and execution. Don't make it your only session: done too often it becomes junk mileage, because you rarely work at top-end strength.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Competition climbers | Use it for movement variety, since you need to be in tune with all the different types of moves; competition days are big days with a lot of climbing, so getting used to that amount of climbing is useful, especially in winter |
| Already doing it 2–3 times a week | It is your standard go-to session: limit how much you do it (junk mileage) and push more into projecting sessions or something more specific to your progression |

## The session
- The session comes from the classic bouldering area of Fontainebleau (France), where climbers completed circuits of boulders. Indoors, do the same: go around one colour circuit of boulders in the gym.

| Element | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Circuit | Around flash level or just above |
| Attempts | Aim for 1 to 3 attempts per boulder |
| Failed boulder | If it doesn't go in 3 attempts, move on to the next one |
| Rest between boulders | Roughly 2 to 6 minutes |
| Session length | About an hour and a half |

- The 2–6 minute rest is fairly open, but if you get impatient between boulders, force yourself to rest a bit longer.
- On a hard boulder, use one of the attempts to try the hard second half on its own, then try to link the whole boulder on the next attempt.

## Pros
- It doesn't feel like training: it resembles a normal social session (going around a circuit with friends), but it counts as a training session.
- It is sociable: you go around the wall on set boulders, so it is a good session to get friends involved in.
- It gives lots of movement variety in one session.
- It is good for capacity: if the gym is big enough, circuits have a lot of boulders, so you build a good amount of capacity. This makes it a good session for a base season: building capacity so you recover quicker and get more work done in sessions later on.
- It is good flash practice: practising the execution of fairly hard boulders, which is important particularly for training your ability to read boulders.
- Execution and efficiency: the first time around a circuit, all the boulders are new; you will probably go around it a second, third or even fourth time before it is reset or you move on to a harder circuit. That is the opportunity for execution: you know the moves, so try to execute the whole circuit much quicker and build efficiency into your climbing.
- A good session for your training, especially in winter (when it is freezing outside).

## Cons
- **Warning:** It can become junk mileage if you do it too often. Don't make it your only session: you rarely work at top-end strength.
- **Warning:** It is fatiguing: generally a long session with a lot of boulders. With bad skin it is not a great session, because if you cut it short you don't get the capacity and high-volume benefits.
- It doesn't work at every wall: it works best with colour circuits (e.g. about 35 boulders of the same level). Without colour circuits, or where the gym resets one area at a time, the session is less tidy; it is also trickier when the wall is busy and people are in the way of your mileage.

| Circuit type | What it gives |
|---|---|
| Colour circuit covering a grade range | Some easy boulders and some harder ones thrown in (a pro) |
| Grade circuit (e.g. V3–V4) | Narrows the range, but exposes styles you're not good at, e.g. struggling on a dynamic V4 while doing the static one easily shows your weaknesses |

## How to progress
| If | Then |
|---|---|
| You flash most of the circuit | It is on the easy side: on the next circuit, look for slightly harder boulders and remove some of the easy ones, stepping up the intensity as the weeks go on |
