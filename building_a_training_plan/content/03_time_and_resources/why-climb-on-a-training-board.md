# Why Climb on a Training Board

**Type:** Gear & boards, Principles · **Trains:** Finger strength, Pulling strength, Power, Endurance

**TL;DR:** Board climbing is one of the best tools for finger strength, and its coordination and variety transfer extremely well to outdoor climbing. A board gives you a consistent testing ground to track long-term progress, lets you work your top physical attributes without being limited by movement complexity, and makes tension, endurance and power drills easier to set up. Before getting systematic, build a set of 5–10 board warm-up problems you can climb comfortably.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Want to work the top 5–10% of your physical attributes | Climb on a board: being limited by movement complexity does not help, and board climbing supplements skill training |

## Finger strength
- Board climbing is one of the best tools for finger strength. It is less targeted than hangboarding, but its coordination and variety transfer extremely well to outdoor climbing.

## Measurable progress
- To train consistently over the long term and track long-term progress, you need a consistent testing ground: a training board.
- Many of the best climbers have good intuition about their training from years of experimenting. To understand where your own progress comes from, you need that consistent testing ground.

## Physical vs. skill climbing
- Commercial gym sets are often about complex movement and problem solving, which is good for skill development.

## Versatility
- There are many ways to train on a board: tension training drills, endurance training drills, power training drills and more.
- Suitable boulders for these are hard to find in a commercial gym set; a board makes it easier.

## Before you start
- **Warning:** Many boards start at quite a high difficulty level. Before getting systematic with board training, establish a baseline of board problems you can comfortably warm up on.
- Have a set of 5–10 go-to warm-up problems on the board, increasing in difficulty; they are a good way to judge your readiness to train on the board.
