# Anaerobic Capacity on a System Board: Boulder Triples and Board Repeaters

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Power endurance · **For:** only a board available

**TL;DR:** Anaerobic capacity is the powered-out feeling in the forearms, fingers and upper body: you fail because you power out, not because you are pumped. On a board, repeat boulder problems until you power out (boulder triples), or do board repeaters in set positions and specific grips: 7 seconds hanging, 3 seconds rest, around 5 times per set, with the feet on the floor or on the board to set the intensity, without going too fingery.

## Anaerobic capacity
- A board can be used for the rest of your energy-systems training, not only strength and power; keeping your feet on the ground lowers its intensity.
- Anaerobic capacity is the powered-out feeling: not forearm pump, but feeling you can't hang on any longer, in the forearms and fingers as well as the upper body. You should feel really powered out after each set: failing because you are powering out, not because you are pumped or generally fatigued as in the other energy systems.

| Method | How |
|---|---|
| Repeat boulder problems | Repeat boulder problems, even on quite a short board, until you are powering out; boulder triples are a really good example |
| Repeaters on the board | As on a fingerboard: hold set positions, using specific grip types or specific body positions to mimic your goal routes or your style of climbing. 7 seconds hanging, 3 seconds rest, around 5 times per set. Keep the feet on the floor to get the intensity right, or put the feet on the board to make sure the intensity is high enough |

- **Warning:** In the repeaters, still use really specific grips, but don't go too fingery: this is a really, really hard session.
- Example repeater set: two pinches, compressing for the 7-second hang; in the 3 seconds of rest, put a foot behind you and shake out quickly, then go back.
- Positions to choose for the repeaters: whatever you struggle with, e.g. gastons, short and narrow compression, or wide positions.
