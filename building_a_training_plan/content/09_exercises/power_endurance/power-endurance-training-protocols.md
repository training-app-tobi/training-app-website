# Power-Endurance Training Protocols

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Power endurance

**TL;DR:** Five sport-specific interval protocols for power endurance, all on the wall: bouldering 4x4s on problems two to three grades below your max, Square Dance on a board (about 2 minutes on, 2 off), EMOM boulders two to four grades below your max for 8 to 10 minutes, 30/30 all-out intervals for elite climbers, and campus rung sprints. Do one or two of them a couple of times per week, at most two or three sessions a week, for two to four weeks ahead of a period of performance climbing.

## How to use these protocols
- Do one or two of the five protocols below a couple of times per week, with at most two or three power-endurance sessions a week, for two to four weeks ahead of a period of performance climbing. What power endurance is, why intervals build it and why not to train it for longer are in the note Power Endurance: What It Is and How Long to Train It.

## Bouldering 4x4s
| Element | Do this |
|---|---|
| Problems | Four different boulder problems two to three grades below your max (e.g. a V8 boulderer would likely feel best on V5 or V6) |
| Problem choice | Consistently doable; fairly straightforward and short: no climb longer than 30 seconds or too intricate |
| Hold and move type | Favor big moves on big holds over technical climbs on mostly small holds: the target is full-body pulling power, not technique or finger flexor strength |
| Laps | Climb the first problem four times in a row, with only a brief shake-out and rechalk between goes |
| Rest between laps | At most as long as the preceding lap took, then get straight back on |
| Rest between problems | About 5 minutes after the four ascents |
| Sets | Repeat on the other three problems |
| Target | Each problem completed four times without reaching failure, but brushing right up against it; at the right difficulty you just barely complete the final round of four |
| Adjustment | If you fail before the end, or finish with plenty left, change the problem choice next time |

- Interactive boards (Kilter, Moon, Tension) are ideal platforms for 4x4s because of their size and how easily you can switch between problems.
- Trading off with a partner fits the timing: the partner does their four ascents during your 5-minute rest.

## Square Dance
| Element | Do this |
|---|---|
| Where | Interactive climbing board, systems wall or campus board |
| Start | Hands matched on a low hold, feet centered beneath your body |
| Movement | Reach as high as you can to latch a hold or rung directly above your shoulders; match it with the other hand, feet staying in place. Hang in this extended position for a couple of seconds, then drop the first hand back to the starting hold and match it with the second hand. Hang briefly, then repeat starting with the second hand |
| Work | Keep repeating for about 2 minutes |
| Rest | 2 minutes; you won't feel fully recovered at the end of the rest, but enough to get back on |
| Rounds | Two to four in total |
| Adjustment | If it becomes too intense to keep going, decrease the distance between holds |

## EMOM (Every Minute On the Minute)
| Element | Do this |
|---|---|
| Timer | 8 to 10 minutes |
| Problems | The same number of boulders as minutes, each two to four grades below your max |
| Work | When the timer starts, climb the first problem; start a new problem every minute |
| Rest | Only the time between finishing a climb and the next full minute |
| Adjustment | Switch to easier boulders as needed to complete all 8 or 10 sets |

- Expect heavy breathing.
- **Warning:** Climb smoothly, with efficiency and fluidity, not hurriedly: rushing to gain rest fosters poor technique. Efficiency over urgency.

## 30/30 intervals
- An interval scheme for elite-level climbers wanting to power up for cutting-edge ascents.
- Followed exactly, it exhausts alactic power and builds aerobic power (which drives alactic recovery) without getting too lactic and acidic, which lowers the risk of overtraining syndrome over a few weeks of power-endurance training.

| Element | Do this |
|---|---|
| Where | A spray wall, home woodie, system board or Treadwall with a wide variety of holds |
| How | No specific problems and no focus on sending: climb intuitively at all-out intensity, using the smallest holds and the biggest moves possible without falling off. As you fatigue, use somewhat bigger holds and smaller, less powerful moves |
| Work | Exactly 30 seconds on the wall (all-out climbing) |
| Rest between intervals | 30 seconds off (shaking out, chalking, breathing deeply) |
| Intervals per set | 6 |
| Rest between sets | 5 to 10 minutes, complete rest |
| Sets | Another set of six; well-conditioned climbers can continue up to four sets of six |
| Extra intensity | Elite climbers may add a 2.3 or 4.5 kg weight belt or vest |

## Campus rung sprints
- The closest thing to traditional sprint intervals in climbing: "arm sprint" up the larger rungs of a campus board, as fast as possible.

| Element | Do this |
|---|---|
| Strong board climbers | Ladder up skipping rungs (e.g. 1-3-5-7-9, hand-match on rung 10, then carefully jump down) |
| Everyone else | Hand over hand, using every rung |
| Speed | As fast as possible; for most people the sprint to the top takes 3 to 8 seconds |
| Rest | 30 seconds between sprints |
| Total | Five to ten sprints |

- **Warning:** End the session if you fail to reach the top on any set, or if shoulder and scapular fatigue starts compromising technique (shrugged, disengaged shoulders).
