# Submax Batch: Eight Boulders at 85–90%

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Power endurance, Technique

**TL;DR:** Find or create 8 boulders at 85 to 90% of your max ability level and add more attempts each week. The drill develops power endurance, session recovery capacity and mental resilience, while reinforcing efficient climbing at a submaximal but still highly challenging level. Lock in before every try: you only get a few attempts, so don't waste any.

## Protocol
| Element | Do this |
|---|---|
| Boulders | Find or create 8 boulders |
| Intensity | 85 to 90% of your max ability level |
| Difficulty | Boulders that took you about 3 to 12 tries: hard, but not too hard to send in a single session |
| Progression | Each week, add more attempts |

- At this difficulty most untrained athletes can only do about 4 boulders per session; the goal is to increase that number.

## What it trains
- The submax batch is designed to develop power endurance, session recovery capacity and mental resilience, while reinforcing efficient climbing at a submaximal but still highly challenging level.
- Intensity is 85 to 90% of max: just below limit, but fatigue compounds quickly.
- It is a progressive overload strategy: it trains your ability to maintain power output and precision over more and more total hard efforts per session, because each week you add more attempts.
- Because the boulders are hard but sendable, the drill forces you to stay focused in the face of fatigue, failure and frustration. This strengthens your ability to stick to a plan even when you're tired.

## Beta and energy
- Re-strategize your beta if it breaks down and manage your session energy: as you increase the number of attempts each week, you have to refine your beta and learn to manage your energy between attempts.
- Focus on refining beta and adjusting tactics quickly.

## Lock in
- Don't waste attempts: lock in before every single try, because you only get a few.
- This is the main focus of the drill: show up and give everything you have, with your focus, your discipline in refining and rehearsing your beta, and trying as hard as possible.
- It doesn't matter if your foot slips or you hit a hold while reaching: you might only get two tries on each of these hard boulders, so make every one count.
