# Linked Problems

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Power endurance

**TL;DR:** Linked problems are a power endurance exercise: climb one boulder problem, down-climb on any holes to the start of a second problem, then climb that one too. The aim is to be overloaded for 15 to 20 moves instead of the 6 to 8 moves of a normal boulder problem. Rest about twice as long as in normal bouldering, about four times as long as the work effort.

## Protocol

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Climb one boulder problem as marked on the wall (or as predetermined on a home wall) to the top |
| 2 | Down-climb on open holes (any holes), as efficiently as possible, to the start of a second boulder problem |
| 3 | Shake out at the start of the second problem if needed |
| 4 | Climb the second problem |
| Rest | More rest between sets than normal bouldering: usually about twice as long as in normal bouldering, which is about four times as long as the work effort (rest:work about 4:1) |

- Keep the climber under load during the down-climb, so they are in a real climbing situation for the whole set.
- The aim is to be overloaded for 15 to 20 moves, instead of the standard 6 to 8 moves of a normal boulder problem.
