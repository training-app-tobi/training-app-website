# Two Power Endurance Sessions: Increasing-Rest Intervals and Intensive Triples

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Power endurance

**TL;DR:** Two power endurance sessions that keep every interval at high power output. Timed intervals with increasing rest: 30–60 second efforts, starting with three times as much rest as climbing and adding 20 s of rest each interval. Intensive triples: a problem three times on the minute, then 3 minutes rest, three rounds per set, at about flash or onsight grade, one or two sets. Run them in a cycle of 6–8 sessions, no more than two a week, progress intensive triples by only one variable, and aim for quality, not fatigue.

## How to use these sessions
- Run them in a cycle of 6–8 sessions, no more than two a week, and recover enough between intervals that each one is still done with a high power output. Where power endurance fits and how much to do are in the note Power Endurance Cycles: Where They Fit and How Much.

## Session 1: timed intervals with increasing rest
| Element | Do this |
|---|---|
| Effort | A 30–60 second sustained effort |
| Options | A circuit on a spray wall; linked problems on a boulder wall; on a board (e.g. a Tension Board), go to the top of the wall, traverse down on good holds, then send a problem coming back up, to get the duration where it needs to be |
| First rest | At least three times as much rest as climbing: 30 s climbing → 1 min 30 s rest; 1 min climbing → 3 min rest |
| Following rests | Climbing time stays the same; each interval gets 20 s more rest than the previous one (1st interval: 3× rest; 2nd: +20 s; 3rd: +another 20 s; and so on through the workout) |
| Aim | Arrive at the start of each interval at a more similar level of readiness to climb |

- Typical problems in a bouldering gym or on a fixed board very often are too short, so not enough fatigue builds up within a single interval.

### Fixed clock versus increasing rest
| | Fixed intervals (always 30 s on, 90 s off) | Rest extended by 20 s per interval |
|---|---|---|
| Quality | Can be effective, but output quality degrades through the session and you get very fatigued very quickly | More high-quality work |
| How it feels | After maybe 15 minutes you are badly pumped and it feels like a great workout | More pleasant than being wrecked after 15 minutes of climbing |
| Long-term result | Athletes who constantly get tired very quickly don't improve their fatigue management | Over a 6–8 session cycle, a better level of power endurance |

## Session 2: intensive triples
| Element | Do this |
|---|---|
| Triple | Do a problem three times on the minute for 3 minutes: start of minute 1, climb the problem and rest the remainder of the minute; start of minute 2, climb it again; start of minute 3, climb it again |
| Rest after each triple | 3 minutes (6 minutes in total per round) |
| Set | Three rounds in total, with 3 minutes between them: about 18 minutes |
| Rest between sets | A big 8–10 minute break (drink, stretch, whatever you like) |
| Sets | Go through the same protocol again: two sets take around 45 minutes and give about 18 boulder problems, a great amount of power endurance stimulus. Never done a lot of power endurance training: start with just one |
| Intensity | Start at around your flash or onsight grade and boulder in that zone |

- Compared with circuits or 4x4s, the body refreshes quickly, so you can stay on relatively hard boulders.
- Aim for the highest-quality climbing you can, not for fatigue or soreness. It is better to go through the first two, three or four sessions feeling you are under-doing it than to burn so bright in the first couple of sessions that you don't follow through the whole cycle.

## Progressing intensive triples
Each progression is done over 6–8 progressive sessions across the cycle. Choose the one that matches your power endurance need.

| Goal | Progress by | Keep unchanged | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harder moves under fatigue | Increase the difficulty of at least one of the boulders, or slowly increase the difficulty of all of them | Rest periods and volume | You must still climb well and recover as much as you can in the brief windows between boulders |
| Greater high-intensity capacity (more tries on hard boulders; useful for long bouldering competitions or a trip with lots of hard bouldering volume) | Add more total circuits at the end: from two sets to three, maybe four, making the session longer and longer over the cycle | Boulder difficulty and rest periods | |
| Stringing together longer continuous series of moves | Shorten the rolling clock instead of using 1 minute (e.g. 30 s climbing, 30 s rest): one problem every 55 s, then every 50 s, maybe the following week every 45 s | | Eventually leads to very high fatigue, but quickly raises tolerance to the build-up of waste products in the muscle, so you get used to handling the fatigue and the pain. The most uncomfortable option, but builds the ability to do continuous hard moves on a longer boulder or a route |

- **Warning:** Pick only one progression. Decreasing the rest, increasing the difficulty and lengthening the workout all at once ends in failure.
