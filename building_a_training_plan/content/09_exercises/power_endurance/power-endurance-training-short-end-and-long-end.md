# Power Endurance Training: Short End and Long End

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Power endurance

**TL;DR:** Short end power endurance (12–15 moves, anaerobic capacity): a 12–15-move boulder one grade harder than your maximum onsight, or a feet-on campus board sequence, climbed 3 times with 2.5 min rest, then 5 min rest, 3 sets in all; expect to start failing at the end of the last set, without getting massively pumped. Long end power endurance (30+ moves): double laps on routes or circuits just below your onsight level, 1 min between the two laps and 10–15 min before repeating, 4 times; very pumpy, so do it only after building aerobic and anaerobic capacity.

## Order of the two stages
- Train short end power endurance first (at least 18 weeks to adapt), then long end (6–8 weeks). Why, and what power endurance gives you, is in the note Power Endurance: Short End First, Then Long End.

## Short end power endurance (12–15 moves)
- When you fall off after a number of moves it feels like "fading": you think "I'm not even pumped", but you no longer have the strength to hang on. This is often called being "powered out".
- The energy used is anaerobic (the chemical reaction that makes it doesn't need oxygen). Short end power endurance is also known as anaerobic capacity.
- **Warning:** Short end training does not involve getting massively pumped. Getting pumped loads to learn to tolerate the pump means constantly climbing at your max to failure, which is not a good thing; other sports do not take the approach of constantly doing max performances, and neither should climbers.

### Short end sessions
| Exercise | Choice of problem | Protocol | What to expect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linked boulders | 12–15 move boulder problem (vertical or overhanging), one grade harder than your maximum onsight: not completely at your limit, you can do it after working it for a handful of attempts | Climb it 3 times with 2.5 min rest between climbs. Rest 5 min and repeat 2 more times | On the last set you will probably start to fail at the end of the problems |
| Feet-on campus board | Ideally something where every move and hand type is exactly the same throughout; 12–15 moves with feet on | Climb it 3 times with 2.5 min rest between climbs. Rest 5 min and repeat 2 more times | On the last set you will probably start to fail at the end. If you get too pumped, lower the grade or increase the rest time |

- Make the exercises replicate your goal as much as possible: the angle, the shape of the holds and the type of moves.

## Long end power endurance (30+ moves)
- Start it once you have worked on your aerobic and anaerobic capacity for a while: now you train with a pump. It works the very high-intensity end of the aerobic energy system.
- Short end training built the ability to do hard moves over a short time; long end training is about climbing hard over a much longer time with a deep pump. People who are good at this get pumped just like everyone else, but can carry on climbing for longer.
- The exercise combines highish volume (a good period of time on the wall) and high intensity; it is very hard.

### Long end session
| Exercise | Choice of route | Protocol | What to expect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Double laps | A couple of routes or circuits just below your onsight level | Climb the first route or circuit, rest 1 min, repeat it. Rest 10–15 min and repeat 4 times | You will get very pumped |

- **Warning:** Be careful training long end power endurance. It is very effective, so it is tempting to train it all year round, but you only get the most from it if you have built your aerobic capacity and anaerobic capacity first. Its balance of high volume and quite high intensity means you risk overtraining if you don't train it with care.
