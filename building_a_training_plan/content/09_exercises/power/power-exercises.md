# Power Exercises: General Power Work, Kickboard Campusing, Power Bouldering and Jumping Pulls

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Power

**TL;DR:** General power work: jumping, bounding, box jumps and kettlebell swings for the legs; medicine ball throws, explosive push-ups and explosive pull-ups for the upper body. If you can keep going for 6–10 reps, add force. Specific power: campus off the kickboard so the loaded movement is fast too, power boulder on big holds with two or three explosive moves per drill for two or three sets (fixed boards suit it), and do jumping pulls from a box.

## How to use these exercises
- Use a few general power exercises for a couple of cycles, then spend most of your time on the specific ones (the note Power Development: Training Explosive Strength explains why).

## General power exercises
- Lower body: jumping and bounding; in the gym, box jumps, vertical jumps and kettlebell swings, all movements that need a "snap" to happen.
- If you can do multiple reps and keep going (6, 8 or 10 repetitions of a powerful movement), you're probably not at high speed or at high force: increase the force to increase the power overload.
- Upper body, medicine ball work: chest pass, overhead throw, ball slam against the floor. These are high velocity because medicine balls are light, usually 4.5 to 9 kg, so they sit higher on the velocity end of the curve and lower on the force end.
- Other upper-body options: explosive push-ups, explosive barbell exercises, explosive pull-ups. These general exercises lay the basis for specific training.

## Campus board
- Campusing is often not really power training: people move slowly on the loaded movement and only quickly when moving the unloaded hand to the next rung.
- To develop power on the campus board, either be strong and powerful enough to move quickly, or launch off your feet using the gym's kickboard.
- Using the kickboard isn't taking it easy: the coordinated foot-and-arm movement is useful in climbing anyway, and with the legs you can jump further and generate more upper-body power. If you can only move slowly and then snap to the next rung, using your legs develops power better.

## Power bouldering
- Bouldering gym climbing is often not powerful: even short, savage problems may be slow strength needing high force, and you can't move explosively on very small holds.
- For total-body power and explosiveness, stick to pretty big holds and make the reach or the complexity of the movement the limiter, not your ability to stay on the holds.

| Element | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Moves | As simple as two big moves per arm on open holds |
| Where | Fixed boards (Moon Board, Kilter Board) are great for it |
| Per drill | Two or three explosive movements, treated as an exercise or drill rather than a boulder problem |
| Sets | Two or three |
| Then | Build a habit of power first, then go on to hard boulder problems |

## Jumping pull-up
- Jumping pull: hold a pull-up bar or campus board and jump off a box (or the floor) to generate explosive pulling. A general exercise that can be quickly applied to specific strength.
