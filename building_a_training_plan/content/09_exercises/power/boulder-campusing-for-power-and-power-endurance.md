# Boulder Campusing for Power and Power Endurance

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Power, Power endurance · **For:** Advanced

**TL;DR:** Boulder campusing means climbing an overhanging wall hand over hand with no feet. For maximum power, do 3 fast campus ascents of a 3- to 5-move problem on good holds, resting 3 to 5 minutes between them. For power endurance, campus 10 to 20 hand moves (15 to 35 seconds). It is a very advanced method: you need about 15 to 20 solid pull-ups, strong scapular stabilizers and rotator cuff, and clean technique, or you risk wrecking your shoulders.

## What it is
- Boulder campusing: climb up a section of overhanging wall hand over hand, with no help from the feet. It is similar to laddering on a campus board, so power training isn't limited to a campus board. It is a popular indoor exercise among advanced climbers.
- **Warning:** The same injury warnings as campus board laddering apply: campusing with straight-arm "catches", shrugged shoulders and a caved-in chest will get you injured, sooner rather than later.

## Before you start
- If you can't do about 15 to 20 solid pull-ups, you are probably not strong enough to boulder campus with good form. Instead, train weighted pull-ups to build more base strength, which you can then convert to higher power with boulder campusing and on the campus board.
- If you aren't doing targeted training of your scapular stabilizers and rotator cuff (scapular pull-ups, internal and external rotation, Ys and Ts on a sling trainer, etc.), begin.

## Maximum power vs power endurance
| Goal | How it is developed | On a campus board | On a boulder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maximum power | Brief bursts (under 10 seconds) of near-limit, high-speed movements | 1-4-7-10 | 3- to 5-move route, climbed in no more than 6 to 8 seconds |
| Power endurance | A rapid succession of near-maximal bursts of power lasting 20 to 60 seconds | "Campus ladder" up and down (1-3-5-7-9-9-7-5-3-1, and repeat) for about 45 seconds. Boring but effective | Campus a long section of overhanging wall for 10 to 20 hand moves (15 to 35 seconds). More interesting |

## Boulder campusing for maximum power
- Maximum power is best trained on a campus board, where you can focus on straightforward, high-speed movements near your very limit.
- To train maximum power on an overhanging boulder, choose a 3- to 5-move route with medium to large holds you can use with an open-hand or open-crimp grip. The ideal route has no small or tweaky holds, no awkward arm positions and no reaches so long that you can't avoid (dangerous) straight-arm positions.
- Climb fast: if the sequence takes you more than 6 to 8 seconds, the moves are too big and powerful for you. Choose an easier problem, or better, use the campus board instead.

| Protocol | Ascents | Rest between ascents |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | 3 campus ascents of the boulder | 3 to 5 minutes |
| Very strong climbers | 3 ascents of each of 3 different problems | Rest completely |

- Either way, always rest completely between ascents: quality over quantity is the key to developing higher power.

## Boulder campusing for power endurance
- Campus a long section of overhanging wall for 10 to 20 hand moves (15 to 35 seconds).
- Even on jugs, boulder campusing for power endurance is a very advanced method: it needs good kinesthetic awareness of scapular positioning and scapular stabilizers and rotator cuff muscles strong enough to keep proper form throughout the ascent.

## Technique and safety
- **Warning:** Good campusing technique is essential. Many climbers have wrecked their shoulders (often needing surgery) through overzealous campus training with poor technique, caused by insufficient base strength and/or campusing while very fatigued.
- Have a friend film a short video of your boulder campusing so you can assess the quality of your technique.
- Favor boulders with fewer moves and short reaches to make sure you use good campus technique.
- Balance hard training with adequate rest to optimize adaptations and reduce injury risk.
