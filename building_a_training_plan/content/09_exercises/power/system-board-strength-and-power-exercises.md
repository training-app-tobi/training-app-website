# System Board Strength and Power Exercises

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Pulling strength, Finger strength, Power

**TL;DR:** Strength and power work on the system board improves upper-body strength and the ability to move between holds. Start with ladders (2 or 3 laps per position) to get the fingers stronger and warm up, then move to the harder drills: diagonal locks, pull into position (3 to 5 full reps per side), lob, lock and hover (2 to 3 seconds), lock down, one-up and two-up.

## What strength and power work is for
- The strength and power workouts on the system board are dedicated to improving upper-body strength and the ability to move between holds.

## Exercises
| Exercise | How | Dose / progression | What it's for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ladder | Climb straight up and down on open holds, using any of the variety of holds on the system tiles. Switch between jugs, crimps and pockets | Usually 2 or 3 laps on any given position | The basic strength and power exercise: get the fingers stronger and get warmed up for the harder sets |
| Diagonal locks | Big reaches left, right, left, then repeat going the other direction | — | Cross-body positioning |
| Pull into position | A one-arm movement; a somewhat contrived movement | Usually 3 to 5 full reps per side. Make it harder by reducing the size of the start hold: a smaller and smaller start hold makes it much harder | Forces you to develop strength in that one arm through the whole range of motion |
| Lob | A semi-dyno done on really bad feet with same-side positioning: right hand and right foot planted, lob up to the left, and vice versa | — | — |
| Lock and hover | Pull up, lock off, hover above the target hold, then take that hold; move on up and do the same again | Hover for 2 to 3 seconds | Traditional climbing, or any time you deal with small hand- or footholds |
| Lock down | Pull all the way through the range of motion, pressing the holds down as far as possible. Best done on the jugs | — | Bridges the gap between what the lats can do pulling and what the triceps have to do |
| One-up | A dyno that goes right to the end of your range of motion, but the feet don't leave the wall. Take a good hold, pull it all the way down, reach up and grab something at the limit of your reach | — | — |
| Two-up | A long reach to a hold, then an explosion to one slightly above it | — | A favourite power exercise; great for climbers who aren't used to leaving the rock and don't have a bouldering mentality |
