# Strength and Power Boulder Sets: 6×3 Hard and 4×3 Fast

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Finger strength, Power · **For:** weak for the grade

**TL;DR:** Train strength with 6 sets of 3 reps on hard, known boulder problems 1-2 grades below your maximum, and power with 4 sets of 3 reps on moderate problems 5-6 grades below your maximum, climbed with a heavy speed element. Rest 3 min between reps and between sets in both, train fresh, and stop when form or session quality drops. As a very rough guide, only two strength or power sessions per week.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Weak for the grade | Use strength and power training, both as a boulderer and as a route climber |
| Lacked power for a long time | Train power more regularly (power training has a significant skill element) |

## What strength and power are
- Strength is the ability to exert force against resistance (your own bodyweight or an object); in the simplest sense it is the maximal force applied in a single contraction. This raises questions of how to measure it, for how long, and which muscles are most specific to climbing performance.
- Power includes a time component: it measures the velocity of a muscle contraction (and joint angle change), so high power is a function of both force and speed. Because of the strength-velocity relationship, maximal power is seen at about one third of max strength and one third of max speed.

## Why they matter for climbing
- Climbing at very high intensity is often limited by the forearm's ability to hold a static grip position (hands not opening out). High finger strength lets the forearm resist the high forces that try to open grip positions.
- Your ability to exert power depends on your strength level. With low power but high strength you may feel great on static/slow moves but struggle on dynamic sequences. This is highly simplified: skill level also greatly affects it.

## Campusing
- **Warning:** Campus exercises are for adults only; the greatest benefits are seen in more advanced adult climbers. Intermediate climbers who want to try them should seek advice from someone they trust. In almost all circumstances juniors should stay well away from a campus board, for multiple reasons.

## Strength session
| Element | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Volume | 6 different boulder problems, 3 reps each = 6 sets of 3 reps |
| Each rep | A hard boulder problem |
| Rest between reps | 3 min |
| Rest between sets | 3 min |
| Grade | 1-2 grades below your maximum boulder grade |

- Increasing the rest is completely acceptable, to recover better for the next boulder.
- Use known boulder problems, so technical limitations are reduced.
- The grade must be close to your maximum to make strength gains, so train when fresh.
- Choose "basic" style problems, so you aren't tempted to "trick" your way around the difficulties.
- **Warning:** In all strength sessions, form must stay good throughout and session quality high. If you lose either, stop for the day and make adjustments for the next session.

## Power session
- Power sessions often carry both a strength and a power stimulus; first identify which muscle group you are trying to develop power in. Example: a campus session may develop finger "strength" but upper-body "power".
- Power workouts must have a heavy speed element and be relatively short.
- **Warning:** Do power workouts fresh and well warmed up, never at the end of a session.

| Element | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Volume | 4 sets of 3 reps |
| Each set | 1 boulder problem repeated 3 times |
| Rest between reps | 3 min |
| Rest between sets | 3 min |
| Grade | 5-6 grades below your maximum (at your maximum it is very hard to include a significant speed element) |

- The problems should be a moderate grade for you, on holds that are comfortable in size and shape.
- Aim to feel "powered out" by the end of the session, with failure only in the last few reps, if at all.
- When testing the problems, set big moves that feel within your comfort zone.

## How often, and when in the season
- **Warning:** As a very rough guide, only two sessions per week of strength or power training, for boulderers and route climbers alike. Anyone considering more should speak to a coach or a trusted individual: this training is easy to overdo.
- When in the season to train strength and when power is in the note Strength in Base Periods, Power 2–6 Weeks Before a Goal.

## Summary points
- Power training improves force applied at speed in the upper body and forearms; exactly the same methods can be applied to the lower body.
- Your training should take into account your training history, injury profile and goals.
