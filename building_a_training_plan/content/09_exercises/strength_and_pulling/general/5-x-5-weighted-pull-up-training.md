# 5 x 5 Weighted Pull-up Training

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Pulling strength, Power · **For:** fewer than 10 pull-ups

**TL;DR:** Do weighted pull-up training three times per week for ten weeks to increase pull-muscle and lock-off strength, lunging power and pulling endurance by 20% or more. Each session is five sets of five weighted pull-ups with at least 3 minutes of rest after each set, at a weight that brings you to near-complete failure on the last rep of the fourth and fifth sets. Test before and after. If you cannot do at least 10 consecutive true pull-ups, do the spotted bodyweight program first.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Already plenty strong | This program is not for you: train for one-arm pull-up strength instead |

## The program
- Weighted pull-up training three times per week for ten weeks, aimed at increasing pull-muscle and lock-off strength, lunging power and pulling endurance by 20% or more.

## Test your pull-up strength (before and after the 10 weeks)
| Test | What it measures | How | 10-week goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Endurance test | The number of true pull-ups you can do in one set | A true pull-up goes all the way down to straight arms and pulls up until the chin is fully above the bar (arms in lock-off position); partial pull-ups don't count. Do the test after a complete warm-up, on a pull-up bar or the bucket holds of a hangboard. Record the result in a training notebook | A 20% increase in the number of pull-ups |
| Strength test | The maximum added weight with which you can do a single true pull-up | Equipment: a few weight belts and a vest with removable weights, or better, a dip belt or harness from which weight plates can hang. After a warm-up, do a series of one-rep pull-up sets, adding weight each time until you find the maximum weight for one true pull-up. Record the result | Your 20% goal weight |

Strength test starting load and increments:

| Climber | Start with | Increase by |
|---|---|---|
| Really strong climbers | 25% of bodyweight added | About 4.5 kg per step |
| Most others | 10% of bodyweight added | 2.3 kg per step |

## Hypergravity training
- Simulate hypergravity (more than gravity's natural pull) by adding weight to the body: do all of your pull-up training with added weight (weight vest, belt, or free weights hanging from your harness).
- Why it works: the neuromuscular system adapts to the apparent increase in gravity, and long-term you develop a new level of strength to match the higher weight. On the rock at normal bodyweight you feel lighter, like the feet feel light after taking off ski boots or a heavy backpack.

## The 5 x 5 weighted pull-up protocol
| Element | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Frequency | Three times per week for ten weeks, on normal training and/or climbing days |
| Sets x reps | Five sets of five weighted pull-ups |
| Rest | At least 3 minutes after each set |
| Timing in the session | Ideally in the middle part of the workout, when the muscles are well warmed but still relatively fresh |
| Training weight | Experiment at first to find a weight that is difficult but doable, bringing you to near-complete failure on the last rep of the fourth and fifth sets. For many people it settles around half of their one-rep max added weight (from the strength test) |
| Progression | Increase the training weight as needed to stay with this protocol throughout the ten weeks |
| Retest | After the ten weeks, take two to four days off from serious training or climbing, then do a solid warm-up and repeat the strength and endurance tests |

- Vary the distance between your hands slightly with each set.
- **Warning:** Do not "hang rest" in the middle of a pull-up set.

## If you can't do 10 pull-ups
- Weighted pull-up training is not appropriate if you cannot complete at least 10 consecutive true pull-ups; do the bodyweight program below instead.

| Element | Bodyweight program |
|---|---|
| Frequency | Three days per week for ten weeks |
| Sets x reps | Five sets of five to eight pull-ups per session |
| Method | Begin each set with your own power, then have a spotter lift you at the hips just enough to complete five to eight reps |
| Outcome | Within 10 weeks you should be able to do 10 pull-ups on your own and feel much stronger on the rock |
| Next step | Move on to the weighted 5 x 5 protocol |

## Injury risk
- **Warning:** Over-enthusiastic training and too few rest days are common contributing factors to injury for ambitious climbers. Climbers who seldom get injured do high-intensity training followed by sufficient rest and low-load training, develop stronger muscles and tendons season over season, and have a better chance of advancing their climbing.
