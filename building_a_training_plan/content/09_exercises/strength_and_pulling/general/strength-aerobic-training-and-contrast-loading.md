# Strength Aerobic Training and Contrast Loading

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** General strength, Pulling strength, Endurance · **For:** stalled progress

**TL;DR:** Strength aerobic training builds local muscular endurance: a 4-rep strength set at a load you could lift 4–6 times, then straight away 10 reps at 4 seconds per rep at about 40–50% of that load, a big rest, and 2–3 more rounds, on just one exercise such as pull-ups. Contrast loading alternates heavy and light days (e.g. hang light on Monday, heavy on Wednesday, light on Friday) and adds sets at the same load over a four-week cycle, keeping volume up while giving tendons and muscles a break.

## Strength aerobic training
| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Strength set | Example: weighted pull-ups. Load a weight you can do 4–6 reps with and do 4 reps at normal tempo (a load that makes those 4 reps challenging). |
| Tempo set | Immediately after, strip the weight off. 4 seconds per repetition, no pause at the bottom and no pause at the top, 10 reps (a total of 40 seconds of movement), at about 40–50% of the strength-set load. If you use a lot of weight on your belt, you might just drop to bodyweight. |
| Rest | Take a big rest. |
| Repeat | Repeat the same pattern 2–3 more times. |

- The tempo set is agonizing: it really loads up the local muscles you're using and is quite challenging.
- Exercise choice: don't do it with a lot of exercises (not six in one workout). If you have never tried it, start with one exercise. Pull-ups are great; some leg exercises and the bench press are also good.
- Effect: aimed at increasing muscular endurance. It doesn't produce big jumps in strength, but it does produce big jumps in muscular endurance; your max number of pull-ups could take a big leap forward.
- For a specific need for local muscular endurance, this method fits the bill.
- **Warning:** It is painful and really beats you up, so don't do a lot of it: take one exercise, focus on it with strength aerobic training and see how it feels.

## Contrast loading
- Fundamentals: one day of heavy loading and one day of lighter loading, alternating between them. Can be used with lots of different exercises.
- Good on the hangboard, for pressing and for pull-ups.

| Week | Day 1 | Day 2 | Day 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Heavy | Light | Heavy |
| 2 | Light | Heavy | Light |
| Following weeks | Keep alternating | Keep alternating | Keep alternating |

- Simple plan example: hang your normal training session at a light load on Monday, at a heavy load on Wednesday, back to the light load on Friday.
- Progression: as in ladder programming, stay at the same load but do more sets each session (heavy, more sets; light, more sets; heavy, more sets). Over a four-week cycle this builds up a lot of volume in these exercises.
- Loads: specific numbers can be put to it, but the spirit is to go pretty hard on heavy days and back off quite a bit on light days, to something really achievable.

| Exercise | Heavy day | Light day |
|---|---|---|
| Single-arm overhead press | About 32 kg | About 16 kg |
| Pull-ups | Heavy weighted pull-ups | Bodyweight or reduced-weight (assisted) pull-ups |
| Hangboard | Add load (the simplest way) | Bodyweight |

- Why it works: contrasting heavy and light loads gives tendons and muscles a break while keeping volume up, so you keep stimulating growth and improvement without always working at a very high percentage.
- The key is that it is a stimulus you probably haven't been using: it can bring freshness to training and open doors you haven't had access to while stuck in a rut.
