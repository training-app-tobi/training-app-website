# Timed Sets to Break a Strength Plateau

**Type:** Exercise/protocol, Planning · **Trains:** General strength, Pulling strength · **For:** stalled progress

**TL;DR:** When adding load to the same sets and reps stops working, move the primary exercise to timed sets: 2 or 3 reps on the minute, each set done in under 10 seconds, with total reps 20% higher than before (up to 2x). Start at about 10% less than your 5RM, add sets up to 30 reps, then add load in tiny steps, 2 sessions per week for 16 sessions. Then retest: most people end up with a working 5RM 5–8% higher than ever.

## Who should do what

| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Hitting a strength plateau (adding load to the same sets and reps no longer works) | Move the primary exercise to timed sets: 2–3 reps on the minute, total reps 20% higher (up to 2x), 2 sessions per week for 16 sessions |

## Why strength stalls
- Usual progression: add resistance. If you can do 3 sets of 5 reps at a given weight, add about 0.9 kg to the bar next workout; when you can do 3x5 at that load, add more, and so on. This works for a long time, but progress eventually stops, even if you stay consistent and motivated. Applies to big muscles (legs, back), fingers or combinations of muscle groups.
- What holds you back is a bad training habit showing up in today's training: chasing the weight too aggressively, not resting enough, or lack of consistency. Instead of a lateral move (a new exercise, a new workout structure or a different type of training), move the primary exercise to a timed-set arrangement: just a couple of reps, then a forced adequate rest, repeated many times.

## Baseline example
- Stuck at 3 sets of 5 pull-ups with 4.5 kg on the weight belt: with timed strength, do sets of 2 reps at the same load (bodyweight + 4.5 kg), but many sets.
- The 3x5 session is 15 total reps at about the 5RM load, which is probably around 85% of your single-rep max.

## Setting up timed sets
| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Set timing | Sets are done on the minute (OTM) and must be completed in under 10 seconds, so stick to 2 or 3 reps |
| Total reps | Increase total reps by 20% to start (vs. the old 3x5), and be willing to go up to a 2x increase |
| First session | About 18 reps (15 + 20%), 2 reps each minute = 9 sets |
| Starting load | Even less than the 5RM you are moving away from, about 10% less. The first OTM session is quite easy (in 9 sets it hardly feels tiring), but it is a safe place to start |

- Starting load example: 3x5 pull-ups at bodyweight 68 kg + 22.5 kg = 90.5 kg total → new set-up is 2 reps on the minute at bodyweight + 13.5 kg, i.e. 10% less than the 5RM.

## How to progress
| Phase | Sessions | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Build volume | 1–4 | 9 → 11 → 13 → 15 sets of 2 reps, i.e. 30 reps per workout |
| Add load | Following sessions, over 6–8 weeks | Add load only when all 30 reps are perfect (rep 30 indistinguishable from rep 1). Go slowly: add just 0.5–0.9 kg, and only advance if every rep is perfect. Keep adding load; you shouldn't quite "hit a wall" |
| Frequency | Whole block | Only 2 sessions per week, so 16 timed-set workouts in 8 weeks |
| Retest | Workout 17 | Either a max pull-ups at bodyweight test, or a standard 3x5 with loads heavier than your best-ever 3x5 |

## 3x5 retest
| Set | Load |
|---|---|
| 1 | Your best-ever load (it should feel easy) |
| 2 | Add 2.3 kg |
| 3 | Add another 2.3 kg |

- 9 out of 10 people who follow the workout carefully end up with a working 5RM 5–8% higher than ever, which is a large gain in strength terms.

## Things to keep in mind
- It's not magic, just different: timed sets mainly fix volume deficiencies and poor resting habits. If your volume and rest are already good, expect smaller results.
- Stick to one exercise. It has also worked in pairs of exercises, or in different sessions on back-to-back days, but keep it simple at first.
- After 16 sessions, do something else: change exercises, go back to normal set-rep schemes, or move to higher reps for a while.
