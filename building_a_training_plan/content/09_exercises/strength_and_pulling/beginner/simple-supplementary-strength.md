# Simple Supplementary Strength: One Pull, One Push and One Hip Hinge

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** General strength, Pulling strength · **For:** Beginner

**TL;DR:** Supplementary strength after climbing supports climbing rather than chasing grades: keep it to one pulling exercise (low row), one pushing exercise (bench press) and one squat or hip-hinge exercise (deadlift or back squat), 6–10 reps for 2–3 sets once or twice a week, resting at least 2 min between sets. Adding lots of exercises that worked for other people is a big mistake at this stage.

## Supplementary strength and conditioning
- Aim: not necessarily to climb harder grades, but to support climbing. Jumping off the wall, landing on mats and the overall physical load mean that healthy hips, elbows and wrists support your climbing.
- Keep it simple: one pulling exercise, one pushing exercise, one squat or hip-hinge exercise.

| Exercise | Reps | Sets |
|---|---|---|
| Low row (pull) | A challenging 6–10 | 2–3; not after every climbing session, once or twice a week |
| Bench press (push) | 6–10; if still working on form and confidence, keep that rep range not challenging at first, work on form, then build the intensity | 2–3 (same as low row) |

- Pull: low row instead of pull-ups. For many people a bodyweight pull-up is a really high intensity; many gyms (e.g. in the UK) have no lat pulldown machine to lower the intensity, and bands are useful but can be a bit floppy. The low row is a compound horizontal pull that works the lats without such an acute elbow angle, which can cause irritation.
- Low row cues: pull the weight all the way back to your side or hip; retract the shoulder blades, squeezing the middle of the back (traps and rhomboids).
- Push: bench press, a compound movement for chest and triceps. It is not just an antagonist exercise: the pecs are a big pulling muscle when overhead and stabilize the shoulder joint, so a strong chest makes strong shoulders; it supports climbing and also benefits performance.
- Hip hinge: deadlift, or back squat if you feel more confident with it. A big compound lift working core and lower body; very posterior chain (hamstrings, glutes) with a lot of core activation to stabilize the trunk; supports knees and lower back for the jumps and falls onto the mat.
- Rest between sets of strength exercises: you generally can't go wrong resting too long; resting too little may help increase muscle fatigue and hypertrophy; rest at least 2 min or longer.
- **Warning:** Other exercises (forearm strength, extensor work, rotator cuff, core work) might be a good choice, but a big mistake at this stage is adding lots of exercises that have worked for other people: too much loses focus and puts more focus on strength and conditioning than on climbing. Push, pull and hip hinge should work for most people.
