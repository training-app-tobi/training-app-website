# Fingerboarding for Beginners: No Failure, Grips, Repeaters and Hanging Position

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Finger strength · **For:** Beginner

**TL;DR:** Never reach failure: fingers opening out or slipping off means the intensity is too hard. Hang at around bodyweight, on a bigger edge if needed. Repeaters: 5 seconds on, 5 seconds off, 3 times, 1–2 minutes rest, 3–5 sets, each hang at relatively low intensity. Without other climbing, no more than 2–3 sessions a week.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Not doing any other climbing | No more than 2–3 sessions a week, absolute maximum, even at low intensity: the same grip on the same holds every time loads the fingers a lot |

## No failure
- **Warning:** Never reach failure in any exercise. If the fingers open out or slip off, the intensity is too hard: reduce it so you complete every set and rep safely, with a little left in the tank. This is the first and most important rule.

| Grip | Failure sign | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| Half crimp | Fingers start creeping open under load, which puts a lot of stress on the soft tissues of the fingers | End the hang, feet on the ground, rest until the next set, reduce the intensity |
| Open grip | Little finger starts slipping off and the hand opens out | Feet on the ground, rest for the remainder of the set, reduce the load for the next set |

- Six sets of max hangs is only 1 minute of effort: completed at a manageable intensity, it is a really good training stimulus without overloading you. Going to failure is what pushes you over the limit.

## Load
- Hang at around bodyweight, on an edge you can hang on comfortably for the whole planned session, even if that means a bigger edge than others use: it still gives a really good strength-training stimulus, without taking weight off or complicated load calculations.
- To reduce the load (only a small edge or a jug available, or when progressing): a foot on a chair or object under the fingerboard, with the area kept clear.
- **Warning:** Bodyweight repeaters are often recommended as a starting point, but if your maximum hang for about 5–10 seconds is less than bodyweight, repeating that effort several times is too intense. Bring the intensity down so you complete every set and rep.

## Grips
- **Warning:** Full crimp (thumb wrapped over) is quite intense to begin with and not recommended: it puts a lot of strain on the finger joint.
- **Warning:** Half crimp (fingers at 90 degrees, last finger joint straight, no thumb; also called chisel) puts a lot of load through the forearms and is a really good way to get stronger, but puts much more stress on tendons and joints: build up to it if it isn't naturally comfortable.
- Front-three drag is comfortable and can build a lot of strength, but trains you to use only that position and transfers less to other skills.
- Until you reach quite a high intensity, keep using whichever of half crimp or open you prefer; as you progress and get more comfortable, mix the two and share the training between them.
- Finger length changes how a grip looks (in a four-finger open grip, fingers other than the index may be quite bent, which is normal): don't judge your grip by what others do; go by what you use and how it feels.

## Repeaters
| Element | Protocol |
|---|---|
| Set | 5 seconds hang, 5 seconds rest, 3 times: a 30-second set |
| Rest between sets | About 1–2 minutes |
| Sets | About 3–5 |
| Intensity | Relatively low for each hang, because the total hanging time adds up to a lot of volume; often feet on the ground or on a chair |
| Adjusting | Feet on the floor, bend the legs to put more weight on the hands; if the fingers open out or slip off, stand up a bit more to take load off |

## Hanging position
- **Warning:** Chin up, look slightly up at the bottom of the fingerboard, head neutral and between the arms. Moving the head forward or looking down at the feet puts a lot of stress on the neck.
- **Warning:** Don't arch the lower back into a semi-front-lever position: it engages the chest a lot and stresses the lower back and shoulders. Stay directly under the fingerboard.
- Avoid a deep lock-off at the start: it is a very specific position to train. If your joints feel slightly unstable, keep a slight elbow bend so the biceps are slightly engaged.

## Building up
- Entry standard for more advanced assessments and training methods: a bodyweight hang on a 20 mm edge for 10 seconds. If you can't do that yet, max hangs and repeaters build up to it and to harder hangs and more complex training later. Take your time, build up slowly and be consistent.
