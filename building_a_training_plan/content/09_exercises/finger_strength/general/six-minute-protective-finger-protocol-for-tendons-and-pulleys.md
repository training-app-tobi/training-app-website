# Six-Minute Protective Finger Protocol for Tendons and Pulleys

**Type:** Exercise/protocol, Injury & health · **Trains:** Finger strength · **For:** Training hard frequently, injured or returning

**TL;DR:** A brief, moderate-intensity 6-minute protocol strengthens the finger flexor tendons and annular pulleys as "prehab". Do 3 rounds of 30 s finger rolls, 30 s finger extensions and 1 minute of 10 s on / 5 s off repeaters on large to medium holds. Unlike intensive hangboarding, it can be done almost daily, on rest days or as part of the warm-up.

## Why fingers need protecting
- Climbing is hard on the finger flexor tendons, the annular pulleys (A2 and the others) and the finger joints.
- If you climb and/or train frequently at a rigorous level, there is a good chance of finger and joint pain, and possibly a more significant overuse or acute injury.

## What the protocol does
- A brief, moderate-intensity 6-minute "protective" finger protocol strengthens the finger flexor tendons and annular pulleys.
- It works as "prehab": it enhances collagen synthesis in tendons and ligaments and so improves the health and strength of these structures.
- **Warning:** Intensive hangboard protocols must be limited to just a few days per week: they are hard on the muscles, nervous system and connective tissues, and doing too much gives a good chance of ending up injured.

## When to use it
Unlike intensive hangboarding, this protocol can be done almost daily.

| Use | How |
|---|---|
| Rest day | As a prehab session |
| Training or climbing day | As part of the warm-up before the main climbing or training of the day |

## Six-minute protective protocol
Do one round, then repeat it 2 more times: 3 rounds = 6 minutes of non-fatiguing loading.

| Step | Exercise | Duration | Details |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Finger rolls (or squeeze donut) | 30 s | Moderate load |
| 2 | Finger extensions | 30 s | Finger-extension trainer or similar |
| 3 | Repeaters, 10 s on / 5 s off | 1 min (four 10-second hangs) | Large to medium-sized holds; open hand, open crimp, or half crimp (half crimp only if uninjured) |

- **Warning:** Do not let the exercise escalate into anything more stressful or more voluminous than described above.
