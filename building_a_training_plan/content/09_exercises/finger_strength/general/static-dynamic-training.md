# Static-Dynamic Training: Barbell Finger Rolls for Forearm Endurance

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Finger strength, Endurance · **For:** stalled progress

**TL;DR:** Static-dynamic finger rolls build forearm muscular endurance in the weight room: 2 full-range barbell finger rolls, then a 10-second hang at full extension at the fingertips, repeated for 5 minutes or more. Do them standing at a rack with the spotting trays below you; loads are very light (few athletes go beyond a 20.5 kg barbell), and they are useful when you are stuck in a weight room with no climbing training.

## Static-dynamic training (finger rolls)
| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Setup | Barbell finger rolls, standing at a barbell rack with the spotting trays below you, so a dropped barbell doesn't crash onto the floor. |
| Reps | 2 reps of rolling the barbell up as far as it will go and letting it back down (full contraction, full range of motion). |
| Hang | Right after the second rep, hold the weight at full extension at the tips of your fingers and hang there for 10 seconds. |
| Repeat | 2 more reps, 10 seconds, 2 more reps, 10 seconds, and so on. |
| Duration | Start with 5 minutes of this. Going to 5 minutes and more is a big key to developing muscular endurance. |

- Effect: a very focused muscular endurance effort. It changes the muscle length and moves blood through the forearm, and simulates well the forearm feel of big endurance training days, when you're really beating up your forearms.
- Practical: can be done in the weight room, is fairly simple to implement and works with very light loads; few athletes move beyond a 20.5 kg barbell.
- Other tools: it can also be done with a block or a gripper, but the barbell seems the best option.
- It could also be done with squats, presses, pull-ups and similar, but the exercise gets extremely difficult very quickly; finger rolls are the most appropriate place to do it.
- Useful when stuck in a weight room where you can't do any climbing training.
