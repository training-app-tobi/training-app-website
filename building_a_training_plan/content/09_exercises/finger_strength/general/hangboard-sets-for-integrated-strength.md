# Hangboard Sets for Integrated Strength: Three Positions, 10-Second Hangs

**Type:** Exercise/protocol, Principles · **Trains:** Finger strength · **For:** Intermediate to advanced

**TL;DR:** Climbing alone gives uneven, irregular finger overload, so use the hangboard: 3 sets of 10-second hangs in three hand positions, a starting point simple and safe enough to keep until it stops bringing improvement. Train the hold types you use most and those that give you the most trouble, train three positions exclusively through a 4–6 week phase, and change programs every couple of months rather than chasing the best protocol.

## Finger strength

- In an Integrated Strength session (the note Integrated Strength: Hangboard, Weights and Mobility in One Session), finger strength sets alternate with heavy resistance sets and mobility drills, each serving as rest for the others.
- Climbing alone to gain finger strength can give uneven loading, lets you avoid certain difficult hold types, and isn't a regular enough overload for optimum improvement. The tool is the hangboard: repeated sets of isometric hangs.
- **Warning:** trouble comes from going too hard or too long on the board: what makes you strong so quickly can also injure you. Even good programs lead to trouble, mostly from hurrying through sessions or progressing too quickly.

| Starting point | Sets | Hang | Positions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hangboard | 3 | 10 seconds | Three different hand positions |

- The starting point is so simple and safe that you should stick with it as long as possible, at the very least until you no longer see any improvement from it.
- Specificity: train both the hold types you use most often and the ones that give you the most trouble.
- The hold combinations on a hangboard are almost endless; don't get sucked in. No protocol is "the best"; the best program is probably to change programs every couple of months. Pick three positions and train them exclusively through a 4-6 week phase. Ask yourself: do you want to be trained or entertained?
