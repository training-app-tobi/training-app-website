# Short, Hard Finger Strength Sets Between Heavy Lifts

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Finger strength · **For:** in-season

**TL;DR:** In an integrated strength workout, each heavy lift and mobility exercise is followed by a short finger set: 4–6 hangs of 2–3 s with good rest, or an easy version of 3 s hangs on a rolling 20 s clock for 2 minutes. The 3 s hang must be challenging: bodyweight on a 20 mm edge is probably not enough, so add load or use harder holds, and vary arm angles, campus movement or applied board work as needed.

## Finger strength set
- In an integrated strength workout (the note Integrated Strength: Heavy Lifts, Finger Strength and Mobility in One Session), each circuit is one heavy strength exercise, then one mobility exercise, then one finger strength exercise, done three times.

| Set | Hang | Rest | Reps | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard | 2–3 s | Good rest | 4–6 short repetitions | — |
| Easy version | 3 s | 17 s (rolling 20 s clock) | 6 | 2 minutes |

- Finger strength is not only straight-armed hanging on a hangboard: you can change arm angles, do movement on a campus board, work difficult finger positions, or do applied finger strength on the board. Work out what you most need more work on and apply that in this training.
- **Warning:** The 3 s hang must be challenging. Hanging bodyweight on a 20 mm edge is probably not enough to overload the muscle; tendons, connective tissue and muscles adapt to very high loads and high force production, and without that you are probably wasting your time. Add load, or choose holds that are challenging at bodyweight.
- Light or even sub-bodyweight loading (as in a no-hangs program) has been effective for finger strength, but only with regular, very low-volume loading across many days in a row. This format is different, so the intensity has to go up.
