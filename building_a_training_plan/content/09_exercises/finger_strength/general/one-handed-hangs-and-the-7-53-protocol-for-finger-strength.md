# One-Handed Hangs and the 7/53 Protocol for Finger Strength

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Finger strength · **For:** Intermediate, Advanced

**TL;DR:** The one-handed protocol is 5-second one-handed hangs on a 20 to 24 mm edge, alternating hands, with 3 to 5 minutes of rest, for up to 5 or 6 hangs per hand, 2 days per week at most. It is a very difficult method, appropriate only for well-trained climbers who climb 5.13/8a/V9 or above. Below these grades, start with the two-handed 7/53 protocol: 3 to 5 sets of 3 hangs of 7 seconds with exactly 53 seconds of rest, twice per week, to build base strength safely.

## Which protocol to use

| Level | Protocol |
|---|---|
| Well-trained, climbing 5.13/8a/V9 or above | One-handed protocol (a very difficult method) |
| Not yet at these grades | Two-handed 7/53 protocol first (an intermediate-to-advanced protocol that uses two-handed hangs to build base strength safely) |

## One-handed protocol

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Warm-up | Thorough, progressive warm-up of 20 to 30 minutes, with a few bodyweight pull-ups, large-hold hangs and a few minutes of generalized cardio |
| Hang | On a 20 to 24 mm edge, a 5-second one-handed hang, then switch sides and do a 5-second hang with the other hand |
| Rest | 3 to 5 minutes before the next set of one-handed hangs with each hand |
| Volume | Continue for up to 5 or 6 hangs with each hand |
| Frequency | An intense max-strength protocol: limit it to 2 days per week |

- Grips: train both the half crimp and the open crimp. Especially strong climbers can also do a hang or two with the three-finger drag and the two-finger pocket.
- **Warning:** Be very careful and listen to your body. Do not flirt with injury.

### How to progress

| Stage | What to do |
|---|---|
| New to one-handed hangboarding | Hold a helper sling or counterweight system with your free hand and pull just enough weight with it to grip the hangboard hold successfully for 5 seconds |
| Can do all 5 one-handed hangs at bodyweight (no sling or counterweight) | Make it harder by holding a weight in the free hand: start with 0.9 to 2.3 kg |
| Long term | Add weight progressively over months and years as your maximum finger strength increases |

## Two-handed 7/53 protocol

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Warm-up | Thorough, progressive warm-up of 20 to 30 minutes, with a few bodyweight pull-ups, large-hold hangs, dynamic stretching and a few minutes of generalized cardio |
| Training weight | The weight you can add and still successfully hold a single 10-second hang on a hold of about 20 mm |
| One set | 3 hangs of 7 seconds with the training weight, with exactly 53 seconds of rest between hangs |
| Rest between sets | 3 to 5 minutes |
| Sets | 3 to 5 in total |
| Frequency | Twice per week, ideally on a gym bouldering or strength/power training day |

- Load check: if the training weight is right, the last set should be a struggle to complete. If it isn't, add 2.3 or 4.5 kg to the training weight for the next workout.
- Followed twice per week, it raises finger strength in a few short weeks.
- **Warning:** Don't use this protocol during a period of performance climbing (outdoor climbing or competition): your nervous system may not be fully recovered to climb your very best.
