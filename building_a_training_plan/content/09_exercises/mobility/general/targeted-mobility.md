# Targeted Mobility

**Type:** Exercise/protocol, Principles · **Trains:** Mobility

**TL;DR:** Assist gravity with furniture sliders and weights so you can load stretches and train the antagonist muscles with reps instead of static holds. When that stops working, use yoga bolsters to stay comfortably in your deepest position for long holds, so the brain learns it is safe. Track your range with yoga blocks or books, the way you track lifting.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Very flexible | Use bolsters to add hyperextension |

## Assist gravity: sliders and weights
- In most stretches gravity is the main force lengthening the muscles, and this works better for some stretches than others. Use furniture sliders and weights to increase the load.
- To gain new range, the brain needs to know you are strong enough to be safe in it, so use muscular tension to get into the stretch whenever possible, rather than a purely passive approach.
- Cheap substitutes: full water bottles instead of weights, paper plates instead of furniture sliders.

### Frog stretch with sliders
- The frog stretch (used to open the hips) has two problems: friction between the legs and the floor often makes it hard to get deep enough to overload the position, and the same friction makes it impossible to train the antagonist muscles by lifting out.
- Fix: put the largest furniture sliders you can get under your knees. You slide easily into your deepest position and likely find a bit more range. With minimal resistance, the abductors can pull you into a deeper stretch assisted by gravity, and the adductors are trained by pulling you inwards. You now do reps instead of static holds.

| Step | Sets × reps | Hold at the bottom |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | A few reps, up to near-max range | — |
| Work sets | 3 × 8 | 3-10 second count |

- This tracks volume and makes sure you spend enough time to gain comfort in the new range.

### Butterfly stretch with weights
- Place a 4.5 kg weight on each knee. If you are stiff, this alone will likely do nothing, but paired with hip strength it lets you pull your knees to the floor much more easily than unassisted.
- Target: get your knees to the floor with just the strength of your hips. If you can't, add the minimum weight needed to get there.

## Get comfortable: yoga bolsters for long holds
- Neurological limiters are one of the main things that stop you from getting deeper: they resist going further into a range to protect you. When the methods above stop working, you need to tell the brain to relax and that you are safe. This often requires really long holds at the deepest position, which are uncomfortable and awkward to maintain.
- Yoga bolsters (long, narrow cushions that prop the body off the floor) support your body in its lowest positions so you can stay there comfortably for really long durations. They stack easily and support the body in strenuous stretching positions.
- Pillows can replace bolsters: a bit too soft and they don't stack well, but free.

### Pigeon pose with bolsters
- Most people are more limited by the back leg, so they compromise the position of the front leg and neither leg gets much out of the stretch.

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Setup | One or two bolsters under the hips, parallel to the front leg, so the hips stay square and upright |
| Hold | Relax into the position, with occasional isometric contractions of the antagonist muscles to lift and then lower deeper |
| Fold | Fold forward for several breaths, then lift back up for several breaths; the cushion lets you sink deeper as the muscles release, and you can stay a long time |
| 2nd set | Switch sides; use smaller bolsters (they come in many sizes) |

### Side splits with bolsters and sliders

| Stage | Bolsters |
|---|---|
| Start | Two large bolsters feel maximal |
| Later | Two to warm up, then reduce to a single small one |

### In the deepest position
- Keep a relaxed breath and periodically use muscle contractions to lift and then release deeper.
- The goal is to explore your total range safely and comfortably; what feels scary at the start becomes familiar towards the end.

## Track your range: yoga blocks or books
- Track mobility the way you track lifting (sets, reps, loads, notes on whether to increase the load or change the variation) instead of judging it by whether it "felt" like a stretch. Measuring makes the training more interesting.
- A stack of books works instead of yoga blocks: any way of measuring distance that is easily repeatable and scalable. Tracking helps motivation dramatically.

### Forward fold example
- Touching your toes is a good first goal for many people, but without a way to monitor it, it is easy to feel you are never progressing.

| Stage | Target |
|---|---|
| Before the toes | Yoga block between your feet: three height progressions to reach |
| Toes | Touch your toes |
| Beyond the toes | Stack blocks and try to touch them with your head |

### How to progress each target

| Step | What you can do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Find a distance you just barely cannot reach |
| 2 | Touch it with a bit of momentum |
| 3 | Touch it and hold it |
| 4 | It becomes part of your warm-up |
