# Hip and Shoulder Mobility Drills

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Mobility

**TL;DR:** The main hip drills are the frog drill (rocking slowly for 45–60 seconds), the tug of war squat and the prying cobra; the main shoulder drills are the arm bar with a light kettlebell (30–60 seconds), rhomboid and lat foam rolls, and the overhead squat with a pipe or dowel. Dozens of other exercises work too: if a different one works best for you, use it.

## Where to start
- Shoulders and hips are where adults lose mobility. Test them first with the frog stretch and the behind-the-back fist reach (in the note Hip and Shoulder Mobility Tests: Frog Stretch and Fist Reach), then train them with these drills.

## Hip mobility drills

- Three main hip drills make up the bulk of hip mobility training, but there are dozens of good exercises: if a different one works best for you, use it.
- Frog drill: a hip opener that helps squat form and turn-out on the rock. Prone, knees turned out to the sides on a mat (most knees can't handle it on a hard floor); feet turned out; elbows on the ground directly below the shoulders, most weight on the arms. Rock the hips back toward the heels, then slowly up toward the elbows; at the top try to move the knees out slightly; repeat. Keep moving slowly for 45-60 seconds, trying each time to relax into the bottom position.
- Tug of war squat (a standing frog stretch): hold a solid object such as a power-rack upright or a door frame and squat to below parallel. At the bottom, drive the heels into the floor, keep the torso upright and gently rock back and forth to relax into the hip stretch. Use it to practise the perfect squat bottom position.
- Prying cobra (hip flexor stretch): lie prone, hands palms down beside the chest at the level of the bottom of the ribcage, legs straight, tops of the feet on the floor. Tighten the abs and press up until the arms are straight; keeping the abs tight prevents excessive lumbar extension and puts the stretch into the hip flexors. To increase it, gently rock right and left.

## Shoulder mobility drills

- Often a climber's greatest opportunity for advancement is better shoulder function, whether strength, stability or mobility; very few climbers don't need some work. Three main exercises.
- Arm bar: a big shoulder mobility and stability exercise, excellent for thoracic mobility and shoulder-girdle stability. Take a light kettlebell and set up as for a get-up (bell at full extension in the right hand, right knee bent, foot on the floor). Stretch the left arm overhead; using the left arm and leg as an axis, bring the right knee to the chest and roll to the left, chest toward the ground, head resting on the left biceps or the ground. Straighten the right leg and try to push the right hip toward the ground. Creep the left hand "back" along the floor and keep both shoulders pulled down away from the ears. Hold or pulse 30-60 seconds.
- Rhomboid foam roll (between the shoulder blades): lie across a foam roller placed just below the shoulder blades, hips off the ground, head supported by the hands; move slowly back and forth along the length of the shoulder blade. Rolling range about 15 cm: too high stresses the neck, too low stresses the lower back. Alternative: hold a good stretch in various positions to open up the front of the shoulder.
- Lat foam roll: lie across the roller with it just below the armpit, hips off the ground, positioned so the roller presses the lat; move slowly back and forth. Be careful where you cross the outer edge of the scapula, which can be very painful. Spend plenty of time on each side.
- Overhead squat with a PVC pipe or dowel: hold it overhead with locked elbows and lower into a full-depth squat; as the torso leans forward, the overhead position stretches the pecs and lats. Keep the arms aimed straight at the ceiling and set the hands as wide as needed to keep the elbows straight.
