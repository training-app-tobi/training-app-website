# Positioning Drills: Rest Anywhere, Diagonal and Banded Climber

**Type:** Exercise/protocol, Technique · **Trains:** Technique, Antagonists & core

**TL;DR:** Three positioning drills. Rest anywhere: traverse on open holds and settle into whatever position you are in when a timer or a partner calls stop, 15–30 s each time, in 3–5-minute sets, 3–6 sets. Diagonal: a cross-body tension hold on two points of contact on a 35–40° wall, about 5 s each at straight arm, 90° lock-off and full lock-off, both sides, 3–5 sets. Banded climber: traverse while a partner pulls your hips back with a band, 5–15 moves per set, 3–5 sets with 3–5 minutes rest.

## How to use them
- Do them within the 20–30 minutes of skill work in a bouldering or rope-climbing session, and regress or progress each drill as described in the note Drill Work for Technical Skills.

## Rest-anywhere drill
- A positioning drill to become comfortable in any resting position (e.g. for competition) and to recover there.
- It does build endurance and recovery, but it is better at building comfort in challenging positions and reducing anxiety on the wall. It mixes movement work and positioning.
- Transfer: outside you are not always given a big jug or big footholds to rest on, and good clipping positions are often hard to find.

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Movement | Traverse left, right, up and down (e.g. on a spray wall) on open holds, in no set pattern |
| Stop signal, alone | Set a timer to go off every 15, 20 or 30 seconds |
| Stop signal, with a partner (more variability) | The partner watches and calls stop at an undisclosed time |
| Rest position | Whatever hand holds and footholds you are on: hold it 15–30 seconds, settling in (find a good hip position, stand on your feet, take some weight off your hands), then continue climbing |
| Set | 3–5 minutes of this |
| Rest between sets | At least double the climbing time; with a partner, they do their set while you help |
| Sets per session | 3–6 |

## Diagonal drill
- A cross-body tension drill (positioning), typically on a steep wall; regress to slightly overhanging terrain.
- Develops strength from toes to fingers and tension across the body in challenging positions, and teaches that you can be strong and move well on two points of contact instead of always keeping all four on the wall.

| Setup | Detail |
|---|---|
| Type | Static drill, nothing moves except the working arm |
| Wall | About 35–40°, big holds |
| Hold spacing | Foothold and handhold about the distance between your elbow and your knee apart (varies with body size and strength) |
| Example | Left foot on a fairly large edge at about knee height, right hand on an open hold or jug at about shoulder height, creating a line between right hand and left foot |

| Step | Position | Hold |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pull onto the wall on those two points of contact with a straight, locked-out arm and a strong, packed shoulder | About 5 seconds |
| 2 | Pull in to a 90° lock-off at the working elbow | About 5 seconds |
| 3 | Pull in to a full lock-off against the wall | About 5 seconds |
| 4 | Come off, rest 30 seconds to 1 minute, repeat on the opposite side (right foot, left hand) = 1 set | — |
| Between sets | Rest 3–5 minutes | — |
| Per session | 3–5 sets | — |

| Regression (if the time on the wall is too hard) | How |
|---|---|
| Shorter holds | Hold each position about 3 seconds |
| Rest between positions | Come off the wall between the straight-arm, 90° lock-off and full lock-off positions and rest |
| Easier angle | Reduce the wall angle |

## Banded climber
- A positioning drill on hip position, and the consequences of not keeping the hips close to the wall and over the feet.
- It makes you use the big prime movers (glutes, hamstrings, quads, lower body) and core instead of just pulling with your arms; push the work into your midsection and lower body to keep hips over feet and as close to the wall as possible.
- It fatigues you quite fast.

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Partner | Needed; alternate (one pulls the band, and rests, while the other climbs) |
| Location | Best low to the ground where you can traverse a long way back and forth |
| Terrain | Vertical or overhanging depending on desired difficulty; start on vertical to learn the drill |
| Execution | Climber wears an elastic band around the hips; the partner stands behind and gently pulls back, drawing the hips away from the wall; the climber pulls the hips back in while moving across the wall |
| Moves per set | Start with 5–15 |
| Rest between sets | 3–5 minutes |
| Sets per session | 3–5 |
