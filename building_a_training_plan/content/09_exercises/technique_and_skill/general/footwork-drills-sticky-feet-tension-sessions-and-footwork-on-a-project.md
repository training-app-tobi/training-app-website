# Footwork Drills: Sticky Feet, Tension Sessions and Footwork on a Project

**Type:** Exercise/protocol, Technique · **Trains:** Technique, Antagonists & core · **For:** footwork as a limiting factor

**TL;DR:** Place each foot once and don't readjust it (sticky feet), place it as quietly as possible (silent feet), and on a really steep wall keep your feet on through every move (tension session). Start in the warm-up on easy climbs, then build the drills into almost every session and, bit by bit, into your projecting.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Footwork is a limiting factor | Build the drills into your project climbing sessions: at the end of the warm-up or when trying really hard boulders, implement each technique individually and see whether it makes a difference to your climbing |

## Drills
| Drill | How and progression |
|---|---|
| Silent feet | Instead of making lots of noise with your feet (hitting the back of the wall, adjusting on the footholds, tapping the footholds each time), place each foot as quietly as possible. Really good climbers do this automatically. Do a couple of climbs this way, then make the climbs harder and harder: do it in the warm-up, then continue it as you start training |
| Sticky feet | Place your foot on the foothold once: you can twist it, but you can't readjust, move it around or slide it. Rather than looking down, placing the foot and looking away, focus on making the most of each individual foothold. Start in the warm-up on a couple of traverses or easy climbs, then keep the same principle on harder problems in training |
| Tension session | On a really steep section of wall (bouldering wall or board, as long as it is really steep), keep your feet on, with no cutting loose, even on a really big move. Get your foot placed on the next hold before doing the next move |

- Climbers who spend a lot of time on indoor walls increasingly tend to jump to holds and cut off the wall all the time. The tension session trains the core as well as footwork, and it maximises your ability to take weight off your hands.
- Once you have practised the drills in the warm-up, build them into almost every session.

## On a project
| Section of the climb | Focus |
|---|---|
| Easier sections | Place your feet really quietly |
| Hard sections | Focus on the exact part of the foothold you are placing your foot on |

- Take the individual moves of the climb into account.
- Keep tension rather than cutting loose or jumping automatically: you may not need to, and it might save energy.
- Build the drills into your projecting sessions bit by bit; they become your normal technique, so your footwork improves long term.
