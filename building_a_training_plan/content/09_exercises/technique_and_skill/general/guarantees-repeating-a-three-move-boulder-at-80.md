# Guarantees: Repeating a Three-Move Boulder at 80%

**Type:** Exercise/protocol, Technique · **Trains:** Technique, Power

**TL;DR:** Set a three-move boulder at about 80% of your maximal physical and technical abilities and repeat it, aiming to perfect the movements. The first attempt should be a little wobbly but doable and feel at your limit, and every attempt after should be better. If it feels about 90%, it is too hard: bring it down a notch.

## What the drill is
- Guarantees keep the best part of limit bouldering (operating at maximal intensity, sticking moves, deciphering and mastering complex movements) but with successful execution: they remove the filler moves and the odd-chance nature of sticking a limit move.

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Boulder | Three moves |
| Difficulty | About 80% of your maximal physical and technical abilities |
| How | Repeat it, aiming to perfect the movements |

## Why it works
- At first the boulder demands absolute peak, everything you have; then each attempt you get better at the movements.
- This builds muscle memory and confidence, and fine-tunes focus on the essential aspects that make the movement highly repeatable and much easier.
- It is a time to operate at peak and learn to make that a habit.

## What to focus on during attempts
Focus on every fine-tuned element of body movement you need to get perfect, e.g.:
- the toe digging into the foothold
- the ankle twisting and pulling toward the midline
- the body rotating the correct way
- perfecting the dead point
- repeating a positive affirmation or mantra in your head to build confidence and keep focus on the right things

## Setting the right difficulty
| Level | What it looks like | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Right level | You see progress on every attempt. The first attempt is a little wobbly but doable and feels at your limit | Over and over, learn the moves, refine them and get better and better results |
| Too hard (feels about 90%) | It won't allow the high-quality laps you want | Bring it down a notch. You can always bring it back up |

## Example: what improved over the attempts
- Each attempt felt better, with more and more control; paying attention to how much, and when, to dig with the legs gave better momentum out to the last hold (a pinch).
- While locking into a drop knee, releasing tension a bit to create space during the foot match gave a better hip trajectory, so the hips came in and around to catch the pinch with a better dead point.
- Paying attention to the small details gives a lot of progress in learning and refining the movements in only three attempts.
