# Hip Positioning Drill: The Same Move with an Open and a Closed Hip

**Type:** Exercise/protocol, Technique · **Trains:** Technique

**TL;DR:** Do a hard move two different ways, keeping the foothold in the same place and doing the same move: once with the hip opening, once with the hip closing. Don't give up on a position that feels wrong; manipulate the flagging leg, the arms and the rotation to make it work. Open or close the hip dynamically during the move, starting mild and twisting deep as you reach, to buy more time for the deadpoint and land closer to the wall.

## The drill
Keep the foothold in the same place and do the same move twice.

| Attempt | Hip | Knee |
|---|---|---|
| First time | Hip opening | Take the knee and move it away from the midline |
| Second time | Hip closing | Take the knee and move it inwards towards the midline |

- Some of these moves will feel very foreign, awkward and like they don't work; that's okay and part of the drill. It's up to you to figure out how to make the position feel more natural.
- Sometimes you will realise that the position won't work with an open hip, or won't work with a closed hip; that is also part of the drill.

## Challenge yourself every time
- Don't just give up on one of the hip positions if it feels wrong; try to manipulate one of the aspects below.
- All of these teach you more about these positions, which are always present in your climbing.

| Aspect | Pay attention to |
|---|---|
| Flagging leg | Your flagging leg |
| Arms | Where you move your body with your arms |
| Rotation | The dynamic rotation of your body and how much you close: a full drop knee, or just a slight twist inwards with the knee |

## Opening or closing the hip dynamically
- Open or close the hip dynamically during the move to buy more time for the deadpoint and to place yourself more directly over the foothold when you catch the hand.
- Don't open or close the hip completely beforehand. Do a mild opening or a mild closing first, to prepare for a deep twist as you reach, closing the space between yourself and the wall.
- You land closer to the wall when you hit the hand, moving in towards the target instead of falling out at the end of the movement.
- With a slightly wider stance, make it a more subtle twist or a more subtle opening: you don't have much wiggle room and can't come as far back, because you are already so extended.
