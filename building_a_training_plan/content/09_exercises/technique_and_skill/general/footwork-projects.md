# Footwork Projects: Forced-Footwork Boulders for Lower-Body Coordination

**Type:** Exercise/protocol, Technique · **Trains:** Technique

**TL;DR:** Build a short boulder (4–6 moves) that forces two or more hard footwork techniques, and project it over several sessions until the footwork feels easy. Rest 30–60 seconds per move executed, work one boulder per session with a maximum of six tries, and count it as sent only after five smooth, controlled repeats. Choose moves that are achievable but challenging, not next level.

## What it trains
- High-level lower-body coordination and tension management in climbing, especially in situations that demand precise, intentional and powerful use of the feet and legs.
- It isolates and intensifies complex footwork mechanics that are often neglected or glossed over in typical sessions, because these moves are scarce in commercial setting and on system boards.
- It doesn't train general movement or raw strength. It targets:
  - neuromuscular control of the lower body
  - foot-specific strength and proprioception
  - acquiring complex movement patterns
  - visual-motor planning before touching the wall

## Building the footwork project
- Take time to create these boulders in your first session. Pay particular attention to feeling the moves without getting on the wall. With attention and patience in this part, the drill trains you to activate, command and succeed with your feet and legs just as you do with your arms and hands.
- Find or create a boulder that features two or more of these footwork techniques:
  - hard heel hooks
  - hand-heel hooks
  - foot compression
  - a hard foot switch or foot match
  - hard toe hooks
  - drop-knee toe hooks
  - inverted heel hooks
  - deep drop knees
  - narrow drop knees
  - very intense toeing into holds that are angled away from you
- At first, each move should feel awkward and hard to execute.
- Ideally the holds force these movements; if they don't, force yourself to do them using the harder foot beta you are trying to learn.
- The best way to do this is to turn the climb into a numbered circuit: e.g. stay in the same 1.8 m of wall but use 12 different holds at different times to execute six unique moves, with designated hands or feet used only for that specific move.
- Example: match hands on a side pull while in a foot clamp before reaching to the gaston. On a normal boulder you would skip the match and bump without the clamp; here you force yourself to move to the next foothold so you train a different type of foot skill.
- Directional handholds such as non-incut side pulls, underclings and gastons are very effective for forcing more reliance on the legs and forcing these moves.
- If you are newer to making up your own climbs: in your first session make the moves easy and achievable, and get successful execution and feedback before making them harder.
- It's okay to find it hard to make up forced moves or even good boulders for this drill: half of its benefit is learning to identify these types of moves and feel them before getting on them. You get better at this over time.

## Protocol
| Element | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Boulder length | 4–6 moves |
| Rest | 30–60 seconds per move executed on the wall |
| Per session | One boulder, maximum of six tries in total |
| Learning a move | Ideally 1–2 sessions |
| Doing a move five times | Ideally 2–4 sessions |
| Sent | Once you have done the boulder, repeat it five times; only then is it considered sent |

- The goal is, over several sessions, to be able to do the boulder with the footwork techniques feeling easy.
- Smooth and controlled is the aim. Turn the focus up.
- Make sure you can always do the first few moves, so that you get moves in during the session and keep flowing.
- Moves should be achievable but challenging, not next level, so you don't spend too many sessions on a move too far outside your learning zone.

## Warm-up
- Before the drill, warm up the legs with easier versions of the moves on your project, to save energy and make the most of your limited attempts.
- Example: choose 2–3 different moves and do each 3 times, until the muscles feel warmer and stronger and the moves feel easier.
