# Heel and Toe Hooks: Targeted Skill Drill

**Type:** Exercise/protocol, Technique · **Trains:** Technique

**TL;DR:** Create or select problems that force heel or toe hooks or provide heel or toe hook opportunities, and execute five of each per leg successfully, with a maximum of three attempts on a failed hook. Every placement and movement should be controlled and deliberate: quality over quantity. Rest until fully recovered between boulders, and make the hooks harder as the weeks go on.

## What the drill trains
- Goal of the drill: build technical proficiency, body awareness, and strength and power in your toe hooks and heel hooks.
- By repeating difficult heel and toe hooks and identifying them on the climbing wall, you improve your technical skills through repetition and by ascending the learning curve. In the end you are able to see new beta and understand why those moves work in the projects you are working on.

## How to execute
- Quality over quantity: every single placement and movement of your body should be controlled and deliberate.
- Start out slow and fight the urge to rush. The drill is for doing it right and feeling fully every muscle, joint and aspect of your positioning and execution.

## Warm-up
- Before the drill, warm up your calves and hamstrings with a few easier heel hooks and toe hooks before the more challenging ones.

## Protocol
| Element | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Problems | Create or select problems that either force heel or toe hooks or provide heel or toe hook opportunities |
| Volume | Execute five heel hooks and five toe hooks per leg successfully |
| Failed hook | If you fall on a move because the heel or toe hook came off or wasn't secure enough to finish the movement, try it again for a maximum of three attempts, then move on to your next boulder |
| Hook difficulty | From mildly challenging to very difficult for you to execute |
| Rest | As long as you need between boulders to feel fully recovered before the next problem |
| Problem length | You don't need to make full boulders: you can do just one or two moves at a time to get the most ideal heel or toe hook for you |
| Boulder grade | From one grade below your on-site grade up to about one grade below your project grade |
| Progression | As the weeks go on, make the heel and toe hooks harder and harder to ensure the skill keeps progressing |

## Move design
- The moves don't have to be forced.
- However, always try to create a move that requires you to set up the heel or toe hook on the same side of the body as the arm that is going to reach for the next hold.

## Technique cues
- Heel hooks: the hooks should force you to assume a turned-out hip and pointed-toe position.
- Toe hooks: place the foot on the hold, fully flex the foot into dorsiflexion, and keep the hip as high as possible to create the best oppositional force into the hold.
- **Warning:** A common mistake is passively keeping a straight leg for all toe hooks. Occasionally, and more often than you think, bending the leg is key for many toe hooks, as are using open and closed hip positions and turning the foot closed or open.
