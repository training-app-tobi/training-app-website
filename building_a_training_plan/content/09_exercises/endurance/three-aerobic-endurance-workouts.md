# Three Aerobic Endurance Workouts: Extensive Combos, Boulders on the Clock and Route 4x4s

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Endurance · **For:** pumps out on long routes, injured

**TL;DR:** Three low-intensity workouts for specific aerobic endurance. Extensive endurance combos: about 3 minutes of easy continuous climbing alternated with 3 minutes of easy cardio, about 40 minutes, adding a few minutes each week. Boulders on the clock: an easy boulder at the start of every minute (or 90 s), two 20-minute blocks, adding a couple of intervals each week. Route 4x4s: each route 4 times in a row, 4 groups of 4, starting at the easiest grade you can. Stay in the aerobic zone and progress slowly: the adaptations take 6–8 weeks to notice.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| In an aerobic development phase | 2–3 extensive endurance combo sessions per week |
| Just maintaining aerobic fitness | Maybe once a week is fine |

## How hard to go
- Keep every workout in the aerobic zone: no labored breathing (you could hold a conversation), no pump, and still something in the tank at the end. Why, and the markers in detail, are in the note Specific Aerobic Endurance: Train Utilization, Not the Pump.

## Session 1: extensive endurance combos
Extensive endurance is low-intensity climbing that can be done at a continuous pace, combined with low-intensity cardio to save the skin and make the sessions longer.

| Step | Duration | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Climb | About 3 min | Continuous open climbing on pretty good holds; low intensity, no pump |
| Rest/transition | About 90 s | Take off climbing shoes, put on sneakers |
| Cardio | 3 min | Easy cardio, preferably a machine using arms and legs (dual-action air bike, rowing machine, ski erg) |
| Transition | About 90 s | Back to climbing |
| Repeat | | Alternate back and forth; no pump; finish feeling you could have done much more |

- Start at 3 min instead of 30 min of traversing or laps on an auto-belay: long continuous climbing tends to wear out the skin and the feet, unless you already have that capacity.
- If injured: increase the cardio time and keep the climbing really low intensity and low volume.
- Sessions of about 40 min are probably great for full aerobic development.

| How to progress | |
|---|---|
| Each week | Add a few minutes, up to 5, 6 and 7 min of each activity |
| Variations | The intervals can be varied; many climbers increase the climbing time and hold the cardio intervals steady |

## Session 2: boulders on the clock
- Pick nice easy boulders and climb one at the start of every minute (or every 90 s), recovering for the rest of the interval, then repeat.
- The boulder should take less than about 30 s, or less than about half the interval: the interval should be about double the climbing time.

| Boulder takes you | Interval |
|---|---|
| 40–45 s (long boulders or slower climbers) | 90 s |
| 30 s (fast climbers, short boulders) | On the minute |

| Step | Duration |
|---|---|
| Warm-up | |
| Bouldering intervals | 20 min |
| Break | About 10 min (around half the climbing time) |
| Bouldering intervals | 20 min |
| Whole workout after the warm-up | About 50 min |

- Intensity check: the second 20 min shouldn't feel much harder than the first 20 min.
- Slower climbers on 90 s or 75 s intervals: reduce the number of climbs and stay within about the 20 min block for the first few workouts.

| How to progress | |
|---|---|
| Each week | Progress very slowly: add a couple of intervals every week for the whole endurance training phase; eventually you will be doing a lot of climbing |

- Why it takes time: the adaptations (increased mitochondrial density, increased capillary density, increased ability to move energy to the muscles) take a really long time. You won't feel it in 2, 3 or 4 weeks; it takes 6–8 weeks before you notice you can do a lot of climbing without getting as fatigued.

## Session 3: route 4x4s
- Route 4x4s are very different from boulder 4x4s: the aim is to manage fatigue rather than seek it out.
- Grade: a 5.12+ or 5.13- climber might start at around 5.9 or 5.10. It may feel silly, but it is like long slow distance in cycling or running: back off the intensity and let the muscles do a lot of activity to develop the ability to deliver fuel under fatigue.

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 route | Lead it, lower down, top-rope it 3 more times (the route 4 times in a row, very brief rest between) |
| Partner | Switch and let the partner do the same |
| Session | 4 groups of 4 routes = 16 pitches, in the gym or at the local crag |

- **Warning:** 16 pitches is a huge day of climbing, so the intensity must be really low. If you are getting pumped or fatigued, holding your breath, or can't hold a conversation, it is too hard. Start at the easiest grade you possibly can.

| How to progress | |
|---|---|
| Grade | Advance the grades of the routes slightly, only as long as the proper intensity is maintained |
| Volume | Don't add pitches and don't add routes to each set: stay at 4 groups of 4 |
| Timeline | Slowly increase the intensity over several months as aerobic fitness develops |
