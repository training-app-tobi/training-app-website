# Aerobic Workouts for Short Walls: Boulders on the Minute and Hangboard Rowing

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Endurance, Recovery

**TL;DR:** Two ways to train the aerobic system without long walls. Boulders on the minute on a short board, system board or home wall: boulders of 10–15 s stay alactic and the rest of each minute is aerobic; progress slowly, e.g. from 10 V2s to 10 V3s in a 10-minute set after a month. Hangboard rowing, for when skin, time or terrain limit aerobic climbing: a portable hangboard on a rowing machine, half crimp on a 20 mm edge, for 5 minutes or up to 20 minutes continuously, giving local forearm and whole-body adaptations.

## Boulders on the minute on short walls
- Start a short boulder at the top of each minute and rest for the remainder of it.
- Works on short boards, system boards, home walls and cellar boards.
- Boulders of 10–15 s stay alactic and never go glycolytic: no build-up of the byproducts that cause fatigue and lengthen recovery. The rest of each minute is aerobic: heart rate rises and alactic ATP is replenished.
- Measure progress as V-grade and number of boulders in a set time, e.g. 10 V2s in a 10-minute set, then after a month maybe 10 V3s in 10 minutes. Progress is slow.

## Hangboard rowing
For when skin, time or terrain (very bouldery or sharp rock) limit aerobic training: low-level aerobic training of the fingers and whole body, skin-friendly and time-efficient.

| Setup | Grip | Sets |
|---|---|---|
| Rowing machine with a portable hangboard attached | Half crimp on a 20 mm edge | 5 min, or maybe continuous 20 min; many ways to program it |

| Method | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| ARCing with a portable grip device or hangboard (e.g. in the car) | Local aerobic system of the forearms only; very useful |
| Hangboard rowing | Local forearm plus systemic, whole-body adaptations with raised heart rate: much more specific to climbing and a better use of time |
