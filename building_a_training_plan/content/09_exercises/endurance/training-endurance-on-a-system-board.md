# Training Endurance on a System Board

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Endurance, Power endurance, Recovery · **For:** only a board available

**TL;DR:** A board can be used for the rest of your energy-systems training, not only strength and power. Lower its intensity by keeping your feet on the ground and moving your hands around as if going around a circuit, adjust it with foot and hip position, and choose hold types that match your goal. For aerobic capacity use long easy intervals or 1 minute on, 1 minute off × 10; for aerobic power use broken 40s (10-move sequence, 15 seconds rest, 4 times in total).

## Why use a board for more than strength and power
- Training boards are a great tool to improve strength and power, whether you boulder, climb routes or climb trad: finger strength, arm strength and body tension.
- If a board is all you have for training (e.g. a home board, or your local facility is particularly steep), use it for the rest of your energy-systems training as well.
- The main issue with using a board for more than strength and power is that it is pretty intense, because of the size of the holds and the angle of the board.

## Lowering the intensity: feet on the ground
- The easiest way to lower the intensity: keep your feet on the ground and move your hands around as if going around a circuit. It looks a bit funny and feels like you're not doing much, but it gives a really good stimulus.

| Adjustment | How |
|---|---|
| Easier | Move the feet slightly further out or bring the hips in, so more weight is on the feet |
| Harder | Bring the feet further in and the hips further down |

- Once the intensity is low enough to stay on the board long enough for other energy systems, change the intensity or make the training more specific by using different hold types, so a good variety of holds on the board helps.
- If your goal is particularly crimpy, do the energy-systems training on small crimps with the feet on the ground: you train the specific grip type and, by staying on for the right amount of time, the energy system.

## Aerobic capacity
- Aerobic capacity training on a board (which seems counter-intuitive) is endurance-style training that helps recovery and base aerobic conditioning.

| Session | How |
|---|---|
| Long intervals | Climb around for 5, 8, 10 minutes or even more, nice and easy: really low forearm pump, just a regular amount of blood flow, intensity low enough to stay on for a long time |
| Interval style: 1 minute on, 1 minute off | Feet on the ground, 1 minute of circuits moving the hands around different holds, step back for 1 minute of rest, repeat 10 times |

- Long intervals make you much better at recovering on rests and performing on the easier sections of climbs, and build the base for other training.
- In the interval style, the intensity of each minute is quite high compared to the long intervals, but still generally aerobic: pump builds up through the session, but no single minute is too hard. The minute of rest teaches the forearms to recover, remove waste products and re-oxygenate the muscles.
- With the feet far out, quite small holds can be used for this session: a good grip position for the training stimulus while the intensity stays low enough for minute on, minute off.
- Aerobic capacity training in this style is very useful for getting good at specific grip types: e.g. if you are training for somewhere particularly pockety, it adapts the fingers to pockets while the intensity stays quite low. With the feet on the floor and spread out, you can use the holds slowly and in control.

## Aerobic power
- Aerobic power is the top end of aerobic performance: something really sustained, such as sport climbing where it gets really pumpy in the forearms. Aim for a high level of forearm pump: not so hard that you fall off early, not so easy that you never feel you might fail.

| Session | How |
|---|---|
| Broken 40s | Feet-on-the-ground circuit: choose a 10-move sequence and complete it (quite intense for those 10 moves), rest 15 seconds, repeat to 4 times in total = one set |

- Broken 40s work even on a really short, steep board at home or in a facility.
- Tracking progress: because the intensity has to stay quite high and you need to know whether you are progressing, note which holds the circuit uses so you can slowly make it harder across sessions, and note where your feet are on the floor, since that also dictates the difficulty. In the aerobic capacity sessions you can just move around more freely, because the intensity is low.
