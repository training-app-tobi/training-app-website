# Endurance Workouts: Add-On Laps, Short Circuits, Two-Problem Links and Long Intervals

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Endurance, Power endurance, Power

**TL;DR:** Four endurance workouts. Add-on easy laps: finish a normal day with two super-easy laps and raise them a grade when all goes well. Short intervals: a circuit of 4–6 exercises of 5–10 s each on a rolling 30 s clock, six circuits in under 20 minutes. Two-problem links: 4–8 sets of two linked problems around your onsight grade, 60–90 s per set with at least a 1:5 work:rest ratio. Long intervals: relaxed, skilled climbing for at least 5 minutes nonstop, on a base of about 20 pitches a week.

## How they fit
- The short, medium and long workouts match the three intensities of endurance session (work under 10 s, 10–40 s, and up to about 10 minutes) described in the note Fundamentals of Endurance. Add-on easy laps build capacity on top of a normal climbing day.

## Add-on easy laps (try for a few weeks)

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Normal day | Do a normal gym or crag day. Example normal crag day: two warm-ups, a lead-in route, two hard burns and a final easier pitch (10c, 10c, 11d, 12b, 12b, 11c) |
| Add-on | Finish with two super-easy laps beyond what you'd normally do, e.g. a couple of laps of an 8 or 9 |
| Next session | If all went well, raise them by one grade (e.g. to 10a) |
| Over a few weeks | Keep trying harder climbing, but never to the point of tapping out. If the laps get hard, be patient |

- Holding on to easy climbing builds capacity without getting trashed by the session. The true goal is more great days out, not more days trashed from the last session.

## Short intervals

- Aim: build the body's ability to use the alactic (creatine phosphate) system more efficiently (all energy systems are in use all the time). Short bouts improve the ability to generate power again sooner for later sets and give a chance to build strength and power.
- Session: 5–10 s of work for 4–6 exercises in a short circuit, moving between general fitness and specific climbing movements to avoid too much fatigue in one muscle group or pattern. Example circuit on a rolling 30 s clock (do the reps, rest the remainder of the 30 s):

| Station | Work |
|---|---|
| 1 | 3 pull-ups |
| 2 | 5 kettlebell swings |
| 3 | 1 lap up the campus board |
| 4 | 3 clapping push-ups |
| 5 | 10 s hang on the hangboard |
| After the circuit | One circuit takes 2.5 min; rest about 30 s, repeat |
| Total | Six circuits in under 20 min |

- Loaded heavy, it is a strength and power workout as well as improving high-threshold endurance.
- This session is mentally simpler than going to exhaustion on a circuit, so place it in your schedule when you're not quite feeling it.

## Medium intervals: two-problem links

- The two-problem link is the king of power endurance sessions. Use it to increase power endurance for a specific route or trip, and be specific with hold type, angle and difficulty of the problems.

| Step | Protocol |
|---|---|
| Warm-up | A good bouldering warm-up that ends with longer problems or shortened rests, to get a slight endurance pump |
| Rest | 4–6 min |
| Sets | 4–8 sets of two linked problems around your onsight grade or slightly easier |
| One set | Climb problem 1 to its end, downclimb on open holds without resting or taking much time, traverse near the bottom to the start of problem 2 (roughly the same difficulty), climb it to the top. This is preferred over jumping down and walking to the next problem. A set takes 60–90 s |
| Rest between sets | Start with at least a 1:5 work:rest ratio (90 s set → rest about 8 min) |
| Session length | 35–75 min after the warm-up |

| Result | Next step |
|---|---|
| Solid performance across all sets | Increase difficulty next session |
| Performance tapers off | Add a couple of minutes of rest between sets |

- **Warning:** these sessions feel right but play out fairly quickly. Doggedly pursuing pumpy session after pumpy session leaves you tired and sore but doesn't move the needle as much as you'd like.

## Long intervals

- Most climbers don't climb enough. Trying to make up for it with very strong fingers or hours on the Moon Board doesn't replace "feel" for the rock and the rhythm of good climbing, which are essential to enduring; economy can't be built without getting off the ground.

| Element | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Base | The average climber aims for 20 pitches per week before worrying about any other specific training |
| Beyond that level | Add more long, easy climbing to the week, aiming to get better rather than more fit |
| Options | Traverses, top-rope laps, a stacked wall of moderates |
| Intensity | First rule: climb at a level where you feel relaxed and can get a lot of moves in |
| Duration | You should be able to climb at least 5 min nonstop without being shut down by fatigue |
| Skill | Keep the skill as close to real climbing as possible; avoid plodding back and forth on a jug-laden wall |
