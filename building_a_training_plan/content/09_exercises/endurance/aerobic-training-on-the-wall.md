# Aerobic Training on the Wall: Mileage Days, Gym Volume and Cardio Circuits

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Endurance

**TL;DR:** Build stamina on the wall with a high number of relatively easy climbs over a long time, at 6 to 8 out of 10 effort: get the blood pumping but avoid a deep pump. Options: a long crag day racking up routes about two number grades below your redpoint limit; as many gym routes or boulders as you can in 2 to 3 hours; 20 to 30 minutes on steeper climbs when short on time; or 2 minutes of cardio then 2 minutes of easy climbing, 6 times with no breaks. A systems board is a convenient tool for it.

## Aerobic training

- A systems board is a convenient tool for training stamina and aerobic capacity.
- On the wall: a high number of relatively easy climbs over an extended period of time. Get the blood pumping, but avoid getting too pumped. Effort 6 to 8 out of 10.
- Climbing to failure uses more aerobic energy than anaerobic.

| Option | How |
|---|---|
| Long day at the crag (or at a gym with a good selection of routes) | Rack up route after route, with the intention of accumulating as many as possible; this naturally caps the intensity, because you can't climb at your limit from dawn to dusk. Starting point: climbs about two number grades below your redpoint limit. |
| Gym session | Complete as many routes or boulders as you can in 2 to 3 hours. Avoid a deep, debilitating pump; choose climbs well below your max. Continuous movement matters more than difficulty. Goal: lots of mileage while avoiding a blistering pump and muscular failure. |
| Short on time | Anything over 20 to 30 minutes still gives effective aerobic training. Choose steeper climbs that recruit a lot of muscle at one time for a more productive session. |
| Cardio + climbing circuit | 2 minutes of cardio (treadmill, rowing machine, stationary bike or jumping jacks) followed directly by 2 minutes of low-intensity climbing on the nearest wall or systems board. Repeat the cycle 6 times with no breaks between rotations. |

### Why it works
- As in endurance sports, the pace is one you could keep up all day, not all-out. Sustaining this sub-maximal effort trains the body to use oxygen more efficiently, so you can go further and faster at the same level of effort.
