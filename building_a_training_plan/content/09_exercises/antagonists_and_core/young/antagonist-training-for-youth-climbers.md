# Antagonist Training for Youth Climbers

**Type:** Exercise/protocol, Injury & health · **Trains:** Antagonists & core · **For:** Youth

**TL;DR:** Antagonist training is perhaps the most important addition to a youth program: climbing builds plenty of pulling strength, but stable joints, good posture and fewer elbow and shoulder injuries need the push muscles and the rotator cuff. Do 15–20 minutes, 2 or 3 days per week: push-ups, dumbbell shoulder presses, dips and external and internal shoulder rotations, at a light weight that allows 20–25 reps, at the end of a climbing workout or on rest days.

## Antagonist training
- Antagonist training is perhaps the most important addition to a youth program.
- Regular climbing (even without climbing-specific strength exercises) builds a lot of pulling strength, the prime movers.
- Stable joints, proper posture, lower injury risk and peak performance need a small commitment to the antagonist push muscles of the upper body: the extension-producing muscles of the chest, shoulders and arms, and the small rotator cuff muscles.
- Dose: 15–20 minutes, 2 or 3 days per week. This vastly reduces the risk of the elbow and shoulder injuries common among avid and expert climbers.
- Starting routine:

| Exercise | Sets |
|---|---|
| Push-ups | 2 |
| Dumbbell shoulder presses | 1 or 2 |
| Dips (with assistance if needed) | 2 |
| External shoulder rotations | 1 |
| Internal shoulder rotations | 1 |

- Each exercise with relatively light weight that allows 20–25 reps.
- Do them at the end of a climbing workout or on rest days from climbing.
- At 16–18, antagonist training remains essential for muscle balance, proper posture and lower risk of elbow and shoulder injuries, all potential issues for hard-training late-teen and twenty-something climbers.
