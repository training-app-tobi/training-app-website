# Antagonist Training: Push-Ups, Shoulder Press and TRX Prone T

**Type:** Exercise/protocol, Planning · **Trains:** Antagonists & core · **For:** plateau in pulling strength or climbing

**TL;DR:** Climbing's main movers are the biceps and lats, so antagonist training means the pushing muscles: chest and triceps. Train them with push-ups (5-12 reps, 4 sets), dumbbell shoulder press (6-8 reps, 4 sets) and the TRX prone T (10 reps, 3 sets), with 2-3 min rest, at the end of the session. Focus on it in the off season and drop it first in peak season.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Plateau in pulling strength or climbing | Add antagonist training to push past it |

## Why
- In a movement, the agonist is the main mover and the antagonist is the opposing muscle. In climbing the agonists are the biceps and lats; antagonist training generally means the pushing muscles, chest and triceps, trained off the wall.
- Becoming very imbalanced and dominant in the pulling muscles opens you up to injuries, often around the shoulders or elbows.
- Climbing is not just basic pulling: in more complex movements and a variety of positions the pushing muscles often get involved.
- Three ways to train antagonists: bodyweight exercises, free weights, and suspension training (TRX or gymnastic rings).

## Doses
| Exercise | Reps | Sets | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Push-up | 5-12 | 4 | About 2-3 min |
| Dumbbell shoulder press | 6-8, with a weight that makes the final rep really hard | 4 | About 2-3 min |
| TRX prone T | 10 (higher reps to help learn the skill) | 3 (fewer, because of the stability and core engagement involved) | About 2-3 min |

## Push-up
- Needs no facilities. Targets mainly chest, shoulders and triceps; use a narrow hand position to target the triceps more.
- Keep the elbows tight to your sides through the whole range of movement.
- Keep the core engaged so the pelvis doesn't tip and the back doesn't arch.
- Full range of motion: lower in control until the nose comes close to the ground.

| Difficulty | Variant |
|---|---|
| Easier | Push-up against a wall (higher body angle), or on the floor with knees down |
| Standard | Floor push-up |
| Harder | Raise one leg (works the core harder through instability) |
| More than 12 reps at the hardest variant | Move to free weights; the traditional bench press is the go-to exercise |

- If you can't do 5 in a given form, use the easier variants.

## Shoulder press
- Climbing is overhead pulling, so overhead pressing may be one of the best antagonist exercises. Weights make it easy to overload the muscles and monitor progress.
- Start with dumbbells rather than a barbell: barbells start at around 20 kg, quite a heavy weight to be introduced to the overhead press with.
- Seated or standing; core engaged; press smoothly through a full range of motion, fully straightening the elbow, imagining pushing the ceiling away.
- Standing, don't use other parts of the body (such as the lower body) to assist the push; that is an exercise in itself. For antagonist work, a strict push press is a good place to start.
- One arm at a time or both arms together; one arm is useful when you only have one dumbbell of the weight you want.

## TRX prone T
- TRX work trains stability and more extended positions that support compression strength. Adjust difficulty by moving your feet relative to the anchor point, which changes the body angle.
- Keep the core stable so the whole chain from hands to feet stays engaged; smooth, controlled movements; you should be able to hold a pause in the end position before returning to the start.
- Keep a very slight bend in the elbows. Start with palms facing forwards; as you lower the chest forwards, take the arms directly out to the sides; finish with palms facing forwards and arms slightly in front of the chest.
- **Warning:** Don't go so far that the arms move behind the body, and don't let the arms rotate inwards or outwards.
- TRX use is a learnt skill: video yourself to check your form.
- If you can perform all 10 reps with really great form, make the exercise less intense.

## When in the year
| Phase | Antagonist training |
|---|---|
| Off season / base season (not focusing on performing or sending hard) | Ideal time to focus on it |
| Approaching the performance season | Possibly increase intensity, particularly for chest and shoulder muscles, which are involved in compression-style climbing |
| Performance phase / peak season | Likely the first thing removed from the program, to save energy for the most specific training and for performance |

- Do these exercises at the end of the session, after the climbing-specific work on the wall.
