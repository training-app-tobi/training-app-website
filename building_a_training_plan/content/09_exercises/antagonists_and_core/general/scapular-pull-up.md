# Scapular Pull-Up

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Antagonists & core · **For:** Beginner, Advanced

**TL;DR:** The scapular pull-up is an excellent warm-up exercise and also a good strengthening exercise for the rotator cuff and the scapular stabilizers. From a full hang, draw the shoulder blades down and together to raise the body slightly without bending the arms; the best cue is to try to bend the bar. Do 6–12 repetitions, rest 3 minutes, then perform a second set.

## Why do it
- An excellent warm-up exercise and also a good strengthening exercise for the rotator cuff and the scapular stabilizers.
- All the muscles of the upper torso that are so stressed in climbing get worked in a very beneficial way.

## Two versions
| Version | Range of motion | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner (entry-level) version | Small, only a few centimetres | A good way to get started, doing short sets |
| Scapular pull-up plus | Much larger | More powerful, certainly a more advanced exercise; more advanced climbers can use the larger range of motion; a couple of sets of it is quite a workout |

## How to do it
- Start position: a normal pull-up position with a palms-away grip and the hands about shoulder width apart, from a full hang with the shoulders just slightly shrugged.
- Movement: draw the shoulder blades (scapulae) down and together so they raise the body slightly, but without bending the arms as in a regular pull-up.
- The best learning cue: try to bend the bar.

## Protocol
| Reps | Form | Rest | Sets |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6–12 | Nearly straight arms and tight spinal erectors and glutes throughout | 3 minutes, then perform a second set | 2 |
