# Pushing Muscles: Bench Press, Incline Press, Lying Triceps Extension and Pullover

**Type:** Exercise/protocol · **Trains:** Antagonists & core, General strength · **For:** competition climbers, boulderers

**TL;DR:** Train the pushing muscles (chest, anterior deltoids and triceps) with weights, so you can push out from a hold, mantle on top of a boulder and press out in a competition problem. The routine is bench press (or a two-dumbbell alternative), incline shoulder press, lying overhead triceps extension and lying barbell pullover. Restrict the bottom of the range on the pressing moves, build up the load slowly and choose sets and reps by goal.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Competition climber, boulderer, or doing pressing or mantle movements | Develop the triceps: otherwise you will reach the top of the climb and not have enough to finish |
| Very powerful, fast movements | Do the lying barbell pullover (e.g. for speed or competition climbing) |
| Hunched, restricted overhead | Do the lying barbell pullover for chest mobility and strength through the full range |

## Why train the pushing muscles
- Many climbers neglect the pushing muscles: chest, anterior deltoids and triceps. The weakness shows up in specific movements, where they struggle to push out from a hold, mantle on top of a boulder or press out in a competition problem.

## Bench press
- **Warning:** Use safety bars most of the time, even if they restrict the movement a little, so you can't end up with the bar dropped onto your chest and have to climb out from under it.
- If you can, train with a partner: on the reps where you are only just pushing out, a partner who lifts the bar slightly makes a big difference to the overall training stimulus.
- Movement: bar down to the chest, then back up to straight arms.
- Grip: people not used to bench press often use a very narrow grip, which bends the wrist, feels uncomfortable and stops them producing power. Go a little wider than you might think, around shoulder width or a tiny bit wider, and keep the wrist relatively straight.
- Racking the bar: don't be delicate about it. People try to hook the bar on slowly when they are already getting shaky; get comfortable throwing the bar back onto the rack behind you.
- Form: keep the feet planted and don't allow any wobble; if you are not used to the movement it can feel daunting as the weight goes up.

| Bench press (% of bodyweight) | What it means |
|---|---|
| Around 100% | A good standard for a good climber, male or female |
| Beyond 100%, up to 200% | Useful |
| Beyond 200% | Look at other training methods to improve as a climber |
| 300% | Only if you want to become a powerlifter |

### Alternative without a bench press setup or barbell
- Two dumbbells (chest flys): bring the dumbbells down to either side of the body, then push them back up above you. It trains the same muscles in a similar way; not quite the same, because two separate objects are moving.
- **Warning:** Don't go too low: only go down to about parallel with the shoulders and chest. Going too far stretches out the shoulders, which climbers are not used to and are often tight in, and that can cause issues down the line.

## Incline shoulder press
- Why: the bench press works the plane straight above nipple height and across the chest; the incline press takes the arms slightly above the head, which involves the anterior deltoids and upper chest a lot more. This matches climbing positions: holding holds above the head, compressing, or holding a static position while clipping. It also develops strength for taking undercuts above the head.
- Execution: two dumbbells, bring them into position slowly. At the top, add a slight twist to get the full range of motion where the muscles are already contracted.
- **Warning:** At the bottom, don't go too far down: many climbers are tight at the front of the chest and shoulders, and holding load in that stretched position can be uncomfortable and aggravate existing issues around the joint. For strength gains, restrict the bottom part of the range and make the most of the top part.

## Lying triceps extension
- Triceps options: triceps extensions on a cable (below the waist), overhead with a dumbbell, or lying overhead triceps extension. The preferred one is the lying overhead extension: it puts more load on the long head of the triceps, gives a good range of motion and transfers well to climbing-specific movements, particularly mantling. Barbell or dumbbells.
- **Warning:** Don't hit yourself on the head in the middle of the movement.
- Execution: lie down, arms above the head, extend the arms. Keep the weight slightly above the head: a more comfortable position for the arms that keeps the arm working.
- If you keep developing only the pulling muscles, you end up restricted by the strength and stability of the pushing muscles on the other side of the arm (the antagonists). Keep training both sides.
- The lying triceps extension also stretches that part of the arm and works the full range of motion, reducing the risk of tightness building up and leading to elbow issues.

## Lying barbell pullover
- Exercise: lying, take the barbell from over the head and bring it back down. It works the chest, in particular the pec minor, and the lats, contracting the chest in the same way as on steep terrain or in big dynamic moves. Cable machine or dumbbells also work.
- Cue: don't just do the movement, squeeze the target area. Here, squeeze the chest by trying to twist the wrists and elbows towards each other, even though the hands are fixed on the bar; this engages the chest throughout.
- Load: a standard barbell is around 20 kg; make sure you can do the motion comfortably, with the full range of motion, at that weight. Dumbbells can be easier and may let you engage the chest muscles more.

## Doses and progression
- Build up the load slowly.

| Goal | Sets and reps |
|---|---|
| Endurance | High reps, lower sets |
| Strength | High loads, lower reps |

- If unsure about an exercise or finding the intensity difficult, have a professional check your form in person and run through the exercise with you.
