# Troubleshooting a Training Week: Three Worked Examples

**Type:** Planning, Story/case study · **Trains:** Finger strength, General strength, Power endurance, Endurance, Antagonists & core, Mobility, Recovery

**TL;DR:** For every piece of training, ask what stimulus it gives and whether that matches the goal. Spread maximal sessions through the week, don't stack hard days back-to-back, and drop a couple of reps rather than lose a rest day. Turn general conditioning into climbing-specific fingerboard and bar work that also builds work capacity, place the highest-intensity work first and early in the week, and keep the droppable half sessions for the end of the week.

## Start with the intent
- When reviewing any plan, first ask what the intent of each piece of training is: what stimulus it gives, and whether that matches the goal (e.g. a strength stimulus when the goal is to get stronger).

## Example 1: route climber in a strength cycle, six-day week

Route goal 7a+, in a strength training cycle aiming at basic strength gains.

| Day | Training as planned |
|---|---|
| Monday | Sport climbing |
| Tuesday | Fingerboarding + strength and conditioning + pull-ups |
| Wednesday | Bouldering or sport climbing |
| Thursday | Rest |
| Friday | Fingerboard max + sport climbing |
| Saturday | Fingerboard max + strength and conditioning |
| Sunday | Rest |

### What's wrong
- Too much training to recover between sessions and get high-quality sessions in: two max fingerboard sessions back-to-back (Friday and Saturday), when max sessions are always better spread through the week; possibly a third max fingerboard session on Tuesday; plus a lot of sport climbing on top.
- Whether the sport climbing helps strength depends on its type (short routes still work strength), but the total volume means fewer strength gains than focusing purely on strength would bring.
- **Warning:** This is a classic pattern of a very motivated climber putting lots of training into the week: stacked up, the ordering and cumulative effect make it very hard to keep improving, especially later on as the climber approaches their physical potential. Six days in the block with a single rest day is normally an area of concern.
- Concurrent training (mixing several qualities) is fine, but mixing all of it in a six-day block with one rest day is very intense. It depends on the athlete and the rest of their life: some very high-level (9a) climbers operate at this level, but they are unlikely to be working long hours or to have such a busy schedule while keeping the training high quality.

### Fit the week to your day

| Schedule | What to do |
|---|---|
| Can climb first thing in the morning and do another session in the evening | Use the gaps between sessions |
| Work in the daytime and climb every evening | The same week is much harder: split the sessions across the day, or keep everything in the evening and add an extra rest day |

### Ordering
- Order within a day: fingerboarding first, then the other work later in the day, is correct.
- **Warning:** Order across days: don't put a fatiguing sport-climbing day right before a maximal fingerboard day (Monday sport climbing → Tuesday max fingerboarding). You arrive tired, usually with sore skin, and can't try really hard in the strength session.

### Fixes
- If adding an extra rest day compromises a session, it is fine to drop the volume a very small amount (e.g. a couple of reps less on the fingerboard) to gain the rest day: you get high-quality rest and adapt from the training instead of doing back-to-back sessions.
- Reshuffle: ideally put a max fingerboard session mid-week so three fingerboard sessions are spread through the week; take the max fingerboarding off Saturday (you'd be fatigued there) and leave Saturday as general strength and conditioning.
- Focus the training down and spread sessions out to allow recovery: you want to train, but you also need to recover.

## Example 2: boulderer with limited gym access, training at home

Boulderer at 6B+/6C aiming for a first Font 7A. Gets to the climbing gym once or twice a week at most; has a fingerboard and rings at home.

| Session as planned | Days |
|---|---|
| Mobility | Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday |
| Max fingerboard hangs (finger strength, probably at home in the morning) | Tuesday, Friday |
| Unstructured fun climbing at the bouldering wall with friends | Tuesday, Friday |
| Calisthenics | Thursday, Sunday |
| Rest | Two rest days |

### What works
- Mobility most days (yoga or regular stretching, often done in the morning) is a great starting point if you have time for it, but keep it in mind when time is limited.
- Two rest days a week and sessions spread through the week: good. The overall layout and spread of training and recovery looks good and allows recovery from hard climbing sessions.

### Make it specific
- If you have time for extra training, push it into things with a specific reward for climbing. Change calisthenics (good general upper-body strength and conditioning) into sport-specific exercises on a fingerboard or bar, in the planes of movement used in climbing, and use that time in a more focused way, especially when gym time is limited and spent climbing unstructured.
- The fun climbing sessions can stay unstructured (exploring movement, enjoying climbing), though they are the easiest part to change if wanted. Instead, add to the other sessions work that trains the forearms for the anaerobic stress of bouldering and increases work capacity.
- Why a boulderer needs work capacity: enough strength to complete the moves is great, but on a trip or outdoors the number of attempts you can have on each problem can decide whether you complete it.
- A home fingerboard session for this (max hangs, climbing-specific pulling and short energy-systems repeaters) is in the note Home Fingerboard Session: Max Hangs, Offset Pulls and Repeaters.

## Example 3: fitting a set of sessions into four training days

- Goals: finger strength, and work capacity, both aerobic and anaerobic capacity.
- Four training days: Tuesday, Thursday, Friday and Sunday, with Friday and Sunday the big climbing days.
- Session units: 1 = a full session; 1.5 = one and a half sessions, which can be split into smaller sessions (e.g. a half session plus a whole session on different days); 0.5 = exactly the same training at the same intensity with half the sets (e.g. 3 sets at V10 instead of 6 sets at V10).
- Sessions to place: fingerboard 1, anaerobic capacity 0.5, bouldering 1.5, aerobic capacity 1.5, pull-ups 1, shoulder antagonists 1, TRX 1.5, mobility sessions.
- Place the highest-intensity, shortest-volume work first, and spread high-intensity work across the week so recovery is adequate.

| Day | Sessions, in order |
|---|---|
| Tuesday | Fingerboard (earliest in the day), then 0.5 anaerobic capacity (relatively short, non-exhaustive), then 0.5 TRX |
| Thursday | Full bouldering session + mobility |
| Friday | Full aerobic capacity session (a long day with more time), then full pull-up session on the bar and shoulder antagonist work |
| Sunday | 0.5 hard bouldering, then 0.5 aerobic capacity, then full TRX session, then mobility |

### Combining sessions on the same day
- Aerobic capacity after bouldering (a strength stimulus) on the same day is fine as a half session: rest 10 to 20 minutes between them, and use either interval-style aerobic capacity or really easy, long climbing.
- **Warning:** Avoid mixed-stimulus aerobic capacity after strength work (half the climbing time hard, half easy to recover): that stimulus may upset strength gains, though it still isn't too bad to add.
- Group the harder, more extensive conditioning sessions with climbing sessions on the days with plenty of spare time (here Friday: pull-ups and shoulder antagonists after aerobic capacity).

| Goal | Order of aerobic capacity and pull-ups on the same day |
|---|---|
| Forearm work capacity | Aerobic capacity takes priority and goes first, conditioning after (even though the conditioning is strength-focused) |
| Become a better boulderer or get stronger | Pull-ups first, because you can still complete aerobic capacity work in good form after pull-ups |

- TRX: the half session goes on Tuesday because that day isn't hard or exhaustive; the full session goes on the big Sunday day, followed by mobility, to increase general work capacity. The remaining mobility session goes on Thursday.
- A day with little on it (Thursday) spreads out the week, gives adequate recovery and leaves plenty of energy for the sessions.

### Front-load the week
- Put the smaller half sessions late in the week: if you arrive at Sunday tired, go outside, do something different or can't complete all the sessions, they are the easiest to drop. TRX and mobility can be done at home or added to the back of another session.
- **Warning:** Don't load too much training at the back of the week. If something comes up and you can't do it, you push it into the next week on top of that week's training, which leads to overload and injuries, or to not getting anything done. Having lots of time can make this worse: you leave things until late in the week and then try to cram them into the weekend, possibly while also climbing outside. Do the short, high-quality sessions earlier in the week.
- Combine sessions you enjoy doing together, and experiment. E.g. fingerboarding and aerobic capacity work together can feel good; a really hard anaerobic capacity session (getting really pumped) followed by aerobic capacity is fine physiologically but may not suit you because of skin and motivation. Some people don't like doing lots of conditioning together.
