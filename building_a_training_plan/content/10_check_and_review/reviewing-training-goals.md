# Reviewing Training Goals

**Type:** Goals & mindset, Planning

**TL;DR:** When the season shifts from training to climbing outside, check in on your training goals and reflect on the protocols you chose, to inform future training. After a few sessions on rock, check how the trained attributes have transferred, and reuse cues from training. When plans fail through injury or travel restrictions, maintain your gains, which fade slowly and are easier to keep than to build, and set new goals you can still work towards.

## Training goals check-in
- Set training goals with your climbing goals in mind, so that the physical attributes you develop through training transfer to the climbing.
- When the season shifts from training to climbing outside, don't just switch your attention to the crag: check in on your training goals. Training goals help motivation because they can be chosen to suit climbing goals and are measurable, and checking in on the progress made also lets you reflect on the training protocols you chose. There are many approaches to training the same thing, so reflecting on progress informs future training.

## Climbing goals check-in
- After some time getting used to rock, check in on how the physical attributes you focused on in training have translated to the climbing. Don't analyse whether training has translated to the rock in the first few sessions.
- Paying attention to this transfer may itself help you translate the strength, by giving focus and/or cues. Cues you found useful in training may be useful on the rock, because they are well practised.

## Be flexible and adapt
- Training and climbing don't always go to plan, e.g. because of injury or travel restrictions: you may reach your training goals and not get the chance to use them on the climbing you had in mind.
- Gains made in training are not lost straight away, and they are easier to maintain than the time it took to build them. Be creative and look for ways to maintain your training gains: the chance to try your climbing goal may still come.
- Look for new goals you can still work towards despite injury or restrictions.
- Maintaining a positive mindset is important; gratitude and reflection practices can help.
- During injury or time off, focus each day on what you can do and remember how it feeds into your long-term climbing goals.

## Example of a constructive review
| Training goal | Result | What was learned / next step |
|---|---|---|
| 4 laps of an indoor crack (for a crack-climbing trip) | Training goal achieved | The climbing goal it served became impossible because of travel restrictions |
| Assisted front lever (for roof crack climbing) | Achieved, with good progression from assisted front levers, reducing the assistance as strength improves | Keep using this method to keep building the strength. With the roof-crack trip impossible, a local boulder problem that needs the same physical attribute became the new goal, with lots of improvement on it |
| 10-second two-arm hang with +20 kg in half crimp | Achieved; the method worked for a climber for whom finger strength is hard to gain | A finger injury then meant rehab and time off climbing, putting the climbing goals on hold. Next step: maintain the body strength gained, and set a running goal as something to work towards outside |
