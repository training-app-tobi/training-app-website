# Making Successful Climbing Goals

**Type:** Goals & mindset, Planning

**TL;DR:** Set goals with two groups of rules: general rules (inspiring, realistic, demanding, fun) that shape the outcome and keep the process consistent, then specific training rules that make the effort actually achieve the goal. Nail down the general rules first: do the goal for you, set it at the limit of what's possible but within your abilities, let it push you into a zone of discomfort, and keep an element of fun in the process.

## Two kinds of rules
- General rules are overarching principles that affect the outcome and the consistency of the process.
- Specific training rules decide whether the effort you put in will actually achieve the goal (see the note Specific Training Rules for a Climbing Goal).
- Nail down the general rules first; only then look at the specifics.

## General rules
| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| Inspiring | The goal must genuinely inspire you: something you feel in the pit of your stomach and that makes your heart beat a bit faster. Your discipline, willpower and effort over the year will be much more consistent if you get this right. Don't pick a goal to impress friends or parents, to make your social media look good or to satisfy your coach: do it for you. |
| Realistic | To achieve a goal it must be realistic. You can stretch the boundaries and set it at the limit of what's possible, but it must fit your abilities; think hard about this. |
| Demanding | Goals are followed more consistently, are more rewarding and are achieved more often when they are demanding, i.e. they push you into a zone of discomfort, physically, mentally or technically. It's a fine line how hard you push it. |
| Fun | The process of chasing the goal must be fun. Not every day can be fun, but the mind needs an uplifting feeling to stay in the goal process for a long time, however good an athlete you are. The most successful climbers and professional athletes regularly have an element of fun in their goal chasing; don't ignore it. |
