# Effective Goal Setting for Climbing

**Type:** Goals & mindset, Planning

**TL;DR:** Use long-term performance goals (a grade or a lifetime project) together with short-term goals that give daily motivation and tangible progress. Set concrete, measurable training goals and track them over many weeks. Write goals down, define them in detail and tell a friend, keep them challenging but realistic with incremental wins every few weeks, give each a deadline, and write down one or two things you will give up to reach it.

## Long-term and short-term goals
- Long-term goals for many climbers are simply to climb harder or experience more on the rock; for others the ultimate goal is a specific "lifetime project" or a certain lofty grade.
- Such performance goals give sustained motivation as long as they are specific and not completely unreasonable given your current ability.
- Also set short-term goals: they give daily motivation for training and climbing and a sense of tangible progress toward the ultimate goal. Use short- and long-term goals together.

## Performance goals
The most common performance goal is a grade to achieve:

| Horizon | Example grade goal |
|---|---|
| Short term (this year) | 5.12a or V7 |
| Ultimate long term | Someday climb 5.13 or boulder V10 |

- Alongside grade goals, set a few even more specific goals: the route you want to send this year, next season, and your ultimate lifetime project.
- Write down the names of the climbs and post them where you will see them every day. This keeps them top of mind, improves the odds of achieving them and makes it easier to tailor your daily behaviour to pursuing them.

## Training goals
- Set process goals for training that raise motivation and give physical gains you can see and feel.
- They work best when concrete and measurable, not vague (e.g. "improve flexibility").
- Examples: 20 pull-ups; 5 weighted (hypergravity) pull-ups with 22.5 kg around the waist; a 1-3-5-7 campus board sequence.
- Track progress toward them over many weeks of training; the visible progress raises motivation further.

## Five tips for effective goals

### 1. Write down your goals
- Writing goals down makes them more real and far more achievable.
- Keep a training notebook or climbing diary recording your goals, workout plan and climbing accomplishments.

### 2. Define goals specifically and tell a friend
- Define goals specifically and in as much detail as possible, then tell a friend and ask for accountability. The encouragement and accountability of a friend or partner is extremely important.
- Measurable goals are best, but a few style or mental goals also help, e.g. "improve footwork", "rest more effectively en route", "climb more briskly and with more economy".
- For these subjective goals, ask a partner or coach to observe your climbing and decide when you have actually achieved the goal.

### 3. Make goals lofty and challenging but realistic
- **Warning:** Unreachable goals are counterproductive and kill motivation, e.g. "do a one-arm pull-up this year" when you can barely do 10 two-arm pull-ups, or "climb 5.13/V9 by year's end" when you currently climb 5.11/V4.
- Instead, set incremental goals that give a motivating "win" every few weeks.

### 4. Set a deadline for each goal
- Examples: "10 consecutive pull-ups by June 1st", "boulder a V5 by my birthday".
- A deadline drives action, and the drive grows as the deadline nears; goals without deadlines lead to half-hearted action.

### 5. Write down what you will give up
- Write down one thing (or two) you will give up to reach the goal. This step is missing from most traditional goal-setting exercises.
- Achievement doesn't come only from doing more or trying harder; it also requires eliminating or detaching from things that hold you back.
