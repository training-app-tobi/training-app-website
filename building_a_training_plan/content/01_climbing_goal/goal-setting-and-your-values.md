# Goal Setting and Your Values

**Type:** Goals & mindset

**TL;DR:** Before using a tool like SMART, set climbing goals that align with your values (e.g. friendship, peace of mind, fun, creativity, independence); aligned goals give you a far greater chance of sticking to and achieving them. Be really clear about your values and check them for conflicts, because conflicting values can make goals unrealistic and cause stress.

## Why goal setting needs more than a formula
- Goal setting is not a straightforward process for many people. Even if you have found a formula that works for setting and completing goals, goalposts shift and life changes, and then another approach is required; your approach changes as your climbing evolves over the years.
- A common goal-setting tool is SMART (specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound). It has benefits and there is a lot to unpick in it, but there is a more fundamental step that many (arguably most) people overlook: setting goals that align with your values.

## Goals aligned with your values
- Values are things like friendship, peace of mind, fun, creativity and independence. When you set your climbing goals in line with these fundamental principles of who you are, you have a far greater chance of sticking to and achieving them.
- Even for competition goals at the top level, happiness has to be at the core: without it, success in climbing can't be achieved.
- Example: if fun and friendship are core values (in life and in climbing), your climbing goals must include both fun and friendship. Not paying attention to the detail of these values shows up as climbing with people you don't know well and as goals drifting into the serious. After years of training really hard it is easy for climbing goals to become quite serious; with fun as a core value, this is not a good approach.

## Conflicting values
- **Warning:** A conflict between your values can really get in the way of achieving goals, so be really clear about your values. Example: hard work (personal challenge) and peace of mind are both values. Given half a chance, hard work and personal challenge take over and peace of mind takes a back seat; goals then become unrealistic, logistics and available time don't match up, and this causes stress.

## Questions to ask yourself
- What are your values?
- Which climbing goals best align with these values?
- Are there any conflicts in your values that may get in the way of achieving those goals?
