# Goal Setting on a Timeline: Long-, Medium- and Short-Term Climbing Goals

**Type:** Goals & mindset, Planning

**TL;DR:** List your dream goals without worrying about time, money or other obligations, then work backwards from each one to medium-term checkpoints laid out on a calendar in three-month chunks. Break the first checkpoint into short-term goals planned down to reps and sets, and give every session its own specific goal so that no two days have the same goal. Make room for climbing by bumping down things you don't really care about.

## Goal levels
| Level | Time frame | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| Long-term | From now on, no time limit | The dream: grades, summits, "firsts" |
| Medium-term (checkpoints) | Three-month chunks | What has to happen before you can really start on the dream |
| Short-term | Each training session | What you do in each session and what you want to gain from it |

## Long-term goals
- Ask which achievements would make you most satisfied with your climbing from now on: which grades you hope to reach, which summits you want to stand on, which "firsts" you dream of claiming.
- At this stage ignore time, money and other obligations; let the ideas stand on their own.

## Medium-term goals
- Work backwards: take each long-term goal one at a time and break it into pieces. Ask what has to happen before you can really start on the end goal, e.g. a handful of grades between your current level and your target, or skills to master before branching into a new sector of climbing. Each of these is a checkpoint (medium-term goal) on the way.
- Take a calendar and look at the next three months: pick the medium-term goal that fits this chunk of time, then the ones that follow. The result is a map from now to the dream goal, with intermediate goals spaced evenly throughout.
- Order the three-month medium-term goals by application or importance.
- Use the checkpoints as places to pause, catch your breath and congratulate yourself; these quicker wins along the way make the road feel much shorter.

## Short-term goals
- Break the first medium-term goal down further into short-term goals. They decide what you do every day: how you spend each training session and what you want to gain from it.
- Plan the specific workouts down to reps and sets, where they fit in your schedule, how you will measure progress on them, and how you will consistently raise the demand.
- Give every training session its own short-term goal that links directly back to the closest checkpoint, specific enough that no two days have exactly the same goal. Always give yourself the chance to outperform yesterday's performance.

| Session | Goal (example: campus board ladders) |
|---|---|
| 1 | Three sets of campus board ladders on each side as fast as possible |
| 2 | Three sets on each side at least one second faster than last session |
| Next sessions | Keep improving the time, or shift the focus to the distance between rungs |

## Priorities
- It's fine if climbing isn't, or can't be, number one; family, friends, career and other pastimes are just as worthy of attention.
- But bump down anything you don't really care about, e.g. late nights out or an extra hour of screen time, and you'll soon see the difference in your climbing.
