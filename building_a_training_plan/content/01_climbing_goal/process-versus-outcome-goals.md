# Process Versus Outcome Goals

**Type:** Goals & mindset, Planning

**TL;DR:** An outcome goal (e.g. a grade) is the destination, but stopping there tells you where you're going and not how to get there. Turn each outcome goal into process goals: work out the skills and strengths a climber who reaches it would have, compare them with yours, and pick the drills, exercises and routines that close the gap. From the process perspective the outcome is a bonus; the real win is the skills and strength you gain.

## Grade-only goals
- What you choose to do has more impact than what happens to you: you put in the work to turn the goal into reality instead of waiting for the luck of the draw, and the effort of deciding on a direction and pushing on despite obstacles makes the experience more valuable.
- Many climbers set goals about what they would like to happen to them (e.g. achieving a certain grade) rather than what they can actually do about it. Using grades as a metric to gauge progress is fine.
- A goal that is only a grade fails to dig beneath the surface: it starts and stops with hitting a new benchmark and addresses none of the many factors that go into actually reaching that grade. It puts too much emphasis on the result and not enough on the action.

## Outcome and process goals
| | Outcome goals | Process goals |
|---|---|---|
| Center on | The end result | What you do: the process of getting where you want to go |
| Role | The destination; without one it is hard to know where to go at all | The action; things to actively work on that move you closer to the outcome |
| Question they answer | Where am I going? | What is missing between the climber I am now and the climber who can achieve the outcome goal? Which skills and strengths would I need to reach the next level, and what would I need to practice to get them? |

- Everyone has outcome goals and they are an important part of goal setting.
- **Warning:** Don't stop at the outcome goal: you know where you're going but not how to get there. Your first 5.13a doesn't happen if you just wait long enough; it takes specific, consistent, dedicated action that gradually lifts you toward the outcome.
- Process goals keep the outcome in the picture without letting it take up the whole page. From this perspective achieving the outcome is a bonus rather than the main intention; the real win is growing as a climber by learning the skills and building the strength the climb demanded.

## Turning an outcome goal into process goals
| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1. Picture the skills | Work out which skills a climber who could achieve this goal would have, e.g. extended lock-offs from half-pad crimps; enough power to huck for a far-off pocket; trusting the toes on greasy, dime-sized footholds while pressing into a wide dihedral |
| 2. Compare | Compare those skills with the ones you have now and determine what needs to change or improve to get from Point A to Point B: the difference between you and the future version of you that reaches the outcome |
| 3. Bridge the gap | Work out which drills, exercises and routines target those missing pieces, and how to build these practices into your training |
