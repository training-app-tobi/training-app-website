# Which Tests Predict Bouldering Grade at Each Level

**Type:** Test, Principles · **Trains:** Finger strength, Pulling strength, Power endurance, Mobility · **For:** Advanced (V4–V8), Elite (V9–V12), Higher Elite (V13+)

**TL;DR:** Finger strength (7-second max hang, %BW) is the strongest predictor of bouldering grade, followed by pulling strength (2-rep max pull-up, %BW). Both are the most important predictors for Advanced and Elite climbers, and their influence declines as level rises: at V13+ only finger strength is still significant, and it explains only 4.2% of the variation in grade. Power endurance (60% repeaters to exhaustion) and flexibility (box split) are not significant predictors at any level; at the highest level, technique, efficiency, more specific strength and mental strategies likely matter more.

## Why look at each ability level
- As the grade rises, problems get more complex and more strenuous: smaller holds, fewer fingers, longer sustained sequences or shorter, more powerful sequences.
- A single test cannot capture all of this, so the question is whether the best predictor of bouldering grade changes as ability level rises.

## The sample
- 901 climbers: 773 male, 98 female, 40 sex not stated; grades V1 to V15.
- Data were cleaned of outliers and of sport-climbing specialists (942 → 901).
- Performance = highest boulder grade worked within a two-year period.
- Levels: Intermediate (V0–V3), Advanced (V4–V8), Elite (V9–V12), Higher Elite (V13+). The Intermediate group was too small and was left out of the per-level analysis (analysed: Combined, Advanced, Elite, Higher Elite).

|---|---|---|

## The four tests
- Four tests: finger strength, power endurance, pulling strength and flexibility.
- Each climber did them in their own time at a facility of their choice.

### Finger strength: 7-second max hang

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Warm-up | Warm up first |
| Hang | Progressive dead hangs for 7 seconds, both arms, half-crimp grip |
| Progression | Start with a moderate hang, then add load each attempt; aim to reach the maximum within 4–8 attempts |
| Too many attempts | If more than 8 attempts are needed, rest 24 hours and retry |
| Failure | An attempt fails if the grip position changes; the last successful attempt is recorded |
| Total load | Body weight + added load (or minus removed load) |
| Record | Fingerboard used; edge depth (18, 20 or 22 mm); total load (kg); %BW = (total load / body weight) × 100 |

- The test is the maximum load held on 2 arms on a 20 mm edge for 7 seconds, given as a percentage of body weight.

### Power endurance: 60% time to exhaustion

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| When | After the finger strength test |
| Load | 60% of the max total load: (body mass + max-test added load) × 0.6; use a pulley for assistance if needed |
| Repeaters | 7 seconds hang, 3 seconds rest, repeated until failure |
| Failure | Can't complete a 7-second hang, or grip position changes |
| Arm position | Keep the elbow angle between 150 and 180 degrees throughout, never lock off |
| Record | Total time (s) of successful hangs; total load (kg) |

### Pulling strength: 2-rep max pull-up

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Load | Add weight with a harness or sling; increase load to find the 2RM |
| Rest | 3 minutes between attempts |
| Grip | Shoulder-width, palms facing away |
| Rep | From nearly straight arms until the chin reaches the bar, controlled, with no lower-body movement |
| Record | Total load (kg); %BW |

### Flexibility: standing box split against a wall

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| What it measures | Hip flexibility |
| Start | Stand with toes touching the wall |
| Movement | Gradually move the feet wider while toes, stomach and wall stay in contact |
| Support | Upper-body support only for getting into and out of the position |
| Record | Maximum distance between the heels at the widest stance (cm) |

## Results: all levels combined

| Test | R | Share of variance in grade | F(1, 899), p | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Finger strength (7-sec max hang, %BW) | 0.704 | 49.6% (R² = 0.496) | 885.74, p < 0.001 | Moderate-to-strong relationship |
| Pulling strength (2RM pull-up, %BW) | 0.582 | 33.7% (R² = 0.337) | 459.36, p < 0.001 | Moderate positive relationship; a significant predictor, but other factors contribute substantially to performance |
| Power endurance (60% time to exhaustion, s) | 0.007 | R² = −0.001 | 0.046, p = 0.830 | Little to no relationship; not significant |
| Flexibility (box split, cm) | 0.140 | 2% (R² = 0.020) | 17.08, p < 0.001 | Weak positive relationship; significant |

## Results by level
- Advanced (V4–V8): finger strength and pulling strength are the most influential factors.
- Elite (V9–V12): the influence of finger and pulling strength declines; finger strength is still the strongest predictor.
- Higher Elite (V13+): the predictive power of all variables weakens a lot.

- At the Higher Elite level, factors other than strength and endurance play more of a role: more specific strength measures, technique, efficiency and mental strategies may be more critical.

## Conclusion
- Finger strength and pulling strength are the most important predictors for Advanced and Elite climbers; their influence declines in Higher Elite climbers.
- 60% power endurance and flexibility are not significant predictors at any level.
- Physical strength is a key factor in early climbing progression; high-level climbers likely depend more on specific training, advanced movement skills, route reading, mental endurance and efficiency than on raw physical attributes.
