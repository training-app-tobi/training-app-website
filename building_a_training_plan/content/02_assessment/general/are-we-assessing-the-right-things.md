# Are We Assessing the Right Things?

**Type:** Test, Goals & mindset · **Trains:** Finger strength, Pulling strength · **For:** stalled progress

**TL;DR:** Physical assessment numbers such as a 20 mm edge hang or a weighted pull-up don't set the best climbers apart: climbing is about clipping chains on routes and topping out boulders. Use those tests to assess how effective your training is, not your overall performance, and never train just to make the numbers look better at the retest. To assess performance, look at three measures: route/boulder pyramids, how often you make the most of your climbing day and your attitude at the gym or crag. On the pyramid, filling the lower tiers, and filling them faster than the year before, matters most.

## Who should do what

| Situation / goal | Do this |
|---|---|
| Assessing how effective your training is | Measure your 20 mm edge hang and tests such as max pull-ups |
| Assessing performance | Look at three measures: route/boulder pyramids, how often you make the most of your climbing day, your attitude at the gym or crag |
| Season after season, the load you can hang from your waist goes up but you keep climbing the same grades | Don't assume your weighted pull-up or 20 mm edge hang is the main thing holding you back: use those tests to assess your training, and judge your performance by the three measures |

## Physical numbers vs performance

- Physical assessment numbers don't set the best climbers apart: mixed in with a group's physical assessment numbers, an elite climber's probably couldn't be picked out; mixed in with the group's climbing histories, theirs would stand out at once.
- Be cautious about how much value you place on physical assessments and keep the focus on what really matters: performance. Climbing is about clipping chains on routes and topping out boulders, not your max 20 mm edge hang or how much you can lift off the floor. Don't assume your weighted pull-up or 20 mm edge hang is the main thing holding you back.

## What testing is for

- Data now suggests that if you can hang a certain load on a 20 mm edge, chances are you climb a certain grade. Look more at the climbers who can't hang that load but still climb that grade.
- Use stats without letting them take over: when stats come to dominate your judgment, you are no longer using judgment at all. Stats are worth having, but look at and measure some different things too, and don't make something important just because it can be measured; measure it because it is important.
- Measuring your 20 mm edge hang, and tests such as max pull-ups, is important, but more to assess how effective your training is than your overall performance: what gets measured gets managed.
- Season after season, climbers raise the load they can hang from their waist and keep climbing the same grades: they hang on the hangboard with 45.5+ kg added and still can't climb a route on small crimps.
- **Warning:** Worse still is training specifically for the assessment, just so the numbers look better at the retest. Keep asking yourself what the end goal is.

## Three performance measures

To assess performance, look at different measurements. Three matter most:

| Measure | Kind |
|---|---|
| Route/boulder pyramids | Objective |
| How often you make the most of your climbing day | Subjective |
| Your attitude at the gym or crag | Subjective |

- These are less exciting than testing a max weighted pull-up or finger strength on a strain gauge, but put a lot of emphasis on the three and in six months you won't really care about your pull-up numbers.

## Route/boulder pyramids

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| At the start of each year | List the routes and boulders you'd like to send, or at least try |
| From the list | Build a pyramid with your goal grade at the top, working down from there |

- Each year brings you closer to sending the top tier, but filling in the lower tiers, and filling them faster than the year before, matters more. Example: if 3 years ago a V8 took you three sessions and now you send them in 1 session, your 20 mm edge data doesn't carry much weight next to that.
- The pyramid pays off most at the end of the day: when you could easily stop and have a beer with friends, climb the bottom-tier routes still in front of you (e.g. two 11d routes), because you have bought into filling the bottom tier. Do this year after year and soon 11d won't even register on your pyramid.
