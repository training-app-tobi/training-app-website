# Three Hangboard Tests for Finger Strength and Endurance

**Type:** Test · **Trains:** Finger strength, Power endurance, Endurance

**TL;DR:** Use three hangboard tests to track finger strength and endurance, because climbing performance varies too much with style, angle, texture and conditions to be a reliable measure. Test max strength with 5-second weighted hangs on a 10 mm edge, power endurance with 5 s on / 5 s off repeaters on a 10 mm edge, and stamina with one maximum hang on a 20 mm edge. Repeat the tests at regular intervals throughout a training cycle, or every few months during a projecting season.

## Why test on a hangboard
- Climbs on plastic and real rock vary in style, angle, texture and conditions (weather, crowds), so climbing performance is not a reliable measure of ability. A hangboard removes that variability and gives reliable metrics for tracking strength and endurance.
- Regular hangboard tests show what is working in your training, what isn't and what to change. They can also uncover hidden strength or endurance weaknesses when you are failing on a project without knowing why.
- When to test: at regular intervals throughout a training cycle, or every few months during a projecting season.

## Finger strength test
| Element | Protocol |
|---|---|
| Edge | 10 mm |
| Grip | Half crimp, no thumb lock |
| Load | Weight added via weights hanging from the harness |
| Hang | 5 seconds |
| Goal | Find the maximum added weight you can hold for the 5 seconds; it usually takes a few attempts to pinpoint the limit |
| Rest | 3 minutes between every attempt, so each one is fresh |

- Reference: elite climbers can often pass this test with 1/3 of their bodyweight added. Work toward this.
- **Warning:** Warm up very well before starting: maximum hangs are strenuous on the fingers.

## Finger endurance test 1: power endurance
| Element | Protocol |
|---|---|
| Edge | 10 mm |
| Grip | Half crimp, thumb not folded over the fingers to lock them |
| Load | Bodyweight, no added weight |
| Pattern | Repeated 5-second hangs: 5 s on, 5 s off, for as long as you can |
| End | When you can no longer sustain the pattern |
| Score | Number of rounds |

- What it measures: blood flow through the forearm flexors. It simulates a continuously difficult route with little chance to rest.
- Timing must be precise: have a partner time you, or put a stopwatch where you can see it from the hangboard.
- **Warning:** Warm up well to prepare muscles and tendons for the stress.

## Finger endurance test 2: stamina
| Element | Protocol |
|---|---|
| Edge | 20 mm |
| Grip | Half crimp |
| Load | Bodyweight |
| Hang | One hang, as long as you can |
| Score | Hang time |

- What it measures: stamina, i.e. the anaerobic lactic energy system.
- The pump escalates fast, so the 20 mm edge soon feels much smaller.
- **Warning:** Warm up first, to avoid getting flash-pumped.

Reference times: expect 20 seconds to 2 minutes.

| Level | Hang time |
|---|---|
| Moderately advanced | 45 seconds on average |
| Advanced (5.13) | Usually 60–90 seconds |
| Elite | Approaching 2 minutes |

## Limits of the tests
- Finger strength is essential but not the only factor in climbing performance, and these tests don't measure everything that matters. Still, finger strength has a direct relationship with climbing performance, so tracking it keeps training on track.
