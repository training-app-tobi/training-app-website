# Max Hang Test to Set Finger Training Loads

**Type:** Test · **Trains:** Finger strength

**TL;DR:** Once grip, arm position and hang time are chosen, a testing session benchmarks your baseline and lets you prescribe training intensities as a percentage of your score: 4–8 sets on a standardised edge, starting submaximal and adding load, with 2–3 min rest; the score is the heaviest weight held for the chosen hang time. Max hang test: 20 mm edge on a hangboard; max pick-up test: 18–22 mm lifting edge. Repeat the test as often as you like to track progress.

## Testing
- A testing session, once grip, arm position and hang time are chosen (how to choose them is in the note Finger Strength Training: Methods, Grip Types and Progressive Overload), lets you benchmark your baseline (easier to monitor progress) and prescribe training intensities as a percentage of your test score.

| Testing protocol | |
|---|---|
| Sets | 4–8, in your chosen grip type and arm position, for your chosen hang time |
| Edge | Standardised edge size |
| Load | Start at a submaximal weight and increase it across the sets |
| Rest | 2–3 min between sets |
| Score | Your max hang score is the heaviest weight you successfully held for the selected hang time |

- Use test results to set the intensities of a plan, e.g. the note A 12-Week Progressive Overload Protocol for Finger Strength, based on your finger strength score. Any plan should be fully customised to individual needs and training history.
- Determine your max hang or max pick-up and calculate your training loads (for different protocols) from it. Max hang test: 20 mm edge on a hangboard. Max pick-up test: 18–22 mm lifting (pick-up) edge.
- Repeat the test as often as you like to track progress and check that your training is working.
