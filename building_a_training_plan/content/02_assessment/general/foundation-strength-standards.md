# Foundation Strength Standards: Six Movement Benchmarks

**Type:** Test · **Trains:** General strength, Finger strength, Pulling strength, Antagonists & core

**TL;DR:** Benchmarks for the six ways a climber should be able to apply high force: stand from a sub-parallel squat with about bodyweight added; deadlift about 1.5–2× bodyweight and hold the Sorenson Test position for 2 minutes; bench or single-arm overhead press set fractions of bodyweight; row 1/2 (women) or 2/3 (men) of bodyweight and do 5 or 8 tactical pull-ups; 6 knees-to-elbows without swinging and a front lever; a 5-second one-arm max hang on a 20 mm edge. Finger strength varies most between individuals, so test it regularly and look for improvement each training cycle.

## Foundation strength standards
- Foundation strength is the ability to apply high levels of force in six ways: squatting, hinging, pressing, pulling, midsection and finger strength. The standards below show where you stand on each.

| Pattern | Why | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Squatting | | Stand from a sub-parallel squat (butt very close to the ground) with an additional load of about bodyweight. Alternative: a single-leg squat with either leg from the same position at bodyweight or a little more |
| Hinging | The posterior chain keeps the body upright and, in climbing, the hips against the wall, even in roofs. Hip hinge exercises are likely the biggest bang for your buck in general strength | Deadlift about 1.5–2× bodyweight, and hold the Sorenson Test position for 2 minutes |
| Pressing | Often downplayed in climbing, but strong pressing muscles (those that push loads away from the body) are fundamental to good movement, joint stability and continued progress in pulling strength | Women: 2/3 bodyweight bench press, or 1/4 bodyweight single-arm overhead press. Men: bodyweight bench press, and a single-arm overhead press of 1/3 to 1/2 bodyweight |
| Pulling | Most climbers already have much more than enough pulling strength. The rowing motion is more useful to train than the pull-up, and the standards reflect this | Women: strict dumbbell row at 1/2 bodyweight, and 5 tactical pull-ups. Men: row 2/3 bodyweight, and 8 tactical pull-ups |
| Midsection | Not a six-pack or holding a plank for 3 minutes; only performance and the ability to maintain tension count | 6 knees-to-elbows without breaking tension (i.e. no swinging), and a front lever |
| Finger strength | Fundamental to climbing, almost unnecessary in most other activities | A 5-second max hang on a 20 mm edge with each arm individually, and squeezing the equivalent of bodyweight with each hand on a dynamometer |

- Finger strength is a highly individual measure, more than any other factor tested. More important than the number: test regularly and look for improvement over each training cycle.
