# Hip and Shoulder Mobility Tests: Frog Stretch and Fist Reach

**Type:** Test · **Trains:** Mobility

**TL;DR:** Shoulders and hips are where adults lose mobility. As a rule of thumb, you should hold both arms straight overhead without arching your back, squat fully with heels down, and get your hips within about 7.5–10 cm of the ground in a frog stretch. Two tests track progress: the frog stretch with feet against a wall, measuring hip height in fists below the pubis (touching a fist is probably good, more than two fists is work to do), and the behind-the-back fist reach, where a gap larger than your hand length on either side points to a shoulder mobility issue.

## Mobility

- Mobility is the ability to use a joint through its intended range of motion. Knees and elbows are mostly not a problem; shoulders and hips, especially in adults, are.
- **Warning:** rule of thumb: if you can't hold both arms straight above your head without arching your back, your shoulders are too tight. Building massive strength and power in the back and shoulders without maintaining mobility makes you tight, so tight that you are effectively shorter when reaching high above your head.
- Hips: if you can't do a full squat with heels on the ground, or can't get your hips within about 7.5-10 cm of the ground in a frog stretch, you probably won't maximize your body-positioning ability.
- Mobility work is boring and it's hard to tell if you're improving; two easy tests show your progress.

### Hip mobility test (frog stretch)

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Frog position with feet against a wall, shins perpendicular to the wall (right angle at the ankle), thighs parallel to the wall (right angle at the knee) |
| 2 | With most of your weight on the elbows, lean forward onto the arms and move the knees as far apart as possible, realigning the legs to restore the right angles if needed |
| 3 | Once relaxed into the stretch, check how far the hips are from the ground by placing a fist on the ground below the pubis (the bottom part of the hip bone) |
| 4 | Hips touch the fist = mobility probably pretty good; more than two fists from the ground = work to do |

### Shoulder mobility test

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Measure hand length from the tip of the middle finger to the distal wrist crease (the wrinkle closest to the palm) |
| 2 | Make two fists, thumbs tucked inside; reach over your head and back with the left hand and behind your back with the right; bring the fists as close as possible along the spine; a partner measures the distance between them |
| 3 | Repeat with the right hand over the top |
| 4 | If either side is greater than your hand length, you might have a shoulder mobility issue to address |
