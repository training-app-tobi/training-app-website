# PMS and Tracking in the Late Luteal Phase

**Type:** Injury & health, Goals & mindset · **Trains:** Recovery · **For:** Female, late luteal phase

**TL;DR:** Track how you feel, but don't let a previous cycle dictate how you expect to feel or perform: check in on how you actually feel in the moment. Outdoors and well recovered, push as normal; with PMS symptoms, climb slightly easier and spend more time warming up; in training, deload and focus on climbing efficiency.

## Who should do what
| Situation | Do this |
|---|---|
| Outdoors, well recovered, without the usual PMS symptoms | Push yourself and change nothing |
| Outdoors with PMS symptoms | Choose slightly easier climbing, use more mental strategies, and spend more time getting the body warm and the mind into the right mindset |
| Training and building more fatigue (symptoms are worse) | Deload during this phase, spend more time warming up, and use the time to focus on climbing efficiency |

## The late luteal phase and tracking
- The late luteal phase is the phase usually associated with PMS symptoms, and it is often framed as the "lesser" phase for performance.
- Tracking is a good way to understand how you feel during this phase, but a previous cycle's experience should not dictate how you expect to feel or perform in later cycles.
- Balance being prepared and making adjustments with checking in on how you actually feel in the moment.
- When tracking, ask yourself honestly how you feel, and consider reasons other than cycle phase that may explain reduced performance.

## Not every cycle is the same
- Symptoms can differ between training periods and outdoor climbing periods.
- In training you push the body hard to make it adapt; when redpointing or trying to climb your best outside, the focus is more on recovery (and you get more vitamin D).
- Water retention, sleep and mood can be better when well recovered and spending a lot more time outside.
- This may not be the same for everyone.
