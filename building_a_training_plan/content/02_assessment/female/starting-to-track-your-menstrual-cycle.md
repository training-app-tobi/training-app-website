# Starting to Track Your Menstrual Cycle

**Type:** Injury & health, Planning · **For:** Female

**TL;DR:** If you are new to tracking, keep it simple: track your cycle over 6 months to find your average cycle length and how heavy or light and how long your period normally is, and note any recurring physical or psychological symptoms at particular points in your cycle. Once you know your cycle in a macro sense, basal temperature, ovulation pee sticks and blood tests can tell you more. Use what you learn to guide your climbing, training and other life choices.

## How to start tracking
| Step | What to do | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Track for 6 months | Track your cycle over 6 months | Your average cycle length, and how heavy or light and how long your period normally is |
| 2. Notice patterns | Note any recurring physical or psychological symptoms at particular points in your cycle, e.g. water retention, bloating, cramps, fear, irritability, feeling superhuman | Which symptoms recur, and when in your cycle |
| 3. Further steps (once you know your cycle in a macro sense, and if you want to understand it further) | Temperature tracking with a basal thermometer | When you have ovulated |
| 3. Further steps | Ovulation pee sticks (usually used by those trying to start a family) | When you are ovulating |
| 3. Further steps | Blood tests, best done with a review by a sports endocrinologist | Your hormonal profile |

- Free apps can track your cycle and your symptoms.
- **Warning:** If you don't have a period, or your period is irregular, and you are not taking a contraceptive that inhibits a natural menstrual cycle, get it checked by a medical professional.

## Using what you learn
- Once you know your own cycle, use it to inform how your climbing, training and other life factors (work, kids etc.) can influence or be influenced by your cycle, to guide the choices you make.
