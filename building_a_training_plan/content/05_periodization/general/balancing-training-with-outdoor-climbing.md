# Balancing Training with Outdoor Climbing

**Type:** Planning · **Trains:** Finger strength, Power endurance, Mobility, Antagonists & core · **For:** outdoor climbing season

**TL;DR:** Your priority decides the balance between training and climbing outside. If getting outside matters most, drop training sessions when needed; if improving matters most, do the session and accept some tiredness on the rock. Favour low-impact training such as stretching, fingerboarding and small-muscle-group conditioning, keep high-intensity sessions short, and avoid heavier sessions within about 48 hours of climbing outside.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Priority is getting outside | Don't be beholden to a training session: it's okay to drop a session if that serves the objective of getting out climbing |
| Priority is improving | Get the training session done and accept that you might be a little tired when climbing |

## Know your priorities
- Your priority decides the balance between training and climbing outside: are you trying to perform, just getting out to enjoy the weather and have a quality time, or somewhere in between? Clearing up the priority makes the decision simple.

## Focus on low-impact exercises
Some training is much easier to balance with climbing outside than others, if you want to feel good on the rock.

| Harder to balance with climbing outside | Easier to balance with climbing outside |
|---|---|
| High-volume aerobic power | Stretching |
| Anaerobic capacity training | Fingerboarding |
| | Small-muscle-group conditioning |

## Intensity and duration
- You can still push hard in training while aiming to perform outside, but be honest with yourself about how much you exert yourself in workouts.
- **Warning:** If intensity is high, keep the session short, and avoid heavier sessions within about 48 hours of going climbing outside.
