# Planning a Realistic Year of Training: Periodization and 4-Week Blocks

**Type:** Planning, Principles · **Trains:** General strength, Finger strength, Power, Endurance, Mobility, Technique, Recovery

**TL;DR:** Training is planned and progressive; exercising (climbing on the MoonBoard with friends) has a very limited improvement curve. Skip classical periodization: use non-linear periodization (strength, power and endurance session by session), and move to block periodization when that plateaus. Plan 12 to 24 months ahead in 4-week blocks (3 weeks of increasing volume and intensity, 1 recovery week), give each block a main focus, a technical skill and a mental or tactical skill, and plan only the next 4 weeks in detail.

## Exercising vs training
- Exercising is going in and doing some movement, maybe getting a little tired and sweaty. Nothing wrong with it, but it has a very limited improvement curve.
- Training is planned and progressive, and over time it produces a big shift in performance capability.
- Climbing on the MoonBoard with friends is exercising (or performing), not training.

## Periodization styles

| Style | How it works | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Classical periodization | Work in one zone of development for a whole 4-week block (e.g. strength only: heavy weights, hanging from the hangboard, hard boulder problems, letting everything else go), then switch the next block to something else (e.g. endurance only) and hope strength stays up | Doesn't work well for climbing, so it is not used |
| Non-linear periodization | Switch between strength, power and endurance session by session, e.g. strength session Monday, power session Wednesday, endurance session Thursday, then back to strength | Works well because it develops the many facets the sport needs |
| Block periodization | A focused block (e.g. strength) where 2 of every 3 workouts are developmental sessions for that focus, and the third is a maintenance session covering the other facets (explosive power, endurance, mobility, whatever else you need). Cycle: strength day 1, strength day 2, maintenance day 3, repeat | Use when non-linear cycling plateaus and no longer produces results |

- **Warning:** Classical periodization programs look great on paper and make sense mentally, but they don't produce the results you want, because you plateau and lose fitness in the facets you're not developing. Climbing needs strength and endurance at the same time, explosive power and mobility, and high levels of capacity.

### Non-linear periodization
- It forces big gaps between any given stimulus: with a power and an endurance session between hangboard sessions, it is 5 days before you get back on the hangboard, which helps prevent overtraining, injury and burnout on that session.
- Run the same non-linear cycle for 6, 7, 8 or 10 weeks before changing the workouts. Each workout progresses, but you only do it once every 5 or 7 days.
- When non-linear cycling plateaus and no longer produces results, move to block periodization.

### Block periodization
- The cycle doesn't have to fit a specific week: if you're busy, e.g. training only 2 days a week and climbing on weekends, just keep moving the cycle along.

## Planning 12–24 months ahead
- Plan training looking 12 to 24 months into the future, even though that is hard for many climbers, especially young ones, to imagine.
- Make an honest assessment of where you are: ask a friend, coach or mentor what they see in your climbing that needs to improve, and address those weaknesses.
- Also prepare for what you'll be doing. Performing at your best needs more than a few weeks of preparation.

| Planned for next year | Start now |
|---|---|
| A lot of big walls | Develop work capacity, climb on granite, climb more cracks and learn systems |
| A bouldering trip | Spend more time developing those skills |

## Four-week blocks
- Plan roughly 12 months in advance, programmed in 4-week blocks: 13 four-week blocks per year.
- Most athletes can only train through 12 of those blocks; the other 4 weeks are taken by travel, holidays, injuries, illness and so on. Plan on losing about 4 weeks a year, and you'll accept it better when it happens.
- The 12 blocks are each planned to develop specific facets of fitness. Nothing fully develops in 4 weeks, but discrete phases keep training interesting and give a stopping point to review how it's going and adjust the next 4 weeks.
- Even with a whole year pencilled out, only the next 4 weeks are planned in detail.

| Each 4-week block has | Example |
|---|---|
| A main focus | Get stronger |
| One technical skill goal | Heel hooks |
| One mental or tactical skill | Lead head, redpointing |

- **Warning:** Athletes tend to keep progressing until one of three things happens: they get injured, get sick or overtrain.

| Weeks | Content |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Increasing volume and intensity |
| 4 | Recovery week at the end of the month, used to transition into the next month |

- "Three steps forward, one step back": a stepwise progression through the year.
