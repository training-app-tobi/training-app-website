# Alternating Linear Periodization: Strength and Power vs Endurance

**Type:** Planning · **Trains:** General strength, Finger strength, Power, Endurance, Power endurance · **For:** stalled progress, off-season

**TL;DR:** Alternate 3-week blocks: 3 weeks of strength and power (2–4 workouts a week), then 3 weeks of endurance and power endurance, and back again. Strength and power hold for about 20–25 days after you stop training them, and climbing keeps giving both a strength stimulus and general energy capacity, so you re-enter each quality very close to where you left it. Optimally run two cycles (12 weeks), take a recovery week, then go perform.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Flat after a few years of climbing and training | Alternating linear periodization is worth trying |
| Off-season | It is fine to switch back and forth for 3 or even 4 cycles, but stick to the 3-week rule |

## Why programs stop working
- A program that worked really well (e.g. an 8-week, 12-week or half-year plan) works less well the next time you do it, and less again the time after. Often this is because you have adapted to that style of overload.
- Novices improve just by going to the gym and spending time moving and climbing, because there is so much room to get better. This tricks us into thinking it will keep happening and that we just need to do more of it to see the same gain. Once you have a few years of climbing and training behind you, things get a lot more complicated.

## Other periodization models
| Model | How it is laid out |
|---|---|
| Classical (traditional) periodization | A 4–6 week phase of strength building, followed by power building, followed by endurance building, which is supposed to lead to a period of elevated performance |
| Non-linear periodization | Switch between a strength, a power and an endurance (energy-system) focus with each session, on a cycle: session 1 strength, session 2 power, session 3 endurance, then repeat |

- **Warning:** The problem with classical periodization: by the time you reach the performance phase, you have lost the strength gains because you stopped training strength. Power is elevated for a while, but when you move to endurance, strength and power are both on the wane, so when you finally reach the performance phase nothing really great happens.
- Non-linear periodization is a really flexible plan and works really well, especially if you are doing a lot of climbing.
- But non-linear periodization also plays out the same way every other training program does for almost every athlete (it stops working as you adapt).

## The alternating linear plan
| Phase | What to do |
|---|---|
| Strength and power, 3 weeks | Put all your session energy into strength and power development, married together because climbing combines them: e.g. a bouldering day, some hangboarding, maybe some weight lifting; work on strength and explosiveness. For most athletes that is 2–4 workouts a week, doing the same workout or series of workouts for the 3 weeks |
| Energy-system development, 3 weeks | After about 3 weeks most of your improvements start to level off. At the 3-week mark, switch to endurance and power endurance for 3 weeks, then switch back to strength |
| Repeat | Go back and forth between the two 2, 3 or even 4 times in a cycle; each time strength goes up and endurance goes up, then strength again, then endurance again |
| Performance period | Let everything drop back to maintenance level and go do some hard bouldering or sending, or go on a big trip |

## Why 3 weeks
- Strength and power have a decay rate: if you stop strength training, the levels stay with you for about 20–25 days before they really start to taper off. Alternating linear uses this 20–25 day window to switch over to stamina training.
- Even during stamina training, climbing still gives a strength stimulus: you are still pulling hard on holds, locking off and doing all the climbing movements. Likewise, during the strength and power block endurance doesn't drop off, because you are still spending several hours in the gym doing boulder problems, which keeps your general energy capacity up.
- So when you come back to a quality after 3 weeks, your numbers will still be right where they were: you re-enter training very close to where you left it and can push it forward for 3 more weeks before switching again.

## How long to run it
| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Training | Optimally two cycles: 12 weeks in total |
| Recovery | A recovery week |
| Performance | Go on a trip and send your projects |
| After | Come home and repeat |

## Overuse
- Alternating gives you a break from the overloads that build up in the background. Training a lot of stamina can bring some shoulder pain, low back pain and other small overuse problems; training strength can strain the fingers or bring some elbow pain. The 3-week rule pushes you out of each one long enough to give other stimuli, then you come back to it with some freshness and can continue to progress.

## Summary
- Alternating linear periodization isn't the be-all and end-all of programming, but it is a very simple way to apply a different stimulus you probably haven't used before, and it works well for people. The details within the 3-week strength-and-power and 3-week endurance blocks are up to you.
