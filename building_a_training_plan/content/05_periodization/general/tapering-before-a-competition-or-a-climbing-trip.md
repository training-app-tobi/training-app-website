# Tapering Before a Competition or a Climbing Trip

**Type:** Planning · **Trains:** Recovery · **For:** competition climbers, climbing trip

**TL;DR:** Many studies show performance gains after one- to three-week tapers. Before a competition: a gentle overreach week three weeks out (slightly higher volume and intensity), then taper week 1 at 25% less volume with intensity kept above 85% of normal max, and week 2 at 50% less volume with similar intensity, then compete. Before a climbing trip, taper in the week before: keep climbing intensity up to about 85% of normal max, cut volume by 50%, and consider reducing or dropping supplemental strength training, especially taxing exercises such as weighted pull-ups and deadlifts.

## Competition taper
- A taper is reduced training, usually a gradual reduction, carefully timed so the athlete is in peak shape before a competition or high-performance phase.
- Many studies show performance gains after one- to three-week tapers.

| When | Do this |
|---|---|
| 3 weeks before the competition | Gentle overreach week: slightly higher volume and intensity |
| Taper week 1 | Volume -25%, intensity maintained (>85% of normal max) |
| Taper week 2 | Volume -50%, intensity similar to week 1 |
| Then | Compete |

## Climbing trip taper
| Situation | Do this |
|---|---|
| Climbing trip, to maximize sends | Taper in the week before the trip: keep climbing intensity up to about 85% of normal max, cut volume by 50%, and consider reducing or eliminating supplemental strength training, especially taxing exercises such as weighted pull-ups and deadlifts |
