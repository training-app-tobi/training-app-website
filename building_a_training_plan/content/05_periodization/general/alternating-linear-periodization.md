# Alternating Linear Periodization

**Type:** Planning · **Trains:** General strength, Finger strength, Power, Power endurance · **For:** performing year-round

**TL;DR:** Alternate about 3 weeks (about 20 days) of strength work with 3 weeks of power work, then take a 1-week recovery period, and repeat the 7-week cycle. Strength and power are closely related, so each maintains the other, and the short cycles bring you back to strength before it has time to degrade. Train in the gym 2–3 days a week, climb at the crag 2–3 days a week, and let your performance at the crag decide whether you redpoint really hard or not.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Performing year-round | Use alternating linear periodization |

## Linear and non-linear programs
| Program | Structure | Performance |
|---|---|---|
| Linear | 4 weeks focused on hangboarding, then a block of campusing and bouldering, then a power endurance cycle, then a 4-week peak in which you send your hard routes | Only a few weeks of peak performance a year; most climbers would rather perform at a relatively high level more often |
| Non-linear | Switch each session between strength, power and energy-system work, and keep rotating through them no matter how many times a week you train | Longer performance periods; you keep progressing your training through the year while still performing at a high level |

- **Warning:** Power endurance cycles in linear programs are often very long; 4 weeks is far too long for a power endurance cycle.
- **Warning:** The main error of linear programs: the traditional linear programs climbers borrowed them from were aimed at a single energy system (e.g. explosive power in the Olympic lifts, or running a marathon). Climbing needs work across all three energy systems, so a linear program falls short for most athletes.

### When to use non-linear
| Situation | Use of non-linear |
|---|---|
| Haven't used it before | A great program |
| High-level climbers or people that like a more linear approach | A great year-round program |
| Peaking phase, or whenever you are trying to perform well | Excellent |

## Alternating linear cycle
| Phase | Length | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Strength | About 3 weeks (really about 20 days) | Resistance training, hangboarding and maybe some technical bouldering |
| Power | 3 weeks | Power and explosiveness, maybe bleeding into a little power endurance depending on your needs |
| Recovery | 1 week | Recovery |

- That is 7 weeks in total; then go straight back into strength, power and another recovery week, alternating between the two.

## Weekly structure while alternating
| Where | Days per week |
|---|---|
| Gym | 2–3 |
| Crag | 2–3; let your performance at the crag decide whether you redpoint really hard or not |

## Why it works
- Strength and power are closely related, so each supports the other: power work maintains strength and strength work maintains power. This is the big advantage over a linear program.
- The cycles are short (only 20–21 days), so there is never a long break from strength: you push strength up, and before it has time to degrade you are back in another strength cycle.
- Alternating linear training is very successful in long-season sports (e.g. basketball, football) and for people trying to perform year-round.
