# Periodizing a Self-Written Training Plan: Rest Weeks, Specificity and Volume to Intensity

**Type:** Planning · **Trains:** Recovery · **For:** Self-coached climbers

**TL;DR:** Three elements are enough for a basic periodized plan: regular rest weeks (3 weeks of training then 1 week off, or 4 on and 1 off), more and more specificity to the goal towards the end of the plan, and a shift from volume at moderate to low intensity at the start to higher intensity and lower volume later. A specific goal means high-intensity training in that style, a broad goal stays broad, and either way projecting and harder climbing get priority towards the end.

## Periodization
| Element | What to do |
|---|---|
| Rest periods | Regularly through the plan: 3 weeks training then 1 week off, or 4 weeks on then 1 week off. The rest week has reduced load compared with training weeks, not necessarily nothing; it gives the body time to catch up with the training effects |
| Specificity | Towards the end of the plan (e.g. a six-month plan) become more and more specific to the goal (see below) |
| Volume and intensity | At the start, raise volume at moderate to low intensity; this holds even for boulder specialists (a high volume of slightly easier boulders, not completely easy, rather than only projecting). As the plan progresses, intensity rises and training becomes more specific; then reduce volume to recover better and raise intensity each session, getting better at maximum effort |

| Goal | Specificity across the plan |
|---|---|
| Specific route or style | Train at high intensity in that style and keep training in line with the goal |
| Broad goal | Stay broad across the plan |
| Either | Prioritise projecting and harder climbing towards the end |

- These three elements (rest periods, specificity, volume-intensity ratio) are a basic format that is enough; if you want a more complicated structure, research it thoroughly before deciding.
