# Periodise Your Training First: Record, Rate and Schedule Deload Weeks

**Type:** Planning · **Trains:** Recovery

**TL;DR:** Pick the low-hanging fruit first: before any finer adjustment, such as syncing training to the menstrual cycle, periodise at all, with higher-load weeks and deload weeks, because adaptation happens during recovery. Record your weekly sessions, rate how tiring each is (RPE 1–10), judge the recovery each needs and schedule a deload week: 3 weeks on, 1 week off is most common, while 2 on, 1 off may suit some individuals better for recovery.

## Periodise your training first
- Pick the low-hanging fruit first. Before periodising around the menstrual cycle, periodise at all: plan higher-load weeks and deload (or rest) weeks.
- Adaptation happens during recovery. Without frequent rest weeks, energy levels drop, injury risk rises and the response to training is hampered.

### If your training is not periodised yet
| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1. Record | Record the sessions you do each week |
| 2. Rate | Rate how tiring each session is (RPE, scale 1–10) |
| 3. Judge recovery | Judge the recovery each session needs from its intensity (how difficult) and volume (how long), by how tired you feel after the session or the following day |
| 4. Schedule a deload | Use this to schedule a deload week with reduced volume and intensity of training and climbing, to allow supercompensation (the adaptation that leads to higher performance after training) |
| 5. Overlay | Once this structure is in place, overlay it with your menstrual cycle |

### Deload patterns
| Pattern | Weeks on | Weeks off | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3:1 | 3 | 1 | Most common pattern |
| 2:1 | 2 | 1 | Depending on the individual, may be preferred to improve recovery |
