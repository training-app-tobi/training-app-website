# The Strength Continuum: How Much and How Often to Lift

**Type:** Planning, Principles · **Trains:** General strength, Pulling strength, Finger strength, Antagonists & core

**TL;DR:** Spend 2–3 months a year in phases of 6–8 weeks of high-load, high-stability lifting, 4–6 weeks 1–2 times a year in a slightly destabilized phase, and keep sport-specific strength work in season, connecting fingertips to toes. Every movement, including the pull-up, has its own continuum, so training swings back and forth between its variations. Sport-specific strength work fits on a rained-out day or after a short day at the crag.

## Why strength train
- Directly, a stronger athlete can hold on to holds better, lock off better and do harder moves on steep terrain.
- If all you ever do is climb, soft tissues, skin and feet end up being limiters. So climb a lot, climb hard routes, try really hard at the crag, and take the extra energy you have into the weight room.

## The continuum
- Medium-load, medium-stability examples: TRX training, kettlebell training, offset training (one leg in front of the other). Very sport-specific, light-load examples: hangboarding, Moonboarding.

## Through the training year

| Phase | When | Length and frequency | Example exercises |
|---|---|---|---|
| High load, high stability | Off-season: a wintertime strength block or a midsummer rebuild | 2–3 months a year in total: phases of 6–8 weeks, done 1–2 times a year, 3 days a week | Deadlifts, bench presses, weighted pull-ups |
| Slightly destabilized | Pre-season or after heavy strength training | 4–6 weeks, 1–2 times a year | Single-arm overhead presses, single-arm pull-ups, split squats, step-ups |
| Highly sport-specific | In season | Once a week, on top of a lot of performance at the crag | Lots of core stability training, specific training on the wall, hangboard training |

- Sport-specific phase: focus on connecting the fingertips to the tips of the toes, maintaining core tension the whole time, and making things as real as possible. Deadlifts, bench presses and similar can still be done to maintain that strength, but more important is integrating strength from a small contact point in the hand to a small contact point at a distant toe position.

## Every movement has its own continuum
- Every movement (squat, hinge, press, pull, even grip strength) has very stable, high-force variants and low-force, low-stability variants, and they can be found in each movement of the sport. That is why training swings back and forth between these variations of each movement pattern.

| Point on the continuum | Pull-up variation | Character |
|---|---|---|
| High load | Two-arm pull-up with a big weight hanging from the waist, maxing out at 2–3 reps | Very stable: the bar doesn't move, both arms pull hard |
| Middle | Single-arm variations, destabilized variations (e.g. on rings), offset variations | Slightly lower intensity, but more like the sport (different positioning) |
| Sport-specific | Pulling on the campus board, pull-ups on a hangboard, or simply pulling your way up a Moonboard | Extremely sport-specific, but generates far less force than a pull-up bar |

## High-load, high-stability workout
- Set schemes: e.g. 3 sets of 5, 5 sets of 3 or 4 sets of 4: something pretty intense.

| Movement pattern | Very stable exercise |
|---|---|
| Upper-body press | Overhead barbell press or bench press |
| Upper-body pull | Some kind of row or a heavy pull-up |
| Hip hinge | Deadlift (the classic choice) |
| Squat | Front squat or back squat for most people |

- Squat preference: a split-stance squat, even though it is a destabilized variation, because it seems to be healthier for most climbers' legs.

## Medium-load, medium-stability workout
- Keep about the same total reps as the high-load workouts, 15 or even 20 per exercise, but perhaps only 3 sets: e.g. 3 sets of 6 or 3 sets of 8. Many exercises are done per side (e.g. left arm 8 reps, right arm 8 reps).
- Take as much rest as you need between sets so every rep looks clean.
- Use destabilized variations, e.g. a single-arm squat or a single-arm row.

## Low-load, low-stability (sport-specific) training
- These exercises can be done fairly infrequently and still give good gains: as you move into the season, maybe not even once a week, maybe once every 5 days. Aim for perfection of movement with lots of rest between efforts.
- Good times to do it: a rained-out redpoint day, or after climbing for the day. Strength training on the same day as another session is fine: after a short day at the crag, go back to the gym, warm up again and do these very specific strength exercises; they help extend the peak season.
- Example exercises: finger strength work on a system board or a fixed board, campus exercises.
