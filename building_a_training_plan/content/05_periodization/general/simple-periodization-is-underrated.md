# Simple Periodization Is Underrated

**Type:** Principles, Planning · **For:** Elite, training enthusiasts

**TL;DR:** Periodization as usually framed in climbing is overly rigid and a poor extrapolation from other sports, but in a simple form it is underrated: e.g. when the climbing season is bad, take a period of training where you don't go as hard, recover more and do general preparatory gym work. Different training periods let you include different exercises without fitting everything in alongside climbing; long-term planning matters especially for elite athletes with competition dates.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Elite athletes | Long-term planning is important, especially with specific competition dates |
| Training enthusiasts | A degree of planning and training organization is important |

## Training periodization
- The way periodization is almost universally framed in climbing is overly rigid and a poor extrapolation from other sports, and doesn't line up well with climbers' needs.
- Periodization is underrated in a simple form: it doesn't have to be complicated and can be a simple way to add variety. Example: when the climbing season is bad, take a period of training where you don't go as hard, recover more, and do general preparatory gym work.
- Different training periods let you include different exercises or movements without trying to fit everything in alongside climbing.
