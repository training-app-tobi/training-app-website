# Before Syncing Training to Your Cycle: Contraception and Symptom Tracking

**Type:** Planning, Injury & health · **Trains:** Recovery · **For:** Female

**TL;DR:** Before syncing training to your menstrual cycle, periodise your training into higher-load and deload weeks (see the note Periodise Your Training First). Hormonal contraceptives change how the cycle behaves and can mask menstrual disruption, the red flag of low energy availability. Record the symptoms that affect your training over 3–6 months to find your own pattern.

## Hormonal contraception
- The synthetic (exogenous) hormones interrupt the natural fluctuation of the body's own (endogenous) hormones, so the cycle does not behave like a natural one.
- Regular bleeding, e.g. in the pill-free days of the combined pill, is a withdrawal bleed, not a period.
- Some forms of contraception stop periods altogether.
- **Warning:** hormonal contraceptives can mask menstrual disruption, the red flag of low energy availability. Low energy availability shows as poor adaptation, low mood, frequent injury or illness, reduced memory or concentration. If unsure, one possible approach is a break from hormonal contraception to get a better idea of your hormonal health.

## Track your symptoms
- Record symptoms that affect your training over 3–6 months: water retention (bloating), strength, coordination, motivation, recovery.
- This weeds out ordinary "bad days" (not every poor session or tired day is linked to hormones) and shows what affects you personally.
- On hormonal oral contraceptives you may still have symptoms around withdrawal bleeds.
- Contraceptives that act locally in the uterus (e.g. a hormonal coil) are thought not to interfere with blood hormone levels in the same way, so changes through the month may still occur, with or without a period.
