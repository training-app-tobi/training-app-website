# Hormone Cycles and Power

**Type:** Planning, Injury & health · **Trains:** Power · **For:** Female

**TL;DR:** Upper-body power has a large impact on climbing performance, and many female climbers report that it fluctuates over their monthly cycle. Rising estrogen before ovulation increases joint laxity and reduces musculotendinous stiffness, which could affect higher-velocity movements and is linked to higher injury risk. Around ovulation, keep power sessions short and stop while still strong, switch to simpler, more controllable power exercises, or shift focus to another part of the strength-velocity spectrum.

## Power and the monthly cycle
- Research shows upper-body power has a large impact on climbing performance.
- Many female climbers report that their upper-body power fluctuates over their monthly cycle.

## Why it may happen
- In the follicular phase (about day 1 to 14) estrogen rises until it peaks just before ovulation.
- Estrogen acts on connective tissue: it increases joint laxity and reduces musculotendinous stiffness, which could affect higher-velocity movements.
- The research on this was done on the lower body and focused on the knee, so applying it to climbing involves broad assumptions.

## Training power around ovulation
- If power drops, or you feel more vulnerable to injury, around ovulation, account for it when optimising training: switch exercises, switch focus, or rearrange the week. This doesn't necessarily mean not training or dropping all power work; find a balance that suits the athlete.
- **Warning:** The increased joint laxity and reduced musculotendinous stiffness are linked to higher injury risk. Be aware of high motivation around this phase: keep power sessions short and stop while still strong.
- If you use footless bouldering to train power: during the ovulatory phase, move to a simpler plane of movement on a campus board or on basic boulders, which could help reduce the risk of shoulder injury. Alternatively, power pull-ups on a bar give a more controllable session.
- If you feel really powered down at this time, shift focus: work a different part of the strength-velocity spectrum, or return to base strength work such as weighted pull-ups.
