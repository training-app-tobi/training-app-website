# Strength Training and the Menstrual Cycle: The Luteal Phase

**Type:** Planning, Principles · **Trains:** General strength, Finger strength, Endurance · **For:** Female

**TL;DR:** Gain strength in the follicular phase and maintain it in the luteal phase, but keep strength training in the luteal phase and get the most out of it. If water retention affects your training, reduce added weights a little and aim for the same RPE. The luteal phase may favour endurance, so a strength session can be swapped for an aerobic session, as long as it fits your climbing goals; shorter high-intensity sessions and consistent loads are other options.

## Gain and maintain
- Gain and maintain approach: gain strength during the follicular phase, maintain it during the luteal phase.
- Climbing often demands both endurance and strength, but strength is always a key player: if you can't do a move, there is nothing to endure.
- Hormones fluctuate throughout the whole cycle, not only during the period or the premenstrual phase, so the approach to training can be adjusted throughout the cycle to work with your physiology.

## Strength training in the luteal (high hormone) phase
- **Warning:** Adaptations to strength training may be lesser during the luteal phase because of higher progesterone levels and longer recovery times. This does not mean doing no strength training in the luteal phase: climbing is very much a strength-based sport, so instead of avoiding strength training, think about how to get the most out of the strength sessions you do in the luteal phase.

## Account for water weight
- Because climbing is weight-dependent, extra water weight can increase RPE and seemingly reduce performance on weighted exercises (weighted pull-ups, fingerboarding) and on climbing itself. Account for it especially when water weight is a significant percentage of the total added load, e.g. in fingerboarding.
- The sustainable approach: be aware of this change and, if you feel it is affecting your training, adjust the session accordingly.
- There is no need to weigh yourself every day of the luteal phase or to use absolute loads in weighted exercises; there is a middle ground between struggling blindly on with a load and weighing yourself every session.
- If tracking shows that bloating or water retention affects the quality of your training (e.g. you can't maintain your fingerboard load): first, exercise self-compassion ("I am a bit heavier due to water retention, and this is not permanent" rather than "I am getting weaker" or "I am putting on weight"); second, reduce your added weights a little and aim for the same RPE.

## Endurance in the high hormone phase
- There is some evidence that endurance ability and gains from endurance training are increased in the high hormone phase because of increased fat metabolism during exercise. Fats fuel the aerobic energy system, so sessions that favour the aerobic system may feel more accessible, and focusing on endurance in this phase may optimise training from an adaptation point of view.
- Endurance training here doesn't necessarily mean running, endless laps or an hour of continuous traversing. Climbing is a high-intensity sport that relies heavily on the anaerobic energy system.
- The anaerobic system supplies ATP much faster than the aerobic system, so it dominates in high-intensity exercise; reducing intensity generally increases the aerobic contribution. Other dials besides intensity: volume and rest intervals.
- **Warning:** Keep your climbing goals in focus: a boulderer doing endless laps just because they are in the luteal phase will not optimise training.
- These are shifts in training focus; they don't represent everything trained during these phases.

## Options for slower recovery and hard-to-reach maximal strength
Example: a sport climber who needs strength for cruxes and fitness, and feels sluggish, tired and struggles with maximal efforts in the luteal phase.

| Option | How / why |
|---|---|
| Replace a strength session with an endurance session | E.g. an aerobic capacity session (this energy system is important for sport climbing). This increases recovery time between high-intensity sessions and may let you work with your physiology for endurance gains |
| Reduce the volume of higher-intensity sessions | Reduce the length of sessions, or the sets/reps, in higher-intensity strength-based bouldering or conditioning sessions |
| Keep loads consistent | Keep loads for exercises such as pull-ups or fingerboarding consistent throughout the luteal phase, so you maintain rather than push every element of training in this phase |

- Try one of these adjustments and see whether motivation, energy levels, recovery and/or consistency improve over around 3 cycles. This area is in its infancy in climbing (and most other sports), so approach it with a curious, experimental mindset.

## Why it works
- Glycogen sparing: during the luteal phase the body burns more fat during exercise. Glycogen and glucose, not fats, are the main fuels for high-intensity exercise, so high-intensity efforts can feel less readily available in the luteal phase.
- Water retention: estrogen and progesterone both affect water balance, and high hormone levels can lead to water retention. One mechanism: estrogen increases aldosterone, which causes more sodium to be reabsorbed; when sodium is retained, water is retained too, to maintain the osmotic concentration of the blood (a measure of the concentration of dissolved substances in the blood).
- Energy systems: the aerobic and anaerobic energy systems are not binary. Muscles need to supply ATP for exercise and are not "fussy" about its source; which system can supply it is dictated by the rate at which ATP must be supplied to maintain the effort.
