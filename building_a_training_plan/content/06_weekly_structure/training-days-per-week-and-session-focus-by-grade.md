# Training Days per Week and Session Focus by Grade

**Type:** Planning · **Trains:** Finger strength, Power, General strength · **For:** Beginner, Advanced, stalled progress

**TL;DR:** A V4 climber averages 3 training days per week and a V12+ climber 4, while total weekly exercises stay at around 22 at every grade and weekly load rises only 1.6x. What changes is the distribution: beginners fit about 7 exercises into each session, elite climbers about 5, with each session given a single, tighter focus. Three days a week is workable; how you use your days matters more than how many you have.

## Who should do what
| Situation / level | Do this |
|---|---|
| Beginner | Dense, generalist sessions: develop a wide range of skills on and off the wall, build work capacity, and create a solid and complete foundation from which specific training can begin |
| Elite climber | Separate each aspect of training across the 4 days, giving each session a single, tighter focus (see the example week below) |
| Hit a plateau | Ask whether each current session has a clear purpose, or whether every session has turned into a bit of everything. Adding a fourth, deliberately lighter, more focused session to the week may affect training outcomes more than lengthening the sessions you already have |

## Training days and weekly volume by grade
| Measure | Lower grades | V12+ |
|---|---|---|
| Training days per week | 3 (V4) | 4 |
| Total exercises per week | Around 22 (V1-V4) | Around 22 |
| Weekly session load | 240 (V1-V4) | 375 (a 1.6x increase) |
| Exercises per session | Roughly 7 (22 over 3 days) | Closer to 5 (22 over 4 days) |

- Training days: roughly one extra day at the elite end. A clear difference, but smaller than many climbers assume when they picture how hard-grade climbers train.
- Total weekly exercises are nearly identical across every grade band: around 22 per week at every level, from V1-V4 through to V12+.
- Weekly session load rises steadily but modestly with grade. Noticeable, but nothing like the step change most climbers associate with elite training.
- What changes is the distribution: the elite week has more days, and each day has slightly fewer things on it.

## How beginner and elite sessions differ
- Elite athletes complete fewer exercises per session, and these sessions tend to be more focused on training a specific adaptation.
- Elite climbers are no longer building the foundational adaptations, so their training targets a narrower range of attributes.
- **Warning:** The "scatter gun" approach can also hold beginners back: it is hard to execute each aspect of a diverse session well. When a single day tries to cover climbing, hangboard, pulls, core and mobility all at once, each element often receives only a fraction of the attention it needs.

### Example elite week
| Day | Session focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Maximal hangs |
| 2 | Limit bouldering |
| 3 | Strength |
| 4 | Conditioning or skills |

## Is three days a week enough?
- Three days per week is a workable training frequency. The interquartile range is roughly 3 to 4 days for V5-V7 climbers and the same for V8-V11. Plenty of V8 climbers train three days a week, and plenty of V5 climbers train four.
- How you use your days matters more than how many you have: a climber training 3 days a week with a clear purpose for each session will often outperform a climber training 4 days with scattered work.
- When scheduling yourself, ask "what is each of my current days actually for?", not "can I fit another day in?".

## What the numbers describe
- The figures describe committed climbers who log their training for months at a time and follow a plan (engaged training behaviour). Climbers who stopped logging after a few sessions are not included, so the figures are not a read on every climber at those grades.
- Weekly load reflects what each session is prescribed to do (the target intensity of the session template), not what the climber actually trained at: two climbers with the same weekly load may have had very different weeks. So read the patterns across grade bands, not the absolute numbers. The shape holds either way: load rises modestly with grade, exercise count stays flat.
- A training day is any calendar day with at least one completed session.
- An exercise is a distinct entry within a session (e.g. a deadhang, a pull-up set, a climbing block, a hip flexor stretch). Sets within one exercise are not counted separately: a session with four exercises counts as four, not twenty. So "exercises per week" measures training breadth (how many different things a climber works on), not total set count.
