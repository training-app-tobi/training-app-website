# Session Length and Combining Training Modes: The Interference Effect

**Type:** Planning, Principles · **Trains:** General strength, Endurance

**TL;DR:** Training strength and endurance alongside each other, in the same session, can reduce strength gains (the interference effect), though the impact on an individual depends on many factors. To maximise strength training when recovery feels slower, separate strength and endurance onto different days, which also shortens sessions; this is worth doing if strength is a focus, especially without fast, consistent progress, and particularly in the luteal phase of the menstrual cycle.

## Session length and combining training modes
- Many climbers want to improve everything at once (strength and power for boulders or cruxes, endurance to reach the chains), and since a lot of climbing requires both, they train these alongside each other.
- Interference effect: the reduction in strength gains that can be seen when endurance and strength are trained alongside each other and in close proximity (i.e. in the same session). It is complex, and the impact on an individual depends on many factors.
- To maximise strength training when recovery feels slower in any cycle phase, separate strength and endurance training onto different days. This tends to reduce the volume of a given session, which may reduce recovery time.
- This is worth being aware of if strength training is a focus, and particularly if you don't see fast, consistent progress in strength. It can be done throughout training, but may be particularly useful when strength training in the luteal phase.
