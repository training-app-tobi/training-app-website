# Scheduling General Strength Alongside Climbing

**Type:** Planning, Principles · **Trains:** General strength, Pulling strength

**TL;DR:** Supplemental training must not take away from climbing: pick three, maybe four exercises and do them after the climbing session or the next day, making sure the next day's climbing doesn't depend on them (legs and core the day before a tension session is a bad idea; a pull or push workout the day before forces you to rely on what is fresh). Do upper-body workouts after a climbing session rather than on off days, and stop the supplementals once they no longer bring adaptations.

## General strength alongside climbing
- Supplemental training is there to improve climbing, so it cannot spill over and take away from the climbing training.
- Pick three, maybe four exercises and do them after the climbing session or on the next day, but make sure the next day's climbing doesn't depend on what you trained the day before.

| Workout before | Next climbing session | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Legs and core (the day before) | Tension-based session | Don't: it will be a terrible session |
| Pull workout (the day before) | Tension-based session | Works well: you can't rely on cranking and pulling yourself around, so you have to rely on what is fresh |
| Push workout (the day before) | Tension-based session | Same: you can't rely on your arms as much |
| Pull workout (the session before) | Limit bouldering focused on cutting feet and the upper body, or any power session | The pull workout goes in the session before it |

- Don't do pull workouts or push workouts on an off day. Do push or any upper-body workout after a climbing session: you are already warm, so you skip the full warm-up sets, which saves a lot of total load in the week.
- Stop the supplementals once you no longer see adaptations, so you don't overdo them: do the amount you can, still benefit, and don't overdo the week. The target is the week's total load.
